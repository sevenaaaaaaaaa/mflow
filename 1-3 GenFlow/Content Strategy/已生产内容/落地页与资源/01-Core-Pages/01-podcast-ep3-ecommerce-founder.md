## Episode Summary

Jordan Reeves launched Alpine Goods Co. in 2023, selling premium outdoor gear — backpacks, hammocks, and camping cookware — from his garage in Boulder, Colorado. By 2025, he was doing $850K in annual revenue but hemorrhaging money on product photography: $2,400 per SKU for studio shoots, plus recurring costs for seasonal refreshes and A/B test variants.

In early 2026, Jordan took a bet on Lovart. Eighteen months later, Alpine Goods Co. is a $4.2M brand with a 22-product catalog, entirely AI-generated visual assets, and a conversion rate that has doubled from 2.1% to 4.3%. This is his story.

## Show Notes

### [00:02:15] The Photography Trap

**Lena**: Jordan, take us back to Alpine Goods in 2025. What did your creative production look like?

**Jordan**: It was a mess. We had 18 SKUs at the time, and every product needed a minimum of six images — white background, lifestyle in nature, detail shots, the works. We were working with a great photography studio in Denver, but each shoot day cost $3,000 to $4,000 when you factored in location permits, models, and post-production. We were spending close to $50,000 a year just on photography, and we still didn't have enough variants to properly A/B test on Meta and Google.

**Lena**: So you were paying premium prices for a limited number of assets.

**Jordan**: Exactly. And the turnaround was brutal. I'd approve a shot list on Monday, the shoot would happen the following Thursday, and we'd get finals ten days after that. By the time an image hit our product page, three weeks had passed. In e-commerce, three weeks is a century.

### [00:08:40] The Tipping Point

**Lena**: What finally pushed you to explore AI solutions?

**Jordan**: Black Friday 2025. We ran a flash sale on our bestselling backpack — the Trailhead 35L. The ad creative I wanted to test was a split-screen comparison showing the backpack in two seasonal contexts: summer trail and winter snow. Our photographer laughed and said best case, that's two separate shoots, or a composite job that would cost extra and take a week.

I remember sitting in my kitchen at 11 PM, frustrated, and I googled "AI product photography ecommerce." Lovart was the third result. I signed up for the free trial that night.

### [00:12:30] First Impressions

**Lena**: What was your first Lovart project?

**Jordan**: I uploaded three photos of the Trailhead 35L — honestly, they were iPhone photos I had taken for our Instagram Stories. Within 15 minutes, Lovart had generated both the summer and winter composites I wanted, plus six additional lifestyle variations I hadn't even considered. One of them — a shot of the backpack resting against a misty alpine lake at sunrise — became our highest-converting hero image ever. We still use a version of it today.

I called my photographer the next morning and said, "I love your work, but I think our relationship is about to change."

### [00:17:55] The Numbers That Changed Everything

**Lena**: Let's talk metrics. What did the switch to Lovart actually do for your business?

**Jordan**: I track everything obsessively, so here are the hard numbers comparing Q1 2026 vs. Q1 2027:

| Metric | Q1 2026 (Pre-Lovart) | Q1 2027 (Lovart) | Change |
|--------|---------------------|-------------------|--------|
| Creative cost per SKU | $2,400 | $420 | -73% |
| Average assets per SKU | 8 | 34 | +325% |
| A/B tests run per month | 3 | 22 | +633% |
| PDP conversion rate | 2.1% | 4.3% | +105% |
| Ad creative fatigue cycle | 14 days | 4 days | -71% |
| Total creative spend | $48,600/year | $11,200/year | -77% |

**Lena**: The conversion rate jump is staggering. How do you attribute that specifically to better imagery?

**Jordan**: When you have 34 variants per product instead of 8, you can match the image to the audience segment. Our backpack buyer looking at the product page from a Google Shopping ad sees a clean, high-contrast white-background hero shot. But the same product served to someone who came through an Instagram lifestyle ad sees a dramatic mountain silhouette composition. Personalization at the visual level — that's what doubled our conversion rate.

### [00:24:10] The Workflow Evolution

**Lena**: Walk us through your current creative workflow with Lovart.

**Jordan**: It's night and day from 2025. Every Monday, we review performance data from the previous week — which images are converting, which are fatiguing. My marketing lead flags which SKUs need refreshed assets. She writes a creative brief in Notion, which takes about 20 minutes per product. Then she feeds those briefs into Lovart's batch generation tool.

By Wednesday morning, we have 40–60 candidate images across all formats — product page, social square, Stories vertical, email header, Pinterest pin. We review as a team in a 30-minute Slack async thread, pick the winners, and push them live by Friday. What used to take three weeks now takes four days, and we're producing five times the volume.

### [00:30:45] The Unexpected Benefit: Brand Consistency

**Lena**: Was there anything that surprised you about the AI workflow?

**Jordan**: Brand consistency. I didn't expect that. When we were working with photographers — and we worked with three different ones over the years — every shoot had a slightly different feel. Different lighting, different color grading, different energy. Customers might not consciously notice, but subconsciously, it creates friction.

With Lovart, once we set up our Brand Genome — our color palette, our preferred lighting warmth, our composition rules — every single image feels like it came from the same creative director. My marketing lead, who has zero formal photography training, is now producing images that are more on-brand than the professional shoots we paid thousands for.

### [00:35:20] Addressing the Skeptics

**Lena**: You come from a community that values authenticity — outdoor enthusiasts who can spot a fake from a mile away. Have you faced any pushback on using AI imagery?

**Jordan**: That's a great question, and I was worried about it initially. Here's what I learned: our customers don't care how the image was made. They care whether it honestly represents the product. We learned this the hard way — in our second month using Lovart, we got a little carried away with a backpack image that made the fabric look more textured than it actually is. We got three returns citing "product doesn't match photos." That was a wake-up call.

Now we have a rule: every AI-generated image goes through a "truth check." Does the color match reality? Are the proportions accurate? If we're showing it in a context — say, a campsite — would you actually use this product there? As long as the image is truthful, the tool used to create it is irrelevant.

### [00:40:00] Advice for Fellow Founders

**Lena**: What would you say to an e-commerce founder who is on the fence about AI design tools?

**Jordan**: Three things. First, stop thinking of this as "replacing creativity." You are not replacing anything — you are multiplying your creative output. The same marketing team, the same design sensibility, just an order of magnitude more assets.

Second, start small. Don't try to migrate your entire catalog at once. Pick your top three revenue-driving SKUs, generate 20 images each using Lovart's free tier, and A/B test them against your current photos for two weeks. Let the data make the case for you.

Third, invest in your Brand Genome setup. The difference between mediocre AI output and professional-grade AI output is how well you train the model on your specific brand. Spend an afternoon loading in your best-performing assets, defining your color rules, and setting your composition preferences. That upfront investment pays dividends on every single image generated after.

### [00:44:30] What's Next for Alpine Goods Co.

**Lena**: Looking ahead, how does Lovart fit into your growth plans?

**Jordan**: We are launching our winter collection in October — 8 new products, including a four-season tent that's been in development for 18 months. For the first time, our entire launch campaign — every hero image, every social variant, every ad creative, every email header — will be AI-generated. We're aiming for 100 assets across all channels, produced in under two weeks, at a total creative cost of under $2,000.

That math would have been impossible two years ago. Today, it's just Tuesday.

## Key Takeaways

1. **Volume unlocks conversion**: More creative variants = better audience matching = higher conversion rates.
2. **Brand Genome is the moat**: Your brand's AI training data is a competitive asset — invest in it.
3. **Truth matters more than method**: Customers care about accurate representation, not production technique.
4. **Start with revenue drivers**: Prove ROI on your top 3 SKUs before scaling AI across the catalog.
5. **Speed compounds**: Cutting creative turnaround from weeks to days accelerates everything — testing, iteration, seasonal pivots.
