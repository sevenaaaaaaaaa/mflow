## Templates vs AI Generation: The Fundamental Difference

This is the most important distinction to understand about **AI graphic design tools**.

### The Template Model (Canva, Microsoft Designer)
You browse a library. You find something close to what you want. You swap in your text, your images, your colors. The underlying structure — where things go, how big they are, the visual rhythm — was decided by a human designer who built that template six months ago. If your content doesn't fit the template, you either force it (compromising clarity) or start dragging things around manually (compromising the design).

Templates are fast and safe. They're also limiting. Using Canva for every project inevitably leads to the "Canva look" — recognizable to anyone who spends time online. Your graphics blend into the feed instead of standing out.

### The AI Agent Model (Lovart, to some extent DZine)
You describe what you need, what it should communicate, who it's for, and what brand guidelines apply. The AI generates an original layout — not a remixed template. Each design is purpose-built for your specific content and context.

The AI still follows design principles — visual hierarchy, grid alignment, color harmony, typographic pairing — but it applies them creatively rather than mechanically. Two people giving the same brief to Lovart will get different designs, each appropriate to their brand. Two people using the same Canva template will get essentially the same output with different words.
