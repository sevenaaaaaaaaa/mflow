## Core Color Palette: Warm Tones That Stimulate Appetite

Restaurant color psychology is among the most studied in commercial design. Warm colors — reds, oranges, terracottas, warm yellows — stimulate appetite and increase perceived flavor intensity. Cool colors — blues, purples, teals — suppress appetite (there are almost no naturally blue foods, so our brains associate blue with inedible or spoiled). Lovart's **Restaurant Palette Engine** generates color schemes based on your cuisine and atmosphere:

## Typography: Fonts That Set the Table

Restaurant typography splits into three functional roles that Lovart automatically assigns:

**Display Font** (used for the logo name, signage, and hero headlines): Choose a font that telegraphs your restaurant's personality. Serif display fonts (Playfair Display, Cormorant Garamond) signal traditional elegance. Sans-serif display fonts (Montserrat, Bebas Neue) signal modern casual. Script fonts (Alex Brush, Great Vibes) signal personal warmth — but use only for the logo, never for body text (illegible at menu-reading distance).

**Body Font** (used for menu descriptions, website text, and printed materials): Always choose a highly readable serif or sans-serif at 11–14pt. Lovart defaults to Source Serif 4 for traditional restaurants and Inter for modern restaurants — both Google Fonts with full character sets including special dietary symbols.

**Accent Font** (used sparingly for specials, price highlights, and CTA text): A heavier weight variant of the body font (e.g., Inter Bold for "Today's Special") or a complementary font for emphasis. Lovart's **Type Pairing Engine** automatically recommends pairings that share similar x-height and stroke contrast, avoiding the clashing that occurs when self-selecting fonts from a dropdown.
