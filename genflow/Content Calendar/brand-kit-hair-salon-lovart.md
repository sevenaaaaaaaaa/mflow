## Fashion-Forward, Elegant, Aspirational: The Salon Color Palette

[IMAGE 2 PLACEHOLDER — Conceptual Diagram]

Hair salons sell transformation — the visual identity must make the client feel aspirational before they sit in the chair. Lovart's **Salon Palette Engine** generates palettes calibrated to salon positioning:

**Luxury/High-End Salon:** Black (#111111), white (#FAFAFA), champagne gold (#D4AF37), blush (#E8C4C4), and charcoal (#3A3A3A). The black-white-gold combination telegraphs luxury across categories — it's the palette of Chanel, Tom Ford, and high-end fashion. The blush addition softens the severity, making it feel like a beauty destination rather than a corporate law firm. This palette photographs beautifully — blonde, brunette, and red hair all pop against the high-contrast backdrop.

**Trendy/Editorial Salon:** Matte black (#1A1A1A), neon accent (client's choice — electric pink #FF2D55, lime #C4FF0E, or cyan #00E5FF), warm gray (#8E8E93), and white (#FFFFFF). A single neon accent against matte black creates the "cool factor" that attracts fashion-forward clients under 35. The neon color becomes a signature — recognizable in a single Instagram story frame.

**Natural/Organic Salon:** Warm beige (#D4C5B9), sage green (#9CAF88), cream (#F5F0E8), sand (#C4A882), and terracotta (#B87654). Earth tones that signal "clean beauty," "natural ingredients," and "low-tox." This palette appeals to the growing segment of clients seeking ammonia-free color, organic products, and a wellness-oriented salon experience.

Each palette includes an **Instagram Preview Simulation** — Lovart shows how the palette renders in a 3×3 Instagram grid mockup with placeholder hair-transformation photos, demonstrating the visual rhythm a consistent palette creates across content.

## Typography: From Storefront to Strand Test Card

Salon typography is unusually demanding because it must convey luxury (for the pricing) and clarity (for the technical information). Lovart's **Salon Type System**:

**Logo/Storefront Font** — The salon name on the window and the primary logo. For luxury salons: high-contrast serif (Bodoni, Didot, Cormorant Garamond) — the typefaces of Vogue and Harper's Bazaar. For trendy salons: a clean geometric sans with custom letterform modifications (Lovart generates a custom uppercase treatment). For natural salons: a humanist serif or warm sans-serif (Lora, Raleway).

**Service Menu Font** — For the price list and service descriptions. Must balance elegance with extreme clarity — clients deciding between "Partial Balayage" and "Full Balayage" need to instantly distinguish them. Lovart recommends a clean sans-serif (Inter, Work Sans) at 12–14pt for the printed menu. The contrast of serif logo + sans-serif menu creates visual hierarchy.

**Appointment Card Font** — For the takeaway appointment reminder. Must be readable at small sizes (3.5×2-inch card) by clients of all ages. Lovart defaults to a medium-weight sans-serif at 11pt minimum for date/time/address information.

**Social Media Font** — For Instagram stories, posts, and TikTok cover text. Lovart recommends a fashion-forward display font (Bodoni Moda, Playfair Display) for quote overlays and before/after labels, paired with the brand's sans-serif for longer captions.

## The Salon Brand Experience: Touchpoints Beyond the Logo

[IMAGE 3 PLACEHOLDER — Real UI Screenshot]

A hair salon brand is experienced across 40+ touchpoints — from the Google Maps listing photo to the cape around your neck. Lovart's **Salon Touchpoint System** designs the most impactful ones:

**Appointment Reminder Card** (3.5×2 inches) — The object clients carry in their wallet for 4–8 weeks. Lovart designs a double-sided card with appointment details on the front and a "Scan to Rebook" QR code on the back. The design uses the brand palette and typeface — instant recognition when the client pulls it out.

**Before/After Photo Template** — Hair salons live and die by before/after photos on social media. Lovart generates a split-screen template (1080×1080 for Instagram, 1080×1920 for TikTok) with branded labels ("BEFORE" / "AFTER"), a subtle brand-color border, and stylist credit text placement. Consistent before/after formatting improves grid aesthetics and makes your salon's work instantly recognizable in the feed.

**Product Retail Shelf Talker** — For salons that retail products (shampoo, conditioner, styling products). Lovart generates 2×3-inch shelf-talker cards with product name, key benefit (5 words max), and price — all in the brand system. Professional shelf talkers increase retail sales by an average of 18% compared to handwritten price tags (based on salon retail merchandising studies).

**Strand Test Card / Color Record Card** — The technical card where colorists record formulas. Lovart generates a 4×6-inch card with: client name, date, formula fields (with enough space for multiple color lines), developer volume, processing time, and notes. Branded record cards communicate professionalism and systematization — this stylist tracks their work.
