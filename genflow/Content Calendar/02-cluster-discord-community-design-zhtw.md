## Every Discord Image Dimension, Decoded

[IMAGE 2 PLACEHOLDER — Conceptual Diagram]

### Server Icon: 512 x 512 px (minimum 128 x 128 px)

Your server icon is the face of your community. It appears in the server list at roughly 48 x 48 px, expands slightly in the channel sidebar, and blows up when someone views your server details. Upload it as a PNG or GIF at 512 x 512 px for crisp rendering everywhere.

**Design rules for server icons:**
- Keep it simple. At 48 px, details vanish. Bold shapes and high contrast survive.
- If using text, limit it to 1-2 characters maximum. A single letter mark works better than a wordmark.
- Animated icons are supported for boosted servers (Level 1+). Use this sparingly — a subtle pulse or rotation beats a seizure-inducing flash.

### Server Banner: 960 x 540 px

The server banner sits at the top of your channel list and is prime real estate for communicating what your community is about. Discord recommends 960 x 540 px with a 16:9 aspect ratio. File size cap is 10 MB and the format must be PNG, JPG, or GIF (animated banners require Level 2 boost).

**What makes a good server banner:**
- Show don't tell. Instead of text-heavy banners that say "WE ARE A GAMING COMMUNITY," use imagery that evokes gaming culture.
- The bottom 120 px is partially obscured by the server name overlay. Keep critical elements above that zone.
- Match the banner's color temperature to your icon. A neon-green banner with a navy icon screams "I didn't plan this."

### Server Invite Background: 1920 x 1080 px

When people click your invite link before joining, they see a preview screen with your server name, member count, and a background image. This is often the first impression. Upload at 1920 x 1080 px with a 16:9 ratio.

**The invite background is your billboard for conversion.** Use it to reinforce what makes your server worth joining. A clear value prop, social proof, or an aesthetic that telegraphs your community's vibe.

### Role Icons: 64 x 64 px

Role icons appear next to member names in the member list. They're small — roughly 24 px in practice — so keep them iconic, not informational. A crown for admins. A star for VIPs. A wrench for moderators. Custom role icons require Level 2 boost.

### Emojis & Stickers: 128 x 128 px (emoji) / 320 x 320 px (sticker)

Custom emojis are one of Discord's best engagement builders. They create inside jokes, visual shorthand, and community culture. Upload them at 128 x 128 px as PNG images. Stickers come in at 320 x 320 px and must be under 512 KB. More on sticker strategy below.

### Event Cover Images: 800 x 400 px

When you schedule a server event (voice chat, game night, AMA), Discord lets you attach a cover image at 800 x 400 px. These show up in the event banner at the top of your channel list. Use them to build anticipation and make events feel official.

## Visual Branding That Builds Community Culture

[IMAGE 3 PLACEHOLDER — Real UI Screenshot]

Getting the dimensions right is step one. Step two is making your server feel like a place, not a chatroom.

### 1. Define a Visual Identity System

Pick three things and lock them in:
- **Color Palette:** One primary, one accent, one neutral. Apply these to banners, role colors, and embed highlights.
- **Typography Style:** You can't control fonts inside Discord messages, but your banners and graphics should use a consistent typeface. One display font for headlines, one body font for any text overlays.
- **Visual Motif:** A recurring shape, pattern, or illustration style that ties everything together. Think of it as your server's "texture."

### 2. Design for the Dark Mode Majority

Over 90% of Discord users browse in dark mode. If your banners and icons assume a white background, they'll look harsh and out of place against Discord's dark gray UI. Design everything against a #36393f or #2f3136 background. Use softer whites (think #f0f0f0 instead of #ffffff) and avoid pure-black elements that disappear into the void.

### 3. Section Banners for Channel Categories

Channel category banners are an underrated design opportunity. A small horizontal graphic above each category section gives your server structure and makes navigation intuitive. At roughly 600 x 80 px per section banner, you can visually separate "Announcements" from "General Chat" from "Voice Channels."

### 4. Welcome Screens and Onboarding Visuals

Discord's welcome screen feature lets you customize the first thing new members see. Design a welcome graphic (960 x 540 px) that guides them through your server's channels, rules, and culture. Think of it as a mini landing page inside Discord.

## Sticker Strategy: The Engagement Engine You're Ignoring

Custom stickers are free to create (within Discord's 5-30 free upload slots depending on boost level). And yet, most servers never touch them. That's a miss.

**Stickers drive engagement because they're personal.** When someone reacts with a custom sticker you designed specifically for your community's inside jokes, they're not just responding — they're participating in shared culture.

**Sticker design tips:**
- Use 320 x 320 px PNGs with transparent backgrounds
- Keep designs simple and expressive — stickers are tiny
- Create sticker packs around themes: reactions (hype, agree, facepalm), inside jokes, community milestones
- Animate them if your server is Level 1+ boosted — animated stickers get 3x more usage
