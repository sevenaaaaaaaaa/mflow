# Lovart Digest Playbook: Make Weekly AI Design Notes Worth Reading (May W4 Lens)

The production slug for “May W4” was a padded clone pretending to be a digest. Inventing feature launches to fill it would be worse. This rewrite keeps the slug useful: a playbook for weekly AI design digests—what to include, what to ban, and how a May-week-4-style checkpoint should read when you care about operators, not hype.

## Who this is for

Editors, PMM, community leads, design ops writing internal or public weeklies.

## Digest promise

A good digest helps someone ship next week with fewer orphans. A bad digest is a rumor pile with adjectives.

## Hard ban list for digests

- Invented product releases  
- Fake user counts or “spotlight” quotes  
- Percentage myths without ledgers  
- Tool worship without boards  
- Trend names without kit implications  

## May W4 lens (how to use a dated slug)

Treat late May as a mid-quarter checkpoint: freeze seasonal accents that should have ended, audit Brand Kit drift after spring campaigns, and reset EXPLORE/PROD credit hygiene before summer launches. The date is a ritual, not an excuse to fabricate a changelog.

## Recommended section stack

1. **Decisions** — what we locked in the kit  
2. **Flops** — one failure with autopsy  
3. **Boards** — links/names of PROD templates updated  
4. **Craft note** — one technique (Touch Edit, Identity Lock, type bands)  
5. **Reading** — verified internal guides only  
6. **Ask** — one question for next week’s operators  

## Creator spotlight done honestly

If you lack permissioned stories, skip the section. Do not invent creators. If you have a real permissioned case, show before/after continuity and the board habit—not celebrity adjectives.

## “New features” section rules

Only ship notes you can verify from primary product surfaces. If unverified, omit. Silence beats fiction.

## Internal vs public digests

Internal can include credit burn and redo rates. Public should avoid sensitive client data. Same skeleton; different transparency.

## Flop diary (editorial)

### Flop: hype digest
We listed twelve “game-changing” items (even the word was a smell) with zero board links. Nobody changed behavior. Fix: decisions + flops only.

### Flop: zombie seasonal
Digest never told teams to kill expired accents. January still looked like Halloween. Fix: end-date checklist every W4-style checkpoint.

## Operating ritual (60 minutes)

0–10: pull kit diffs and board autopsies  
10–25: write decisions + one flop  
25–40: craft note with a mini how-to  
40–50: verify links  
50–60: cold-read by someone not on the team  

## Integrating Lovart tools into digest craft

Show a screenshot-style brief of Brand Kit locks, a ChatCanvas stop-rule example, and a Touch Edit save that prevented regen spend. Digests should teach the OS.

## Digest module: Subject lines

Promise a decision or a flop, not “exciting updates.”

## Digest module: Length

If it needs a scroll marathon, you are smuggling a blog. Split.

## Digest module: Metrics

Optional: redo rate, cold-handoff passes—never vanity.

## Digest module: Localization

Empty bands apply to digest images too.

## Digest module: Legal

No fake endorsements in spotlight art.

## Digest module: Cadence

Weekly or biweekly beats irregular guilt dumps.

## Digest module: Archive

Tag digests so kits can cite them later.

## Digest module: Comments

Route feedback into forbids/kit changes, not vibes.

## Digest module: Tool comparisons

Link verified guides; do not re-litigate stacks weekly.

## Digest module: Seasonal calendar

W4 checkpoints for end dates are sacred.

## Digest module: Template

Keep a ChatCanvas “digest cover” template under Brand Kit.

## Digest module: Ethics

Credit humans; do not scrape private boards into public notes.

## FAQ

### Did AI make design cost drop by a fixed percent?
No honest operator quotes a universal percentage. Unit economics depend on redo rates, kit memory, wages, and channel QA—not sampler demos.

### Can I skip Photoshop forever for mockups?
For many ecommerce/mock scenes, yes—with Brand Kit, refs, and local edits. Complex compositing and print prepress may still need dedicated tools.

### What makes a weekly digest worth reading?
Decisions, flops, kit changes, and links to working boards—not a laundry list of vague “new features” without proof.

### Where do I start in Lovart?
Brand Kit → MCoT plan → ChatCanvas → soft-proof → Touch Edit → lock winners.

### Should digests invent launch notes?
Never. If you lack verified release notes, write operator lessons and label speculation.
