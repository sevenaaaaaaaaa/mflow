## Choosing Your Color Palette

[IMAGE 2 PLACEHOLDER — Conceptual Diagram]

Pizzeria branding draws from three visual traditions: rustic Italian (terracotta, olive, cream), modern NYC slice-shop (bold red, white, black), and artisanal wood-fired (charcoal, warm amber, natural linen).

### Primary Colors
- **#C1121F (Classic Pizza Red)** — The universal pizzeria anchor. Evokes marinara, checkered tablecloths, and the neon "PIZZA" sign in every American downtown. Use as logo base and box-lid color.
- **#780000 (Deep Mahogany)** — A richer, more upscale alternative to bright red. Works for wood-fired and Neapolitan concepts that want to avoid fast-food associations.
- **#FDF0D5 (Parchment Cream)** — The color of 00 flour and aged dough. Essential as a background or secondary: it warms up red-heavy palettes and photographs beautifully.

### Secondary/Accent Colors
- **#669BBC (Denim Blue)** — Unexpected in pizzerias yet surprisingly effective. References blue-and-white Italian ceramics and contrasts warm reds.
- **#003049 (Prussian Blue)** — For menu headers and delivery-vehicle graphics. High-legibility dark contrast.
- **#EAE2B7 (Olive Gold)** — Olive oil in color form. Use for brand-pattern elements, loyalty cards, and table-tent accents.

## Typography Recommendations

### Logo/Display Font
**Playfair Display** (serif, Google Fonts) — Elegant, slightly old-world. Ideal for sit-down pizzerias and wood-fired concepts. For a quicker, slice-joint energy, **Lilita One** delivers bold, rounded friendliness that prints crisply on takeout boxes.

### Body/Text Font
**Lato** — Clean, warm sans-serif that handles everything from allergen disclaimers to wine-list descriptions. Pair with **Merriweather** for pizzerias that write longform origin stories on their menus.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q1: Should my pizzeria brand kit look different for dine-in vs. delivery?
Yes. Delivery-first branding needs brighter contrast and larger type (people scan DoorDash thumbnails at 1/10th the size of a physical menu). Lovart lets you keep a master palette while auto-generating delivery-optimized variants.

### Q2: What's the one branding mistake pizzerias make most?
Using clip-art pizza illustrations. Stock pizza art triggers a subconscious "frozen aisle" association. Generate custom food photography and vector illustrations through Lovart instead.

### Q3: How do I handle the brand kit across a pizza box, a website, and a neon sign?
Export your Lovart brand kit as a unified style guide with RGB (digital), CMYK (print), and spot-color (signage) values for every hex code. The box printer, web developer, and sign maker each get the correct spec.

### Q4: Can I include sub-brands for wings, subs, and desserts?
Yes — build a parent brand kit with a secondary color palette that shifts per category (e.g., amber for wings, green for salads, raspberry for desserts). Lovart's template system handles this with category toggles.

### Q5: What fonts work on a greasy pizza box?
Matte-finish boxes need fonts with open counters and generous spacing (Lato, Open Sans). Avoid thin serifs (they fill in during printing) and scripts with tight kerning (they smear at small sizes).

### Q6: Is it worth branding my delivery-driver car toppers?
Absolutely. Car toppers are moving billboards seen by thousands daily. A simple logo + phone-number design in your brand kit's high-contrast palette costs under $30 per topper and generates measurable walk-in traffic.
