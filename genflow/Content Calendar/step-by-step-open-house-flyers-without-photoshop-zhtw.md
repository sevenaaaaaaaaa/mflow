## Step 2: Present the Hero Image and Key Information

The front page has one job: make someone walking or driving by stop and take the flyer. Lovart's **Flyer Front Builder** enforces the critical rule — one large hero photo, not a grid of tiny ones. Import your best property photo (exterior shot with good curb appeal, or the most impressive interior space — kitchen or living room with natural light) into the top 60% of the front panel. Below the photo, in the bottom 40%, display: the price (largest text on the page — at least 48pt, bold), a one-line headline describing the property's best feature ("Mid-Century Ranch with Pool and Mountain Views"), the 3 key specs in a horizontal row with icon labels (beds, baths, sqft), and the open house date and time in a highlighted badge format. The address goes at the very bottom in 11pt — prominent enough but secondary to the emotional pull of the photo and description.

## Step 3: Design the Back Panel for Deeper Engagement

Someone who flips the flyer over is interested. Give them substance. Lovart's **Back Panel Builder** accommodates: 3–4 additional property photos in a clean grid (with 4px spacing — tight but distinct), 5 bullet-point property highlights (hardwood floors, updated HVAC, new roof, etc.) formatted as a scannable list with small icon markers, a **Location Map** — Lovart generates a simplified neighborhood map showing the property pin and nearby landmarks (schools, parks, shopping) from OpenStreetMap data, and an **Agent Contact Block** with headshot (40×40px round crop), name, phone, email, and brokerage logo. Add a **QR Code** in the bottom corner: link to the full MLS listing with all photos, virtual tour, and floor plan.

## Step 4: Apply Real Estate Design Psychology

Real estate flyer design influences perceived property value. Lovart's **Real Estate Design Advisor** applies industry-specific best practices: **Color Psychology** — navy blue conveys trust and professionalism; gold accents suggest premium quality (use sparingly — too much gold reads as gaudy rather than upscale). **Photo Selection** — the Advisor flags common mistakes: exterior shots taken on overcast days (auto-suggests brightness and warmth enhancement), photos that accidentally include the current owner's personal items (privacy concern), and photos with lens distortion that makes rooms look smaller (auto-suggests perspective correction). **Typography** — real estate flyers demand high-contrast type: bold headlines at 36pt+, body text never below 10pt (the average home buyer is 45–54 years old; smaller type alienates the primary demographic).

## FAQ

**Q: Should I include the listing price on the open house flyer?**
A: Yes. Most buyers filter by price before anything else. Hiding the price on the flyer increases wasted conversations and reduces qualified foot traffic. Place it prominently on the front.

**Q: How many photos should an open house flyer have?**
A: 4–6 total — 1 hero on the front, 3–5 on the back. Fewer than 4 fails to convey the property; more than 6 overwhelms a half-page format. Quality over quantity: one stunning kitchen photo sells better than six mediocre room shots.

**Q: Can Lovart generate property descriptions for the flyer?**
A: Yes. Use the AI content generator with inputs: property type, location, key features, and target buyer demographic. Lovart produces a 40–60 word description. Always review for factual accuracy — especially square footage, year built, and feature claims.

**Q: Does Lovart include fair housing compliance checks on flyer designs?**
A: Lovart's Compliance Mode flags language that may violate Fair Housing Act guidelines (e.g., "perfect for families" implies familial status discrimination). The checker provides suggested neutral alternatives. Final compliance responsibility rests with the agent and broker.

**Q: Can I create flyers for multiple properties in one batch?**
A: On the $49 Pro plan, use the Real Estate Batch tool — import a CSV with property addresses, specs, photo URLs, and open house dates, and Lovart generates flyers for an entire weekend's open houses in one operation.

**Q: What's the recommended print quantity for an open house?**
A: Print 50–75 flyers for a standard 3-hour open house in an average-traffic neighborhood. For high-traffic areas or luxury properties, print 100. Lovart's print imposition exports are optimized for these quantity ranges. Always print at least 25 extra for door-knocking neighboring homes post-event.
