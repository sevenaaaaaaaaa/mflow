## The Print-on-Demand Workflow

Lovart's merch design pipeline is built for the POD (print-on-demand) business model:

### Step 1: Describe Your Design
```
@design "A retro 80s synthwave t-shirt with a neon sunset, palm trees silhouette, and the text 'NOSTALGIA' in a chrome 3D font. Bold colors. Dark background."
```

The AI generates 4 design variations exploring different takes on your concept — different compositions, different color accents, different typographic treatments.

### Step 2: Refine With Touch Edit
Don't love the typography on variation 3? Touch it and change the font. Want the palm trees bigger on variation 1? Scale them up. The neon gradient not quite right? Adjust it. This is the step where your taste meets AI efficiency — and it's the step Design.com and DZine skip entirely.

### Step 3: Preview on Mockup
Select a mockup template — t-shirt (multiple colors), hoodie, tote bag, phone case, mug, hat, crewneck — and Lovart maps your design onto it with realistic fabric distortion and lighting. See what it looks like draped on a person, not just flat on a screen.

### Step 4: Export
- **Transparent PNG** at 300 DPI (minimum 4500×5100 pixels for a standard t-shirt print area) — upload directly to Printful, Printify, Gelato, or any POD platform.
- **Layered PSD** if you need to make further adjustments in Photoshop.
- **SVG** for designs that benefit from vector sharpness at any scale.

## What Makes a T-Shirt Design Sell?

Lovart's AI has been trained on what works in apparel — not just what looks good on a screen:

### Contrast & Readability
A design that looks crisp on your monitor can turn into a muddy mess on fabric. Lovart's merch mode optimizes for the medium: higher contrast, bolder lines, and colors that work against typical shirt base colors (black, white, heather grey, navy).

### Placement Awareness
A design that works as a square social post does not work as a t-shirt print. Lovart understands print area constraints: center chest (standard placement), full front (all-over), left chest (small logo placement), back (large canvas), sleeve (small accent). Each placement has different proportions and the AI adjusts accordingly.

### Style Trends
Retro vintage, Y2K aesthetic, minimalist typography, maximalist illustration, Japanese-inspired, streetwear bold — Lovart's style library covers the aesthetics that are actually selling right now. Design.com's templates lean corporate. DZine's outputs feel generic without heavy prompt engineering.
