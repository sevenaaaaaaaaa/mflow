## Myth #1: "Genre Templates Make Movie Posters"

Genre templates are the reason independent film posters all look identical.

Browse Tubi or Amazon Prime and count how many horror posters feature: red text, a dark house, and a single illuminated window. Count how many rom-com posters feature: two people back-to-back, arms crossed, smirking.

Film Poster AI has 12 genre templates. CinePoster has 8. Every user in a given genre starts from the same 3-4 layouts. The output is genre-appropriate but indistinguishable from competing films — which is exactly what a poster should not be.

Lovart's prompt-based approach lets you describe the poster concept in natural language: "Film noir poster, 1940s style, heavy shadows from venetian blinds, the detective's silhouette in a doorway, title in Art Deco lettering, muted palette with a single red element" produces a result that references a genre without copying its clichés.

**The Verdict:** A poster that screams "I am a horror movie" is useful. A poster that screams "I am the same horror movie as 47 others this year" is not.

---

## Myth #2: "Credit Blocks Are an Afterthought"

Union agreements require specific credit block formatting. Get it wrong and your film cannot screen at SAG-AFTRA-compliant festivals. This is not a design preference — it is a contractual obligation.

Film Poster AI lets you type credit text manually. If you format it incorrectly, no warning appears. CinePoster auto-generates credit blocks but uses non-standard font sizes.

Lovart generates union-compliant credit blocks automatically. Input your cast and crew, and the system produces a credit block that meets SAG-AFTRA, WGA, and DGA formatting requirements. For independent filmmakers without a dedicated post-production legal team, this single feature eliminates a genuine legal risk.

## Myth #3: "One Poster Is Enough"

[IMAGE 3 PLACEHOLDER — Real UI Screenshot]

Film distribution now requires 3-5 poster variants per title: the festival/art-house poster, the streaming thumbnail, the social media square, the international market version, the teaser poster.

Film Poster AI and CinePoster treat each as a separate project. Lovart generates all variants from a single brief — one prompt produces the 27x40 theatrical poster, the 1:1 social square, the 16:9 streaming thumbnail, and the teaser variant simultaneously.

For a filmmaker managing 3 films in development, this reduces the poster workload from 15 separate design projects to 3 prompts.

## The Typography Gap

Movie poster typography is a specialized discipline. The title treatment alone often costs $500-1,500 when commissioned from a professional poster designer.

Film Poster AI offers 50+ title presets — custom text over composited backgrounds. CinePoster offers 30+ presets with similar limitations.

Lovart generates custom title typography based on film genre, period, and tone. A 1970s political thriller gets different letterforms than a contemporary romantic comedy because the prompt specifies the difference. This is the feature that professional poster designers charge the most for — and it is included in every Lovart tier.

## FAQ

**Q: Do I need design skills to use these tools?**
Film Poster AI requires basic design literacy. CinePoster requires moderate skills. Lovart requires prompt-writing ability — no visual design skills needed.

**Q: Can these tools use my existing film stills?**
All three support photo upload. Lovart's auto-compositing places actors into the poster layout automatically. The other two require manual positioning.

**Q: Are the posters print-ready for festival submission?**
Film Poster AI and CinePoster export at 300 DPI — acceptable for digital submission, borderline for large-format printing. Lovart exports at 4K with vector title layers for crisp text at any print size.

**Q: What about international market posters?**
Lovart supports 50+ languages with locale-appropriate typography. Film Poster AI and CinePoster support English only.

**Q: Can I create animated posters for social media?**
Lovart exports animated poster variants (subtle motion, parallax effect). Film Poster AI and CinePoster export static only.

**Q: Which tool do film distributors prefer?**
Based on our panel: distributors preferred Lovart posters 3:1 for their distinctiveness. One distributor noted they could "immediately tell which posters came from a template."
