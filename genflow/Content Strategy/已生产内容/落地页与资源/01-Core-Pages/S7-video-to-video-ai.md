**Video to video AI** gives you exactly that. Feed it your raw footage. Tell it how you want it to look — anime, oil painting, pixel art, cinematic film grade, charcoal sketch, cyberpunk — and the AI transforms every frame, preserving motion and structure while completely reinventing the aesthetic.

## How Video-to-Video Style Transfer Works

The process is simpler than you'd expect:

1. **Upload your video** — Any MP4, MOV, or WebM file. Short clips (5–30 seconds) work best for style transfer, though Lovart handles up to 3 minutes on Pro and Ultimate plans.
2. **Choose a style** — Select from the style library or describe a custom aesthetic.
3. **Generate** — The AI processes each frame through the style model, maintaining temporal consistency (so frames don't flicker or jump between styles).
4. **Touch Edit** — This is where Lovart diverges from every other tool in this space. After transfer, you can scrub through the timeline and touch-edit individual frames or regions. A character's face looking distorted at second 4? Fix it. Background artifacts at second 12? Inpaint them. The sky not dramatic enough? Adjust it.
5. **Export** — MP4 at your chosen resolution and frame rate. ProRes option on Pro plans.

## Style Transfer Options

### Real Footage → Animation
Transform live-action video into an animated short. Your product demo becomes a 2D cartoon. Your talking-head video becomes an anime sequence. Your travel footage becomes a Studio Ghibli-esque landscape reel. The AI maps real-world motion and depth onto animated visual language.

Pollo AI can do this in short bursts, but consistency breaks over clips longer than 5 seconds. Lovart's temporal coherence engine maintains style from frame 1 to frame 300.

### Real Footage → Oil Painting
Every frame looks like it was painted in oils. Brushstroke texture, impasto lighting, canvas grain. Use it for music videos, artistic brand films, wedding highlight reels — any project where you want to elevate raw footage into fine art.

### Real Footage → Pixel Art
Downgrade resolution intentionally for that retro 8-bit or 16-bit game aesthetic. Sprite-like characters moving through pixelated environments. Works brilliantly for gaming content, music videos in the synthwave genre, and nostalgic marketing campaigns.

### Real Footage → Cinematic Grade
This isn't just a color LUT — it's a full cinematographic restyling. The AI adjusts lighting ratios, adds film grain, applies anamorphic lens characteristics, and tweaks depth of field. Your smartphone footage comes out looking like it was shot on an ARRI Alexa.

Luma AI's video generation can produce cinematic-looking clips from scratch, but it can't take *your* existing footage and cinematic-grade it. That's where video-to-video style transfer has a distinct advantage.

### Real Footage → Sketch / Line Art
Live action becomes hand-drawn linework. Bodies and objects rendered as expressive pen strokes in motion. A striking, distinctive look for title sequences, lyric videos, and artistic social content.

## Temporal Consistency: The Hard Problem Nobody Talks About

The reason most **video to video AI** tools produce disappointing results is temporal inconsistency — the style flickers, shifts, or morphs between frames. One second your character looks like an oil painting, the next they look like a watercolor, and it's jarring enough to ruin the illusion.

Lovart's MCoT engine addresses this with a temporal coherence pass that runs after initial frame-by-frame styling. It compares adjacent frames for style drift, identifies discontinuities, and smooths the transitions. The result is a stylized video that feels like it was *created* in that style, not like a filter was applied hastily.

Pollo AI and Luma both struggle with temporal consistency on clips longer than a few seconds. Artlist doesn't have this capability at all — its AI tools are limited to basic color grading and background removal.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: How long does video style transfer take?**
A: A 10-second clip typically takes 1–2 minutes to process. A 60-second clip takes 5–10 minutes. Longer clips scale roughly linearly — a 3-minute video on the Pro plan processes in about 20–30 minutes.

**Q: Can I use style transfer on talking-head videos?**
A: Yes, with the caveat that some styles work better for faces than others. Cinematic, anime, and oil painting handle faces well. Pixel art and line art will abstract facial detail — fine for artistic purposes, not ideal if recognizability matters.

**Q: Does the audio stay intact?**
A: Yes. The original audio track passes through the transfer process untouched and syncs to the stylized video output.

**Q: Can I combine multiple styles in one video?**
A: Not automatically. The style is applied uniformly. But you can split your video into segments, style each differently, and recombine them in Lovart's canvas — effectively creating a multi-style video.
