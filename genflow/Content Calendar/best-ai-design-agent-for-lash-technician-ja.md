### 2. Lash Mapping Guides: Educating Clients About Their Eyes

Lash mapping — the placement plan that determines curl type, length progression, and density across the lash line — is the technical art behind a beautiful set. Clients who understand mapping become better at communicating what they want ("I want a cat eye with a 12mm outer corner" instead of "I want them pretty"). Mapping guides position you as an expert and reduce revision appointments from "I wanted them different."

Lovart creates mapping guides that educate and upsell. "Design a lash mapping style guide showing our 6 signature styles: 1) Natural Eye — follows the natural lash line contour, uniform length, for balanced enhancement, 2) Open Eye — shorter inner and outer corners, longest at center (8-12-8mm graduation), makes eyes appear larger/rounder, best for almond and downturned eyes, 3) Cat Eye — gradual lengthening from inner to outer (8-14mm), elongates and lifts, best for round and close-set eyes, 4) Doll Eye — extra length in center with soft taper to ends, creates wide-eyed youthful look, best for hooded and deep-set eyes, 5) Kim K — dramatic outer corner emphasis with wispy texture, celebrity glam, 6) Squirrel — longest point 2/3 toward outer corner, natural lift with softness. Each style: an eye-shaped diagram showing the curl type (C, CC, D) and the mm progression across the lash line, a photo of that style on a model, and 'Best For' face/eye shape recommendations. Plus a 'Find Your Style' quiz flow — 'What's your eye shape?' → 'Round' → 'We recommend: Cat Eye or Doll Eye — here's why.' Design: technical but beautiful — like an architect's elevation meets a beauty editorial. Our brand, educational, shareable — clients will send this to their friends."

### 3. Aftercare Cards & Retention Marketing: Keeping Clients Coming Back

Lash retention is the profitability engine of a lash business. A client who gets a full set at $185 and never returns is worth $185. A client who gets fills every 2.5 weeks for 2 years is worth $5,000+. The gap between the two is largely determined by: how well they follow aftercare instructions (retention), how easy you make it to rebook (convenience), and how you remind them you exist between appointments (marketing).

Lovart creates the retention marketing system.
