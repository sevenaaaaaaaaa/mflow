## Questions Nobody Answers

### What makes a movie poster "cinematic"?

Cinematic poster design follows specific visual conventions:
- **Color grading:** Teal/orange for action, desaturated for drama, high-contrast for horror, warm gold for period pieces
- **Lighting:** Dramatic, motivated light sources. Rim lights, god rays, chiaroscuro. Flat lighting reads as "TV," not "cinema."
- **Composition:** Hero character placement, rule of thirds emphasis, depth through atmospheric perspective
- **Typography:** Custom or heavily modified title treatments. Trajan for epics, sans-serif for modern thrillers, distressed serifs for horror
- **Texture:** Film grain, lens effects, atmospheric haze — the textural language of celluloid

AI understands these conventions and applies them when prompted with genre direction. The results read as "movie poster" immediately.

### Which movie poster genres does AI handle best?

Ranked by AI quality:
1. **Horror:** Excellent. Dark, high-contrast, texture-heavy. AI excels at creepy atmosphere.
2. **Sci-fi:** Very good. Neon lighting, vast environments, tech details. AI generates compelling sci-fi worlds.
3. **Action/Thriller:** Very good. Explosions, dramatic poses, urban environments.
4. **Drama:** Good. Character-focused compositions, emotional lighting. Character faces are the weak point.
5. **Comedy:** Moderate. Comedy depends on specific character expressions and timing — hard to prompt.
6. **Romance:** Moderate. The chemistry between two characters is a subtle signal AI doesn't reliably capture.

### Can AI generate professional title treatments?

AI-generated title typography has improved dramatically but isn't yet at the level of custom hand-crafted movie titles. AI can generate:
- **Style-appropriate title concepts:** Font selections, treatments, effects that match the genre
- **Basic custom lettering:** Distressed, glowing, metallic, dimensional effects
- **Title placement:** Positioned correctly in the poster hierarchy

AI cannot (yet) generate:
- **Truly custom letterforms:** Unique, bespoke lettering that becomes the film's brand
- **Complex ligatures and letter interactions:** The subtle kerning, overlapping, and integration that defines professional title design
- **Vector-precise output:** AI titles are raster and need vector tracing for print production

Workflow: use AI for title concepts and style direction → finalize with a typography tool (Illustrator, Photoshop) or commission a title designer for the final treatment.

### How do I create an ensemble cast poster with AI?

The character-poster approach:
1. Generate each character individually with consistent style.
2. Composite characters into the poster layout manually (or use Lovart's Ensemble layout).
3. AI fills backgrounds, atmosphere, and lighting effects.
4. Add title treatment and credits.

This hybrid approach — AI characters + human composition — produces the best ensemble posters. Pure AI generation of multi-character compositions often produces anatomical errors, inconsistent face styles, and awkward character interactions.

### What about poster credits and billing block?

The billing block (the dense legal text at the bottom of every movie poster) is formulaic and specific. AI can generate visually correct billing blocks that contain gibberish — wrong names, fake studios, inaccurate credits.

Solution: use AI to generate the visual layout with a placeholder billing block. Replace the placeholder with your actual credits text. Lovart's Billing Block tool generates correctly formatted credits from your cast/crew input — it's a template, not AI generation, which is exactly what you want for legal text.

### Can AI generate a full key art campaign (teaser, main poster, character posters)?

Yes — this is the strongest commercial application. Workflow:
1. Generate the main poster — this defines the visual identity.
2. Lock the style system (color grade, lighting approach, typographic language, texture treatment).
3. Generate teaser poster (simpler, more mysterious, earlier in the narrative).
4. Generate character posters (individual character focus, consistent background treatment).
5. Generate digital adaptations (social media crops, streaming thumbnail, outdoor billboard).

Lovart's Campaign mode handles this sequence with locked style across all assets. What would take an agency 2-4 weeks takes a Lovart user 2-4 hours.

### How does AI handle period/genre authenticity?

AI understands period aesthetics through training data — 1920s noir, 1980s synthwave, Victorian gothic, 1970s grindhouse. Specify both the period and the reference: "1970s psychological horror poster, Saul Bass-inspired graphic design, limited color palette, distressed texture."

Accuracy is strongest for heavily-documented periods (1940s-1990s cinema) and weaker for pre-cinema eras or highly specific regional aesthetics. The AI captures the "vibe" of a period more reliably than the specific historical details.

### Can I use AI movie posters for commercial film releases?

Yes, with the same AI art commercial licensing considerations. Lovart Pro ($49+) includes full commercial rights. For theatrical distribution, consult your distributor and their legal team — major distributors may have specific requirements for key art provenance.

Independent and streaming releases face fewer restrictions. The primary risk is AI-generated elements that unintentionally resemble copyrighted poster designs. Review your AI poster against existing posters in the same genre before finalizing.

### What resolution is needed for theatrical poster printing?

Standard theatrical one-sheet: 27×40 inches at 300 DPI = 8100×12000 pixels. AI generation at this resolution requires upscaling from the standard 2048-4096 pixel generation range.

Workflow: generate poster concept at 2048×4096 → select final design → AI upscale to 8100×12000 → manual detail check at 100% zoom → export PDF/X-4 with bleed.

### How do I avoid making a poster that looks "AI-generated"?

The tells of AI movie posters:
- Overly symmetrical composition
- Generic "floating heads" layout (the most common AI poster default)
- Inconsistent character scale
- Perfect but soulless lighting
- Title treatments that look generated, not designed

To break the AI look:
- Specify asymmetric, unconventional compositions
- Use negative space intentionally — AI fears empty space
- Request specific cinematic references: "Saul Bass minimalism," "Polish film poster surrealism," "Criterion Collection aesthetic"
- Add human-directed imperfections: asymmetrical cropping, unconventional color choices, deliberate texture

## What Most Guides Won't Tell You

[IMAGE 3 PLACEHOLDER — Real UI Screenshot]

**The best movie posters tell you nothing and everything.** A great poster communicates genre, tone, and emotional promise without revealing plot. AI defaults to literal "here's the main character looking dramatic." Push for mystery, metaphor, and mood over literal representation.

**Film festival posters have different rules.** Festival programmers see hundreds of posters. Yours must read clearly as a thumbnail on a festival website. Test your poster at 200×300 pixels. If it doesn't communicate at that size, simplify.

**The poster is the film's first trailer.** Before anyone watches your trailer or reads your synopsis, they see your poster. The poster makes a promise. The film must keep it. AI can generate a poster that overpromises — be honest about your film's tone and production values.
