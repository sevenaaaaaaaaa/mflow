# Agency Playbook: Manage 10+ Client Brands in Lovart Without Chaos

The third time a junior designer exports Client A's palette onto Client B's launch ads, you stop calling it a mistake and start calling it a systems failure. Agencies do not lose their minds because Lovart is hard. They lose their minds because ten Brand Kits collide inside one messy operating culture.

This Lovart 101 playbook is how I have seen agencies run 10+ client brands without constant bleed: isolate kits, encode bans, batch under Identity Lock, write approval SLAs, and measure accepted assets per client. Lovart is the design agent surface. Process is the product.

## 1. The Agency Problem Is Brand Bleed

Symptoms:

- shared exploration folders across clients
- contractors without kit links
- Friday panic regenerations
- launch week restyles
- no naming conventions
- no reverse audits

Tools cannot fix a missing operating system.

### TL;DR

- One client, one Brand Kit, zero casual borrowing.
- Identity Lock for client campaign volume.
- Approval SLA in writing.
- Naming and destination cards are non-negotiable.
- Measure accepted assets and bleed incidents.

## 2. Workspace Hygiene

```text
Rule                              | Why
----------------------------------|----------------------------------
Separate kit per client           | Prevents palette bleed
Approved vs exploration folders   | Stops orphan promotion
Client code in every filename     | Makes archaeology possible
Access by least privilege         | Contractors see only their clients
Weekly reverse audit              | Drift is found early
```

## 3. Brand Kit as Contract

Treat the Brand Kit like a statement of work attachment:

- color roles
- type roles
- motif bans
- anti-references
- logo clearspace
- export defaults

If it is not in the kit, it is not approved.

## 4. Production Formula

Context → Constraints → Canvas → Correction → Conversion

1. Context: client brief + campaign job
2. Constraints: kit + bans
3. Canvas: ChatCanvas iteration
4. Correction: Touch Edit local faults
5. Conversion: destination exports + names + approval record

## 5. Identity Lock for Multi-Client Volume

When an agency ships weekly social for many clients, Identity Lock is how you prevent "creative boredom" from inventing unauthorized styles. Boredom is not a brand strategy.

## 6. Approval SLAs

Write:

- who can soft-approve
- who can hard-approve for paid
- response time expectations
- what happens when the client is silent
- revision round limits

Silent clients plus unsupervised generators create expensive surprises.

## 7. Contractor Onboarding Packet

Send before access:

- Brand Kit link
- anti-reference list
- naming rules
- destination cards
- banned prompt patterns
- approval SLA
- folder map

"Make it pop" is not onboarding.

## 8. Cost and Margin Math

Track per client:

- accepted assets
- revision rounds
- bleed incidents
- rush regenerations
- unused seat waste

Agencies die on invisible thrash more often than on software fees.

## 9. Best Practices Checklist

1. Kit per client
2. No cross-client exploration mixing
3. Identity Lock on campaign batches
4. Naming with client codes
5. Destination cards
6. Human veto named
7. Weekly reverse audit
8. Freeze kits during client launches unless broken
9. Retire unused seats
10. Document decisions on cards

## 10. Failure Patterns

- Shared "inspiration" folders that become production
- Juniors with access to every kit
- No SLA, infinite revisions
- Launch-week restyles
- JPEG logos in print packs
- Measuring generations instead of accepted assets

## 11. Case Notes

### Case A — 12-client social retainer
Folder isolation + Identity Lock cut bleed incidents to near zero in a month.

### Case B — Product launch pod
Approval SLA stopped silent-client chaos; export packs shipped on time.

### Case C — Failed path
One shared workspace, ten clients, constant palette accidents. Split kits and access; sanity returned.

Days 1-3: inventory clients, kits, access.  
Days 4-7: isolate folders; encode missing kits.

Days 13-16: contractor packet + SLA templates.  
Days 17-21: reverse audit ritual + metrics dashboard.

## FAQ

### Can one team run 10+ brands in Lovart?
Yes with isolation, locks, SLAs, and audits. No with shared chaos.

### Does Lovart replace account management?
No. It replaces fragmented production thrash when process exists.

### What is the first fix if we are already bleeding?
