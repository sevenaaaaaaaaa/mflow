## The Chat-Generate AI Effects Workflow

### Step 1 — Choose Your Effect Category

AI effects fall into broad buckets. Pick yours before prompting:

- **Atmospheric effects**: Glows, light leaks, fog, dust motes, god rays, lens flares, bokeh overlays. These add depth and cinematic quality to flat images.
- **Particle effects**: Sparkles, embers, snow, confetti, magical dust, energy trails, digital particles. These add motion energy and focal interest.
- **Transformation effects**: Double exposure, dispersion (shatter/disintegrate), liquify/distort, pixel-sort, glitch. These create surreal, attention-grabbing visuals.
- **Color grade effects**: Cyberpunk neon, vintage film, golden hour warmth, bleach bypass, technicolor, monochrome with single-color accent. These shift the emotional tone.
- **Composite effects**: Merging two images, background replacement with style transfer, object isolation with glow, shadow manipulation. These solve specific image problems.

Naming the effect category in your prompt — "Apply an atmospheric glow effect to this product image" — focuses the agent on one visual transformation rather than a vague "make it look cool."

### Step 2 — Write an Effect-Specific Prompt

Generic effect prompts produce generic results. Specific effect prompts produce controlled, professional-looking results. Here are prompt templates for each major effect type:

**Atmospheric Glow on Product**:
```
Apply a soft, ethereal glow effect to this product image [attach or describe image]. 
The glow should emanate from the product itself — as if it is gently illuminated from within. 
Warm golden light (#F5D76E) radiating outward, fading to transparency at the edges. 
Soft, diffused, not harsh or neon. The glow should feel premium and subtle — 
like a candle's light, not a flashlight. Preserve all product details and textures. 
The background should darken slightly to make the glow read more prominently. 
Editorial beauty photography with atmospheric lighting.
```

**Particle Dispersion Effect on Portrait**:
```
Apply a dispersion effect to this portrait image. Starting from the subject's right shoulder, 
the body dissolves into floating geometric particles (small triangles and diamonds) 
that drift upward and to the right, becoming smaller and more transparent as they go. 
The left side of the subject remains solid and intact. The particles should be in the subject's 
color palette — skin tones, clothing colors — with a subtle metallic shimmer. 
Dark background for contrast. Cinematic, surreal, album-cover energy. 
The transition zone between solid and dispersed should be gradual — not a hard edge.
```

**Cyberpunk Color Grade on Street Photo**:
```
Apply a cyberpunk color grade to this street photo. Shift the color palette dramatically: 
warm ambient light should become neon pink (#FF2A6D), cool shadows should become deep teal 
(#05D9E8). Add subtle digital artifacts — faint scan lines, slight chromatic aberration 
at contrast edges, a subtle HUD-like overlay at 5% opacity in the corners. 
The overall mood should be Blade Runner 2049 — wet streets, neon reflections, 
high contrast, deep blacks with crushed shadows. The original composition remains intact; 
only the color and atmosphere shift.
```

**Double Exposure Portrait + Landscape**:
```
Create a double exposure effect. Primary image: a profile portrait of a person 
(silhouette-like, side view). Secondary image (visible inside the silhouette): 
a dense forest at sunrise — pine trees, mist, golden light rays piercing through. 
The portrait outline defines the shape; the forest fills and extends slightly beyond the edges, 
fading out. Black background outside the silhouette. High contrast between the dark exterior 
and the warm, luminous interior. Artistic, editorial, National Geographic cover style.
```

### Step 3 — Apply Effects to Your Existing Images

The most practical use of AI effects is enhancing images you already have:

```
Take this product photo [upload or describe] and apply three effects in sequence:

EFFECT 1: Clean background replacement — remove the current cluttered background 
and replace with a smooth, dark gradient (#1A1A2E to #0D0D1A). The product should be cleanly isolated.

EFFECT 2: Add a subtle edge rim light — a thin, bright highlight tracing the product's 
left edge, as if a soft key light is positioned off-frame left. Silver-white, 30% opacity.

EFFECT 3: Add floating accent particles — tiny glowing dots in the brand's accent color 
(#FF6B6B) floating around the product, at varying depths (some sharp, some blurred for depth of field). 
The particles should suggest "premium, active, alive" — not "Christmas lights."

Export the final composited image.
```

Sequential effect application — one prompt per effect, building on the previous result — gives you more control than asking for everything in one prompt.

### Step 4 — Generate Effect Templates for Batch Use

If you apply the same effects across a product line or content series, codify them:

```
[Effect Template: Premium Product Hero]
1. Background: Dark gradient (#1A1A2E to #0D0D1A), clean, minimal.
2. Product rim light: Soft white edge highlight, left side, 30% opacity.
3. Atmospheric glow: Warm (#F5D76E) inner glow behind the product, diffused.
4. Accent particles: Brand coral (#FF6B6B) floating dots, varied depth.
5. Export: 1200x1200, JPG 95% quality.
```

Apply to any product in your catalog for instant visual consistency.

### Step 5 — Know When to Stop

The most common AI effects mistake is over-application. Effects are like seasoning — the right amount enhances; too much ruins. If your image has glow AND particles AND dispersion AND a color grade AND lens flares, it does not look premium. It looks like a teenager discovered Photoshop filters in 2004.

Rule of thumb: one dominant effect + one supporting effect maximum. If you have a glow, you do not also need particles. If you have a cyberpunk color grade, you do not also need dispersion. Let one visual idea carry the image.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I apply multiple effects to the same image?**

Yes. Apply them sequentially — one effect per prompt, building on the previous result. This gives you better control than requesting multiple effects in one prompt. Review each effect before moving to the next.

**Will the effects look fake or obviously AI-generated?**

Over-application is the enemy of realism. Use effects subtly. Prompt for "subtle," "soft," "diffused," "natural" when you want enhancement rather than transformation. The best effects are the ones viewers do not consciously notice.

**Can I save and reuse effect combinations?**

Yes. Create an effect template as a saved prompt in your Lovart workspace. Apply the same effect to every product photo in a catalog, every thumbnail in a series, or every post in a content calendar. Visual consistency at scale.

**Can the agent add effects to a photo I upload?**

Yes. Upload your photo and prompt: "Apply a cinematic color grade to this photo — warm golden hour tones, lifted blacks, slight film grain." The agent processes your existing image.

**What about video effects?**

The agent generates static images with visual effects applied. For video, export your keyframes with effects applied and sequence them. For animated effects (particles moving, glows pulsing), the static generated images serve as style references for video editing software.

**Can I remove existing effects or artifacts from an image?**

Yes. Prompt: "Clean up this image — remove the harsh flash reflection, reduce the over-saturated reds, remove the timestamp watermark in the bottom right, and apply natural color correction." The agent can remove unwanted elements while the effect prompts add desired ones.
