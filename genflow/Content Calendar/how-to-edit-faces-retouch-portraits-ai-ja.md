## The Journey: Five Fixes, One Tool

### Fix 1 — Smile Adjustment (The "I Look Constipated" Problem)

This is the number one face edit request by a wide margin. A forced smile looks unnatural because it only engages the mouth, not the eyes. A genuine smile — the Duchenne smile — involves the orbicularis oculi muscles around the eyes.

**Lovart workflow:**

1. Upload your portrait to Lovart.
2. Use ChatCanvas to select the mouth and eye region (both — do not just adjust the mouth).
3. Type the prompt: `natural genuine smile, Duchenne smile, eyes smiling, relaxed happy expression, preserve all other facial features, maintain skin texture and lighting`
4. Generate. Review.
5. If the smile is too intense (Joker territory), add: `subtle closed-mouth smile, gentle upturn of corners, warm but natural`

**What to avoid:** Never use the word "big smile" or "wide smile" in your prompt — the AI interprets this literally and produces unsettling results. "Natural," "genuine," and "subtle" are safer vectors.

### Fix 2 — Skin Smoothing Without the Plastic Look

AI can smooth skin. AI can also turn you into a porcelain doll with no pores. The difference is the prompt.

**Two-tier approach:**

**For natural portraits (preferred):**
```
subtle skin retouching, reduce blemishes and uneven texture, preserve natural skin pores, maintain realistic skin detail, no plastic effect, no airbrush look, natural skin
```

**For editorial/beauty:**
```
professional beauty retouch, smooth even skin, subtle glow, studio lighting on skin, magazine editorial quality, removed blemishes, preserved natural skin texture
```

The key word is "preserve" — it tells the model to keep what is already there rather than generating new, perfectly smooth skin from scratch. A real face has texture. Remove the texture and you have a mannequin.

### Fix 3 — Eye Correction (Blinking, Red-Eye, Gaze Direction)

Blinking in photos is the universe's way of reminding you that photography is a probabilistic art. AI fixes this.

**Closed eyes / blinking:**
Select the eye region in ChatCanvas. Prompt: `eyes open, looking at camera, natural expression, match original eye color and shape, realistic sclera, catchlight in eyes`

**Gaze direction fix (looking at wrong camera):**
Prompt: `eyes looking directly at camera, natural eye contact, slight catchlight reflection, preserve eye color and shape`

**Red-eye removal:**
Lovart's AI detects and corrects red-eye automatically in most cases. For stubborn red-eye, select the pupil region and prompt: `natural dark pupil, subtle iris detail, remove red reflection, realistic eye`

### Fix 4 — Lighting Correction

Bad lighting cannot be fixed by increasing brightness. Increasing brightness washes out highlights and lifts shadows to gray. AI can re-light the face entirely — simulating a different light source position.

**ChatCanvas prompt for lighting fix:**
```
relight face with [describe desired lighting], preserve skin texture and color, natural light falloff, realistic shadows, [direction] key light, [intensity] fill light
```

**Lighting recipes by problem:**

| Problem | Prompt |
|---------|--------|
| Flat, no dimension | "add Rembrandt lighting, dramatic triangle of light on cheek, soft shadows" |
| Harsh overhead shadows | "fill in shadows under eyes and chin, broad soft frontal lighting, reduce contrast" |
| Backlit/silhouette | "even out exposure, reveal face details, balanced lighting, natural skin tones" |
| Warm yellow indoor light | "correct white balance to natural daylight, remove yellow cast, clean skin tones" |

### Fix 5 — Facial Feature Adjustments (Subtle, Not Surgical)

You can adjust individual features — jawline, nose, eyebrows — but the line between "better version of you" and "different person" is thin.

**Safe adjustments (maintain identity):**
- Subtle jawline definition: `slightly define jawline, subtle contour, natural shadow`
- Eyebrow symmetry: `natural even eyebrows, subtle grooming, match existing shape`
- Under-eye brightness: `reduce under-eye shadows, brighten eye area, natural look`

**Avoid:**
- "Change my nose" — this will produce a different nose, not a better version of yours
- "Make my face thinner" — this restructures bone geometry and breaks identity
- "Make me look like [celebrity]" — this triggers face-swap behavior, not retouching

The principle: retouching enhances what exists. It does not replace it. If you want a different face, use the avatar generator or face-swap features — those are different tools for different jobs.

### The Verification Step

Before you export, check two things:

1. **Toggle the before/after comparison** (Lovart includes a split-view toggle). Does the edited version look like the same person, just in better lighting with a better expression? If you cannot tell it is the same person, you have crossed the retouching line into identity alteration. Undo and adjust.

2. **Zoom to 100%.** Check for AI artifacts around the edited regions — especially hairline, ears, and the boundary between the face and background. These edge zones are where blending errors appear. Lovart's upscaling pass handles most artifacts, but verify.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can AI really fix a bad smile in a photo?**
Yes, and this is one of the areas where AI face editing genuinely outperforms traditional Photoshop. A Photoshop smile fix requires manually warping the mouth shape, which often produces unnatural results. AI can regenerate the lower face with a natural smile expression while preserving the surrounding facial features. The key is including the eyes in the regeneration region — a genuine smile involves both the mouth and the eyes.

**Q: Will the face still look like me after AI retouching?**
If you follow the "retouch, don't replace" principle — yes. Use prompts that say "preserve" and "natural" and "maintain." Avoid prompts that change structural features (nose shape, face width, bone structure). The before/after toggle is your honesty check. If you cannot recognize yourself, you have gone too far.

**Q: Is AI face retouching better than using apps like Facetune?**
Different tools for different philosophies. Facetune overlays adjustments as a filter layer — it smooths skin by blurring, whitens teeth by desaturating, and reshapes by warping pixels. Lovart's AI approach regenerates the selected area to match your prompt while preserving surrounding context. The AI approach produces more natural results for complex edits (smile changes, relighting) but requires more intentional prompting. Filter apps are faster for simple smoothing. AI is better for structural edits.

**Q: Can I fix a photo where someone is not looking at the camera?**
Yes. Use ChatCanvas to select the eye region and prompt: "eyes looking directly at camera, natural eye contact, catchlight reflection." For extreme gaze angles (profile view trying to look at camera), results may be imperfect because the surrounding face geometry does not support the new gaze direction. Test on a small selection first.

**Q: How many face edits can I do on the free plan?**
Lovart's Free tier includes a limited number of daily image edits. For unlimited edits, higher resolution exports, and advanced features like ChatCanvas region-select at full resolution, the $19/month plan is the standard entry point.

**Q: Can AI remove objects or people from the background of a portrait?**
Yes. Lovart's ChatCanvas lets you select background regions and regenerate them independently. Prompt: "clean uncluttered background, remove [object], natural depth of field, match existing lighting." The face remains untouched while the background is rebuilt. This is separate from face editing but often used in the same portrait retouching session.

**Q: Does AI face retouching work on group photos?**
Yes, but selectively. Select one face at a time with ChatCanvas. Apply your retouching prompt. Repeat for each face that needs adjustment. Attempting to retouch an entire group photo in one pass produces inconsistent results because the AI applies the same edit to every face. Individual editing is slower but produces dramatically better results.
