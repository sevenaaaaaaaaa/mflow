## This Week's Top 3 Reads

### 1. The Death of the "Generic Brand" — Why Visual Consistency Is Now Table Stakes
**Source:** *Harvard Business Review* — Design Column

A research team at HBR analyzed 2,000 consumer-facing brands across 15 industries and found that brand consistency — measured by visual coherence across touchpoints — now correlates more strongly with purchase intent than brand awareness. In plain English: customers care less about whether they've heard of you and more about whether you look like you have your act together. The article includes a practical five-point visual consistency audit that every brand team should run. *Read time: 8 minutes.*

### 2. Layout Composition Rules AI Designers Should Know
**Source:** *Designer Fund* — Blog

Designer Fund published an exceptional primer on composition rules that translate well to AI-assisted workflows. The piece covers the rule of thirds, golden ratio, visual hierarchy, and negative space — not as abstract principles, but as prompt-level instructions you can give to an AI design agent. Example: "Use the rule of thirds — place the focal point at the intersection of the upper-right grid lines, leave the lower-left quadrant as negative space." *Read time: 6 minutes.*

### 3. How 3 DTC Brands Cut Their Design Spend by 60% Without Sacrificing Quality
**Source:** *Modern Retail*

A data-rich case study following three direct-to-consumer brands — a skincare startup, a pet food company, and a home goods label — that transitioned from agency retainers to AI-augmented in-house design. The numbers are striking: average cost reduction of 62%, average speed increase of 5×, and — perhaps most interestingly — an increase in creative output volume of 3×. When design became cheaper and faster, they simply made more of it. *Read time: 10 minutes.*

## Design Tip of the Month: The 3-Version Rule

[IMAGE 3 PLACEHOLDER — Real UI Screenshot]

One of the highest-leverage habits we've observed among power users: **never ship version one.** When you generate a design you like, immediately ask the agent for two alternative treatments before settling:

- **Version 1:** Your original concept
- **Version 2:** "Same brief but 50% more bold — bigger typography, higher contrast, stronger focal point"
- **Version 3:** "Same brief but 50% more minimal — more white space, lighter typography, subtler colors"

Comparing the three extremes almost always reveals a better direction than the original. Sometimes you'll pick version 2 or 3 outright. More often, you'll remix the best elements from each into a version 4 that's stronger than anything you'd have arrived at directly. The cost of doing this with an AI agent? About 30 seconds. The improvement in output quality? Consistently noticeable.
