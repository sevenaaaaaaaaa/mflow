# A Step-by-Step Guide to Create Ad Creatives Without Photoshop — Facebook & Google Ads

Your campaign is set up. Audience targeting is dialed in. Budget is approved. Copy is written. And you are staring at Ads Manager's creative upload field, realizing you need five different image sizes and you have one product photo that was taken at a trade show booth in 2024.

Here is how to create professional ad creatives for Facebook, Instagram, and Google Ads without Photoshop, without a designer, and without defaulting to the same stock photo your competitor used last week.

## Step 1 — Understand the Ad Format Landscape

[IMAGE 2 PLACEHOLDER — Conceptual Diagram]

Before generating anything, know your canvas sizes. Each platform and placement demands different dimensions:

**Facebook & Instagram Feed**:
- Square: 1080 x 1080 px (most versatile — works in Feed, Marketplace, and right column)
- Vertical: 1080 x 1350 px (takes more screen real estate — often higher CTR)
- Horizontal: 1200 x 628 px (for link ads, article-style placements)

**Instagram Stories & Reels**:
- Vertical: 1080 x 1920 px (full-screen, immersive, text and key visuals must stay in the center 80%)

**Google Display Ads**:
- Medium Rectangle: 300 x 250 px
- Large Rectangle: 336 x 280 px
- Leaderboard: 728 x 90 px
- Wide Skyscraper: 160 x 600 px

**Google Discovery Ads**:
- Square: 1200 x 1200 px
- Vertical: 960 x 1200 px

You do not need to memorize these. You need to know that when you prompt the agent, you should specify the placement. "Facebook Feed square, 1080x1080" versus "Google Display Medium Rectangle, 300x250" produces fundamentally different compositions.

## Step 2 — Frame Your Creative Strategy

An ad creative is not a pretty image. It is a visual argument. Before prompting the agent, answer three questions:

1. **Who is seeing this?** Cold audience that has never heard of you? Retargeting audience that visited your site but did not buy? The visual language differs — awareness ads need brand introduction, retargeting ads need urgency or social proof.

2. **What is the one thing they should understand in under one second?** "This is a skincare product" is not enough. "This serum visibly reduces fine lines" or "This software saves you 10 hours a week" — specific, benefit-forward, scannable.

3. **What action should they take?** "Shop Now," "Sign Up Free," "Get the Guide" — the CTA drives the composition. A product-hero image with a "Shop Now" button has a different layout than a testimonial quote with "See Case Study."

Write your answers down. They become your creative brief and feed directly into your prompt.

## Step 3 — Generate the Hero Creative

Start with your primary format — typically a square feed ad — and get that right before generating variants. Your prompt should include format, subject, composition, copy elements, treatment, and brand constraints:

> "Facebook Feed ad, 1080x1080 square. Product: a sleek black water bottle on a gym bench, condensation droplets visible, morning gym lighting — bright but soft. Background: out-of-focus gym interior, natural light from large windows. The bottle should occupy roughly 40% of the frame, positioned slightly right of center. Headline text at the top: 'Stay Hydrated. Stay Focused.' in bold white sans-serif with a subtle dark overlay bar behind it for readability. Sub-text below the bottle: '32oz Insulated | 24hr Cold | Leakproof' in smaller white text. A 'Shop Now' button element bottom-center — rounded, in brand teal (#0A9E9E). Keep the design clean and modern. The product is the hero, not the text."

What makes this prompt effective:
- It names the platform and format first.
- It describes the scene — gym, morning light, condensation — not just the product.
- It specifies composition — "40% of frame," "slightly right of center" — so the layout is controlled.
- It includes every text element with positioning instructions.
- It includes the CTA button with color code.
- It states the visual hierarchy intention — "the product is the hero."

## Step 4 — Review Against Platform Rules

[IMAGE 3 PLACEHOLDER — Real UI Screenshot]

Before generating variants, check your hero creative against platform ad policies. Facebook's 20% text rule has been relaxed — text-heavy images can now run, though they may receive reduced delivery. Google Display Ads maintain stricter text limitations and content policies.

Key checks:
- No misleading claims or exaggerated results.
- No "before and after" images that imply unrealistic transformations (Facebook specifically flags these for weight loss, skincare, and financial ads).
- No excessive text overlay that triggers reduced reach.
- Brand-appropriate imagery — nothing that could be flagged as adult, violent, or sensational content.

The agent follows your instructions, not platform policies. You are the compliance layer.

## Step 5 — Generate Multi-Format Variants

From your approved hero creative, generate the remaining formats. The agent can adapt the composition for different aspect ratios while preserving the visual identity:

> "Same campaign creative, now adapted for Instagram Story, 1080x1920 vertical. The water bottle should be centered and slightly larger since there is more vertical space. Headline text at the top third. CTA at the bottom third. Keep the same gym lighting, color palette, and type treatment."

> "Same campaign, now adapted for Google Display Medium Rectangle, 300x250. Tighter crop on the product. Headline shortened to 'Stay Hydrated' since the canvas is smaller. CTA button more prominent relative to the smaller frame size."

Each format gets its own optimized composition while the brand identity — color, lighting, type treatment, product presentation — remains consistent across the campaign.

## Step 6 — Export and Organize for Ads Manager

Ad platforms require specific naming, sizing, and file conventions. Build a system:

**File naming**: `[Campaign]_[Platform]_[Format]_[Variant]_[Date]`
Example: `Summer_Launch_FB_Feed_1080x1080_Hero_A_2026-05`

**Organize by campaign**: Create a folder per campaign. Inside, sub-folders per platform (FB-IG, Google, LinkedIn). Inside those, the creatives.

**Check file sizes**: Facebook recommends files under 30MB. Google Display ads under 150KB for some formats. If your exported file is too large, compress it — quality can drop slightly for ad delivery without visible degradation.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I use AI-generated images for Facebook and Google ads?**

Yes. Both Meta and Google permit AI-generated ad creatives, provided the content complies with their advertising policies. There is no prohibition on AI as a creation method — the policies regulate what the ad shows and claims, not how the image was made.

**How many creative variants should I test per ad set?**

Facebook recommends 3-5 creative variants per ad set for effective testing. Dynamic Creative Optimization (DCO) can combine multiple images, headlines, and CTAs automatically. Generate 3-5 distinct visual approaches for the agent, and let the platform optimize delivery.

**What if my product does not photograph well on a phone?**

You do not need a perfect product photo. You need a clear, well-lit product photo. The agent handles composition, background, and lifestyle context. If your current product photos are genuinely terrible — dark, blurry, cluttered — invest in one afternoon of natural-light photography with your phone before generating ads.

**Can the agent include pricing in the ad creative?**

Yes. Specify the price and its placement in your prompt. Note that price-in-ad creative performs differently by industry — fashion and e-commerce can benefit; SaaS and services often do better with benefit-first creatives that reveal pricing on the landing page.

**How do I ensure my ad creatives look native — not like obvious ads?**

Prompt for natural, candid-feeling compositions rather than studio-staged shots. "Lifestyle photo, natural light, real home setting, the product in use — not posed, not studio" produces creative that blends into feeds better than "product on white background with sale badge."

**Can I generate video ad creatives?**

Current chat-based AI design agents specialize in static image generation. For video ads, shoot your own footage and use the agent to generate branded thumbnail frames, end cards, and text overlay templates that you composite in a video editor.

**What about Google Shopping ads — do the image requirements differ?**

Google Shopping requires product images on a pure white background (RGB 255,255,255) with the product filling at least 75% of the frame. Prompt the agent for "Amazon/Google Shopping compliant — pure white background, product-only, no props, no text overlay, no watermarks, the product filling 80% of the frame."
