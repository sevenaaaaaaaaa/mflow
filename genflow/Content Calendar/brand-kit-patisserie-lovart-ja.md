## The Scene: Jewelry That You Eat

A patisserie is not a bakery — it's a jewelry box of edible art. Your customers pay $12 for a single entremet because they are buying beauty first, flavor second. ## Recommended Color Palette

Patisserie branding lives in the world of haute couture — refined, feminine-leaning (but not exclusively), and unmistakably luxurious.

|---|---|---|

## Font Recommendations

- **Primary Logo:** Cormorant Garamond Light — ethereally thin serif evoking Parisian luxury
- **Headlines:** Bodoni Moda — high-contrast Didone for menu cards and window displays
- **Body Text:** Crimson Text — elegant, readable serif for descriptions and origin stories
- **Accent:** Petit Formal Script — delicate script for flavor tags and special occasion items
- **Digital:** DM Sans — clean sans-serif for web and social legibility

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

**Q1: Should my patisserie brand feel French even if I'm not in France?**
French pastry terminology and visual cues are globally recognized quality signals. You can reference the tradition without appropriation by focusing on technique (Ladurée-style macarons) rather than cultural cosplay (faux French accents in your logo). Elegance transcends language.

**Q2: What packaging communicates luxury on a budget?**
A simple white or kraft box with a single gold-foil logo sticker reads as premium for pennies. Add branded tissue paper inside — it's the unwrapping experience that creates perceived value. Lovart generates die-cut sticker templates in seconds.

**Q3: How do I photograph pastries to look expensive?**
Natural light from a single direction (window light is ideal). Shallow depth of field — focus on the front pastry, let the rest blur. Avoid phone flash at all costs. Use a marble or matte black surface. Lovart's brand kit includes a photography style guide with specific camera settings.

**Q4: Should my macarons match my brand colors?**
Yes — this is one of the most powerful branding tools available to a patisserie. Develop one or two macaron flavors in your exact brand colors. They become Instagram-bait and reinforce brand recognition with every post.

**Q5: How do I design for seasonal collections (Christmas, Valentine's, Mother's Day)?**
Create a seasonal template that layers over your core brand — same fonts and layout logic, but with season-specific accent colors and illustration motifs. Lovart's ChatCanvas lets you generate seasonal variants from your base brand kit in one prompt.

**Q6: What's the role of scent in patisserie branding?**
You can't brand a smell, but you can design around it. Use warm visual cues (steam wisps in photography, vanilla bean illustrations, browned butter tones) that trigger scent memory. Your visual brand should make customers smell butter and caramel through their screen.
