# Brand Kit for Vegan Restaurants: Menus, Delivery Thumbs & Storefront Continuity

Vegan restaurants lose brand trust in two boring ways: every image looks like stock spinach cosplay, or every dish becomes a different restaurant depending on who prompted last. A Brand Kit is how you stop both. This is not “make it greener.” It is operational identity for menus, delivery thumbnails, storefront boards, and social—built in Lovart so a second person can continue the look on a Saturday rush.

## Who this is for

Chef-owners, hospitality marketers, small agencies embedding in plant-based concepts, social managers tired of random “aesthetic.”

## What the kit must encode

- Logo lockups (horizontal/stacked) and clear space
- Color tokens that match the room—not a default eco palette
- Type roles: menu body, headlines, delivery title cards
- Photography/food styling rules: light, props, plate language
- Forbids: fake awards, melted garnishes, cliché leaf spam, competitor restaurant rhyme
- Channel crops: delivery app thumb, story, menu PDF, facade poster

## Anti-cliché rules (write them into the kit)

Forbid automatic kale showers, neon “VEGAN” stamps, and plastic glow vegetables unless that is truly your room. If the restaurant is spicy, late-night, or fine dining, the kit should smell like that—not like a generic wellness brochure.

## Capture before generate

Shoot hero dishes under service light when possible. AI cannot invent accurate spice oil sheen from a bad phone photo forever. Use honest refs; Identity Lock plates when the dish must stay itself across channels.

## ChatCanvas weekly rhythm

Monday: load Brand Kit; list week’s dishes and promos.  
Tuesday: delivery thumbs with four-take stop.  
Wednesday: menu or board specials.  
Thursday: social siblings.  
Friday: soft-proof rejects; archive autopsies.  
Weekend: junior/shift lead continues from board notes—not from DMs.

## Delivery thumbnail craft

Thumbs are cruel. Soft-proof tiny. Prioritize dish readability over tablescape ego. Keep type bands empty if the app overlays names. Match shadow recipes across the week so the shopper feels one kitchen.

## Menu systems

Print menus need sober type. AI key art can frame sections, but prices and allergens stay human-controlled. Empty bands are your friend. Do not cyberpunk the calorie line.

## Storefront and interior continuity

Posters and window vinyl should share kit tokens with delivery thumbs. Guests who discover you on an app should recognize the room. That recognition is the kit working.

## Flop diary

### Flop: the green paste
Every asset dunked in identical mint green. Cuisine was bold Ethiopian-inspired vegan. Guests expected juice bar. Fix: kit colors from textiles and ceramics actually in the room.

### Flop: dish identity melt
Three prompts, three burgers that were cousins. Fix: Identity Lock + plate refs + stop rule.

### Flop: award fantasy
AI invented “Michelin-ish” seals. Remove. Forever. Forbids.

## Staff handoff

Laminate a one-page kit card: colors, type roles, forbids, example thumbs. If only the founder’s prompting dialect works, you do not have a kit—you have a hobby.

## Ethics and claims

Do not imply certifications you lack. Do not invent celebrity dining. Food photography edits should not misrepresent allergens or portion reality in ways your jurisdiction treats as deceptive—ask counsel for your market.

## Kit module: Color from the room

Sample ceramics, walls, uniforms. Build tokens. Ignore stock “eco green” packs unless they match.

## Kit module: Props policy

One wood tone, one linen, limited ceramics. Prop chaos reads as catering from nowhere.

## Kit module: Chili and spice honesty

Red oil and char are brand if that is the food. Do not sanitize into pastel wellness.

## Kit module: Dessert discipline

Sugar gloss lies easily. Soft-proof; keep portions honest.

## Kit module: Drinks and mocktails

Glass caustics go fake fast. Capture helps; Touch Edit locally.

## Kit module: Chef portrait rules

Identity Lock if the chef is a face of the brand; no random cousin faces.

## Kit module: Seasonal overlays

Seasonal accents get end dates in the kit.

## Kit module: Delivery dark mode

Some apps crush shadows; test exports.

## Kit module: Multilingual menus

Empty type bands; real fonts for final PDF.

## Kit module: Partner pop-ups

Guest chef nights get labeled kits so the core brand does not dissolve.

## FAQ

### Can AI pick my brand fonts for me?
It can explore directions. You still decide licensing, legibility, and kit lock rules. Exploration is not a license.

### Should vegan restaurants use earthy greens by default?
Only if it matches the actual cuisine and room. Cliché palettes are a tell. Brand Kit should encode your truth, not a stock “plant” stereotype.

### What belongs in a freelancer Brand Kit vs a one-off moodboard?
Kit = reusable tokens and rules. Moodboard = temporary exploration. Clients pay for kits they can continue without you in the room.

### Do retro serif and cyberpunk clash on purpose?
They can, if hierarchy and kit rules say so. Random clash is not a trend—it is noise.

### Where do I start in Lovart?
Load or build Brand Kit, write forbids, plan with MCoT, generate in ChatCanvas, soft-proof, Touch Edit locally, lock winners.
