## Choosing Your Color Palette

[IMAGE 2 PLACEHOLDER — Conceptual Diagram]

Chiropractic branding needs structural integrity — colors that feel grounded, professional, and active. Avoid pure hospital whites and overused "spa" teals.

### Primary Colors
- **#1D3557 (Structural Navy)** — Confident, clinical, and universally trusted. Use as the primary logo color, website background, and clinic signage. It signals authority without coldness.
- **#457B9D (Active Blue)** — A mid-tone blue that bridges clinical seriousness and approachable energy. Ideal for adjustment-table linens, staff scrubs, and social-media templates.
- **#F1FAEE (Clean Linen)** — A warm, non-sterile white for walls, intake forms, and website backgrounds. Avoids the "hospital fluorescent" feel of #FFFFFF.

### Secondary/Accent Colors
- **#E63946 (Recovery Red)** — A bold accent for CTA buttons, "New Patient Special" promos, and appointment-confirmation highlights. Use at 10% or less of total brand surface area.
- **#2A9D8F (Healing Teal)** — For practices that offer complementary modalities (massage, rehab exercise). A calming secondary that pairs well with navy.
- **#F4A261 (Warm Amber)** — Human warmth in color form. Use for patient-success highlight sections, testimonial cards, and office decor accents.

## Typography Recommendations

### Logo/Display Font
**Montserrat** — Bold, geometric, and structurally confident. Its clean lines mirror the spinal alignment ideal that chiropractic patients seek. For family-oriented practices, **Poppins** offers a softer geometric alternative.

### Body/Text Font
**Source Sans 3** — Designed for on-screen readability, critical for practices that publish exercise videos and blog content. Pair with **Merriweather** for longform patient-education materials.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q1: Should my chiropractic brand look medical or lifestyle?
Both — the trust signals of medical (clean typography, clinical photography, structured layouts) with the warmth of lifestyle (real patient photos, warm accent colors, approachable language). Lovart's templates balance both automatically.

### Q2: What's the most cost-effective branded asset for a new chiropractic practice?
Google Business Profile images. Your GBP listing is where 64% of new chiropractic patients find you (Chiropractic Economics, 2025). Branded cover and post images from your Lovart kit convert searches into calls.

### Q3: How do I brand for both cash-pay and insurance patients?
Use a unified brand kit with two patient-journey tracks: insurance-focused materials lean into clinical trust signals, while cash-pay materials emphasize lifestyle benefits and membership-pricing visuals. Same palette, different emphasis.

### Q4: Can I use the same brand kit for multiple clinic locations?
Yes — Lovart's Brand Kit includes a master-locking feature. Your core palette, logo, and typography stay immutable across locations, while location-specific elements (address, team photos, local insurance logos) are template-editable.

### Q5: What typography issues should chiropractors avoid?
Avoid thin, light-weight fonts — they feel fragile, the opposite of what spine-health patients seek. Stick to medium and bold weights for headers and body text alike. Lovart's accessibility checker flags insufficient contrast automatically.

### Q6: How often should I update a chiropractic brand kit?
Refresh every 3–5 years, or when you add a new provider or modality. A mid-cycle "brand polish" (updated photography, refreshed social templates) keeps things current without a full redesign.
