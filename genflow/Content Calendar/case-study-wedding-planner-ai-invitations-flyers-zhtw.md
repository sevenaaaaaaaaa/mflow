## The Background

[IMAGE 2 PLACEHOLDER — Conceptual Diagram]

Olivia, 31, runs Atelier Rousseau — a boutique wedding planning and design firm in Lyon. Her clients are couples planning destination weddings in the French countryside — vineyards, châteaus, lavender farms. The aesthetic is "rustic elegance" — natural materials, soft palettes, handcrafted details that feel personal rather than manufactured.

Stationery is central to this aesthetic. It's the first thing guests see (save-the-dates), the formal announcement (invitations), and the thread that runs through the wedding day itself (menus, place cards, signage). Inconsistent stationery — or stationery that feels generic — undermines the entire design narrative Olivia builds for each wedding.

But custom stationery design carries a structural cost problem. Each wedding needs 8-12 unique pieces, all visually coordinated. A freelance stationer charges for the design work per piece or per suite. The cost scales linearly with the number of weddings — there's no economy of scale in custom design work.

## The Pain Points

### 1. The Customization Cost Trap

Couples want their stationery to feel unique — personalized colors, specific floral motifs, meaningful typography, details that reference their relationship. A freelance designer can deliver this, but each customization adds design time and cost. The tension between "unique enough to feel personal" and "affordable enough to be sustainable" meant Olivia was constantly negotiating with designers over scope — and sometimes paying out of her own margin to deliver what the couple wanted.

### 2. The Timeline Pressure

Wedding stationery operates on tight timelines with hard deadlines. Save-the-dates go out 8-10 months before the wedding. Invitations go out 8-12 weeks before. Menus and place cards are finalized 2-3 weeks before — after the final guest count and meal selections. A freelance designer working on 10 simultaneous projects can't always accommodate a 48-hour revision request three weeks before a wedding. Olivia had experienced stationery delays on 4 weddings in the previous season — delays that created cascading stress for couples and planners alike.

### 3. The Revision Economy

Stationery designs typically go through 2-4 revision rounds. Each round costs the designer additional unbillable time — or costs the planner additional revision fees. The revision process creates friction: couples feel guilty asking for changes, designers feel resentful making them, planners feel caught in the middle managing expectations. It's a system optimized for nobody's satisfaction.

## The Solution: How Olivia Uses Lovart

### The Suite Design System

Olivia's stationery workflow:

1. **Client brief collection (ongoing):** During the planning intake, Olivia collects the couple's color palette, thematic preferences (floral, geometric, rustic, modern), meaningful symbols or motifs, and typography preferences
2. **Suite generation (15-20 minutes per wedding):** She inputs the brief into Lovart and generates the complete stationery suite — all 8-12 pieces generated from the same prompt with the same aesthetic parameters, ensuring visual consistency
3. **Client review (variable):** She presents the AI-generated suite to the couple for feedback, makes Touch Edit adjustments based on their requests, and finalizes within 1-2 revision rounds
4. **Print coordination (ongoing):** She exports print-ready files and sends to her printing partner — the files are correctly sized, color-profiled, and bleed-marked for professional production

### Brand Kit as the Consistency Engine

Critical to the workflow: Olivia configures each couple's visual identity as a Brand Kit in Lovart. Color palette, typography, motif style, and composition preferences are locked in before any generation begins. This ensures that save-the-dates generated in January and menu cards generated in May — four months apart — share the same visual DNA.

"Without Brand Kit, I'd be describing the same colors and motifs from memory four months later, and they'd drift. With Brand Kit, the AI remembers exactly what 'Sage and Amber' looks like, down to the hex codes. I can generate a welcome sign in May that perfectly matches a save-the-date I generated in January."

### The Revision Revolution

The AI workflow transformed the revision process. Previously, a couple requesting "can we try a different floral motif?" meant a 48-hour wait and an awkward email to the designer. Now, Olivia generates 5 alternative motifs on the spot, shows the couple immediately, and gets a decision within the same consultation call. Revision cycles that took days now take minutes.

## FAQ

### Can AI design a complete wedding invitation suite with all the coordinating pieces?

Yes. Describe the full suite in your prompt — save-the-date, formal invitation, RSVP card, details card, menu, place card, program, welcome sign, seating chart, thank-you card — and Lovart generates coordinated designs for all pieces from the same aesthetic parameters. Use Brand Kit to lock in the color palette, typography, and visual motif, and every piece will be visually consistent regardless of when it's generated.

### How do I handle client revisions with AI-generated stationery?

Revisions are dramatically faster than with a human designer. Generate 3-5 concept variations initially. Present to the client, gather feedback, and refine by describing the requested changes in a new prompt: "Same design but with lavender instead of olive branches" or "Keep everything the same but switch to a lighter background." Each refinement takes 30-60 seconds to generate. Most clients are satisfied within 2-3 revision rounds.

### What print specifications do I need for wedding stationery?

For professional printing, export at 300 DPI in CMYK color mode with 3mm bleed on all edges. Lovart exports at print-ready specifications. Standard invitation size is 5x7 inches. RSVP cards are typically 3.5x5 inches. Menu cards are 5x7 or 4x9 inches. Place cards are 2x3.5 inches (folded). Verify final specifications with your printing partner before exporting.

### Can AI generate stationery in languages other than English?

Yes. Include the language in your prompt: "French wedding invitation text," "bilingual English-Spanish invitation," "Arabic calligraphy for wedding details." The AI handles non-English typography with reasonable accuracy; review character accuracy and cultural formatting conventions (name order, date formats, honorifics) before finalizing.

### Is AI-designed stationery as good as professional human-designed stationery?

For mid-range custom stationery (what most weddings use), AI-designed output is visually competitive with professional design. The AI excels at composition, color harmony, and typographic layout. For ultra-high-end bespoke stationery (letterpress with custom illustrations, hand-painted elements, specialized printing techniques), a professional designer with craft-production expertise still adds value. For the 95% of weddings that need beautiful, well-composed, personalized stationery, AI meets the quality threshold.

### How do I manage print-ready file formats for different stationery pieces?

Lovart exports in standard print formats (PDF, TIFF, PNG at 300 DPI). For most print jobs, export as PDF with embedded fonts and CMYK color profile. For specialty printing (letterpress, foil stamping, die-cutting), export the design and provide a separate file specifying the specialty-finish areas. Olivia coordinates with her printer to confirm format requirements before final export.

### Can I use AI stationery design commercially — reselling to wedding clients?

Yes. Stationery designed through Lovart is commercially licensed. Olivia sells AI-designed stationery as part of her wedding planning services — it's a standard commercial use case. Check Lovart's terms for any attribution or licensing requirements; as of 2026, commercial use is included with no attribution required.
