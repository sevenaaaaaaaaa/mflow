## Conservative, Professional, Authority-Building: The Consultant Palette

[IMAGE 2 PLACEHOLDER — Conceptual Diagram]

Consulting color palettes must project stability. Clients don't hire consultants who look trendy; they hire consultants who look reliable. Lovart's **Consultant Palette Engine**:

**Strategy/Management Consultant:** Deep navy (#1B2A4A), steel gray (#4A5568), white (#FAFAFA), burgundy accent (#722F37), and parchment (#F5F0E8). Navy and gray are the institutional colors of trust — the palette of McKinsey, BCG, and the professional services establishment. The burgundy accent adds distinction without sacrificing gravitas — it's the visual equivalent of a power tie.

**Boutique/Specialist Consultant:** Charcoal (#2D3748), warm taupe (#8B7D6B), ivory (#FDFBF7), teal accent (#2D7D7D), and rust (#A0522D). Warmer and more personal than the strategy palette, appropriate for solo practitioners and boutique firms where the consultant IS the brand. The taupe and rust add warmth that says "I'm approachable, not just authoritative." The teal provides a contemporary note without sacrificing professionalism.

**Technology/Innovation Consultant:** Slate (#3D4F5F), white (#FAFAFA), electric blue accent (#2563EB), warm gray (#9CA3AF), and amber (#D97706). Trust-blue dominant (appropriate for technology) with an amber accent that signals innovation energy. Cleaner and more modern than traditional consulting palettes, appropriate for consultants advising on digital transformation, AI strategy, and tech implementation.

**The Anti-Trend Principle** — Consulting palettes should age well. Lovart's palette engine avoids design trends (gradients, neon, pastel-dominant, ultra-minimal black/white) that will date the brand within 2–3 years. A consultant's brand should look as professional in 2030 as it does in 2026.

## Typography: Authority Through Clarity

Consulting typography must communicate expertise without calling attention to itself. Lovart's **Consultant Type System**:

**Name/Firm Font** — Serif for traditional consulting (Cormorant Garamond, Playfair Display — the fonts of academic authority and institutional trust). Sans-serif for modern/tech consulting (Inter Display, DM Sans — the fonts of clarity and efficiency). The logo font should feel "established" even if you're a solo consultant in your second year.

**Report Body Font** — The workhorse font for deliverables, proposals, and white papers. Source Serif 4 for traditional consulting (serif body text signals rigor — it's the font of academic journals and legal documents). Inter for modern consulting (sans-serif signals clarity and modernity). Always 11–12pt for print, 16–18pt for screen reading.

**Presentation Font** — For slide decks and proposal presentations. Clean sans-serif at 24–32pt for headlines, 18–24pt for body. Consulting decks are projected in boardrooms — text must be readable from the back of the room. Lovart's **Boardroom Legibility Test** simulates a 16:9 slide projected on a 10-foot screen viewed from 30 feet away.

**Data/Chart Font** — For the charts, graphs, and data visualizations that populate consulting deliverables. A condensed sans-serif (Inter Tight, DM Sans) that fits axis labels and data callouts without wrapping. Data visualization readability is non-negotiable — if a client can't read the chart, the insight is lost.

## Deliverable Templates: Where Consulting Value Becomes Visible

[IMAGE 3 PLACEHOLDER — Real UI Screenshot]

**Proposal Template** (10–18 pages) — The most important document in consulting. Lovart's proposal template includes: cover (client name + engagement title), executive summary, situation analysis, proposed approach (methodology + timeline), team bios, case studies/relevant experience, commercial terms, and next steps. Each section uses the brand kit with a clear visual hierarchy. A branded proposal tells the client "our work will be this organized and professional."

**Deliverable / Final Report Template** (20–60 pages) — For the final engagement output. Includes: executive summary, full analysis with data visualizations, recommendations, implementation roadmap, and appendices. Consistent formatting across 60 pages signals rigor — inconsistent formatting signals sloppiness, which clients unconsciously map to the quality of the thinking.

**One-Pager / Executive Summary Template** — The most-read consulting document format. A single page distilling a complex recommendation. Lovart's one-pager template uses: headline finding at top (largest text), 3 key supporting points (numbered, with data), and a recommendation box at bottom (highlighted in the accent color). Designed for the executive who reads nothing but one-pagers.

**Thought Leadership Templates** — LinkedIn carousel (10-slide PDF for LinkedIn document posts), white paper (8–12 page in-depth analysis), and webinar slide deck. Thought leadership is how consultants build authority between engagements. Lovart's templates make consistent publishing effortless.

## FAQ

**Q: Should my consulting brand reflect me personally or my firm?**
A: Solo consultants: personal brand, with your name as the firm name. The brand should reflect your personality (within professional bounds) — clients hire you, not a firm. Partnership/firm: firm brand with partner profiles within the system. The firm brand is the container; individual partners are featured within it. Lovart's brand kit adapts to both structures.

**Q: How conservative should my consulting brand be?**
A: It depends on your client industry. Strategy consulting for Fortune 500: very conservative. Creative consulting for startups: moderately expressive. Tech consulting: clean and modern. Lovart's **Industry Appropriateness Check** analyzes your target client industry and flags branding choices that would feel too casual or too stiff for that audience.

**Q: Can Lovart turn my consulting frameworks into branded visuals?**
A: Yes. Lovart's **Framework Designer** on the $49 Pro plan transforms your verbal frameworks (2×2 matrices, process flows, pyramids, Venn diagrams) into branded visual frameworks using your brand palette and typography. A branded framework is more memorable and screenshot-able than a text explanation.

**Q: How does Lovart handle boutique consulting firms with multiple practice areas?**
A: On the $99 Agency plan, create an **Umbrella Brand System**: firm master brand (logo, core palette, typography) with practice-area identifiers — Strategy Practice in navy, Operations Practice in teal, Technology Practice in blue. The firm is one brand with practice-area color coding for internal and client navigation.

**Q: What's the difference between consulting branding and agency branding?**
A: Consulting branding emphasizes authority, stability, and analytical rigor (navy, gray, serif fonts, data-rich deliverables). Agency branding emphasizes creativity, transformation, and visual impact (bolder colors, expressive typography, visually-driven deliverables). Your brand should match what clients hire you for.

**Q: Can Lovart's brand kit include templates for consulting-specific pricing models (value-based, retainer, project)?**
A: Yes. Lovart generates a **Commercial Terms Template** with: fee structure options (project-based, monthly retainer, value-based with success fee), payment schedule, expense policy, and terms and conditions placeholder. The template is a visual framework — actual commercial terms should be drafted with legal counsel.
