## 3 Specific Use Cases for Pizzeria Owners

### 1. Menu Board & Takeout Menu Design: Appetite Engineering

Your menu board is the most valuable piece of graphic design in your entire business. It determines what people order, how much they spend, and whether they come back. A well-designed pizzeria menu board uses: visual anchors (high-quality hero shots of your 3-4 best-selling pies), strategic item placement (your highest-margin specialty pies in the upper-left "golden triangle" where eyes land first), clear price presentation (no dollar signs, which studies show reduce spending by making the cost more salient), and appetite color psychology (warm reds, golden ambers, rich browns activate hunger; avoid greens and blues, which suppress it).

Lovart creates pizzeria menu boards engineered for appetite and revenue. "Design an indoor menu board for 'Fornace' — our Neapolitan pizzeria. 10 pies: 4 classic (Margherita, Marinara, Diavola, Quattro Formaggi), 6 rotating seasonal specials. Also: 4 appetizers (arancini, burrata, meatballs, marinated olives), 2 salads, 3 desserts (tiramisu, panna cotta, affogato), full beverage list. Design: rustic Italian with modern edge — wood textures, warm amber lighting feel, our brick oven as background motif. Price hierarchy — specialty pies featured in the visual 'golden zone.' Our logo prominent but not overpowering. Includes 'Ask about our gluten-free cauliflower crust' badge. Format: large format for wall display (48"x36") plus a downsized version for printed takeout menus (8.5x11 tri-fold)." The AI generates a menu that functions as both an ordering tool and a sales tool — every visual decision drives higher check averages.

For takeout menus specifically: "Create a takeout menu magnet for 'Fornace.' Same items as our main menu, but compact. Must include our phone number, website, and a QR code to our online ordering. Our logo and tagline. Design: as a magnet, it lives on people's fridges — it should look good enough that people WANT to keep it visible. Include a '10% off your first online order' promo code for new magnet recipients."

### 2. Slice Specials & Daily Deal Visuals

Slice culture is its own economy. The lunch slice rush (11:30 AM-1:30 PM) is volume-driven — speed and clarity matter more than ambiance. Your slice display case needs signage that communicates what's available and what it costs, instantly. Your "Slice + Soda $6" deal needs a window poster. Your "Happy Hour — $2 off all 12-inch pies 4-6 PM" needs sidewalk signage. These are daily or weekly visual assets that most pizzerias create with a Sharpie on cardboard — which works functionally but communicates "we don't care about details."

Lovart elevates slice culture visuals. "Create a slice counter sign set for 'Fornace': 1) Slice price board — 'Slices $4-$6' with today's varieties handwritten space below (we'll laminate and use dry-erase), 2) 'Slice + Fountain Drink $7' combo deal poster — 11x17, bold, appetite appeal, our branding, 3) Happy Hour sidewalk A-board — 'Happy Hour 4-6 PM: $2 Off All 12" Pies. Dine-In Only.' Two-sided, read from both directions. Design: consistent with our brand, appetizing, but faster and more casual vibe than our dinner menu — this is lunch, not date night. Same logo, same colors, more energy." Professional enough to reinforce your brand, practical enough for daily use with dry-erase markers.

### 3. Delivery Platform & Third-Party App Visuals

Third-party delivery now accounts for 25-35% of many pizzerias' revenue. Your DoorDash, Uber Eats, and Slice listings are digital storefronts where customers make split-second decisions based on photos. The platform algorithms also factor in photo quality when ranking search results — better photos get more impressions. Yet most pizzerias upload whatever cell phone photos they took in 2021 when they first signed up.

Lovart generates delivery-optimized visuals for every platform. "Create delivery platform image suite for 'Fornace': 1) Hero banner for DoorDash (1600x400) — our best 3 pies on a wooden peel, dramatic oven-fire backlighting, 'Wood-Fired Neapolitan Pizza — Delivered' text overlay, our logo, 2) Menu item photos for Uber Eats — each of our 10 pies photographed-style (AI-generated or enhanced from our photos), consistent lighting and angle, 3) 'Family Meal Deal' promotional banner — 2 large pies + large salad + 2-liter soda $42, family-style presentation, 4) Slice banner — 'NYC-Style Slice $5' for quick-lunch delivery orders. All images: bright, high-contrast (people browse delivery apps on phones in various lighting), appetizing, consistent with each other so our listing feels curated, not random." The AI understands delivery photography conventions — brighter than dine-in photography (phone screens have lower effective contrast outdoors), tight food framing (background doesn't sell, food sells), and steam/warmth cues (hot pizza is perceived as fresher pizza).

For Slice-specific optimization: "Our Slice listing needs a 'Why Order From Us' hero image — our brick oven, our Italian-imported '00' flour, our 72-hour cold-fermented dough. Tell our pizza story in one image."

**Q: Can Lovart handle the different design approaches for different pizza styles — Neapolitan vs. New York vs. Detroit vs. Sicilian vs. artisanal?**
A: Yes. Neapolitan design language is minimalist, rustic, "less is more" — natural textures, restrained typography, focus on ingredient quality. New York style is bold, energetic, streetscape-driven — high contrast, "slice culture" energy, no-fuss typography. Detroit style is hearty, industrial, Midwest pride — thick type, warm tones, manufacturing-heritage visual references. Sicilian is family, tradition, abundance — rich colors, generous imagery, "Sunday dinner" feel. Describe your pizza style, and Lovart adapts its design language accordingly.
