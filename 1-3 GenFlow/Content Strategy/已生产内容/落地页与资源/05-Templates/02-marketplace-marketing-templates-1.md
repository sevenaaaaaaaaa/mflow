## Google Ads Templates

Google Ads creative is functionally different from social media creative. The images appear in different contexts — search partner sites, Display Network placements, YouTube, Gmail, and Discovery feeds. Each context has different effective design strategies.

### Template G1: The Discovery Ad — Product Hero

**Format:** Google Discovery Ads (1.91:1 or 1:1)

**Best for:** E-commerce product promotion, DTC brands, retail.

A product-centric layout designed for Google's Discovery feed (which appears in the Google app, YouTube home feed, and Gmail Promotions tab). The product is the undeniable hero. Clean background, minimal text (headline appears as overlay on Google's side), and a subtle brand mark.

**Why it works:** Discovery ads appear in a content feed alongside editorial content. The design needs to feel native — not like an aggressive ad. Product-forward with lifestyle context performs best.

---

### Template G2: The Display Ad — Offer Focus

**Format:** Google Display Network (300x250, 336x280, 728x90, 300x600, 160x600, 320x50)

**Best for:** Retargeting, promotional campaigns, lead generation.

A bold, high-contrast design built for the Display Network's diverse placement sizes. The offer or value proposition is the most prominent element. The design scales across all Display Network sizes — the AI automatically generates correctly-sized variants for every supported dimension.

**Why it works:** Display ads appear in the periphery of web browsing. They have milliseconds to capture attention. Bold colors, clear typography, and a single focused message are non-negotiable.

---

### Template G3: The YouTube Thumbnail — Hook First

**Format:** YouTube video thumbnail (16:9) — used for YouTube ads and Discovery placements.

**Best for:** Video ad campaigns, brand storytelling, product demos, educational content.

The most competitive visual environment on the internet. This template is built on YouTube thumbnail best practices: high contrast, close-up faces or clear focal point, minimal text (3-5 words maximum), and a visual hook that creates curiosity. Includes a small "Ad" disclosure-safe zone.

**Why it works:** YouTube viewers are actively resistant to ads. The thumbnail has to overcome ad blindness in a feed of thumbnails competing for the same click. Emotion, curiosity, or clear value proposition — pick one and execute it perfectly.

---

### Template G4: The App Install Creative

**Format:** Google App Campaigns (1:1, 1.91:1, 9:16)

**Best for:** Mobile app promotion, game launches, utility app user acquisition.

A template suite (three aspect ratios) designed for Google App Campaigns. Shows the app interface or key benefit visually, with clear download CTA placement. The AI generates variants for all required dimensions and for both iOS and Android visual styles.

**Why it works:** App install ads need to communicate what the app does and why it is worth the download — all in a visual that might appear between gameplay or in a utility feed. Clarity over cleverness.

---

### Template G5: The Local Business Ad

**Format:** Google Local Campaigns, Google Business Profile posts (1:1, 1.91:1, 4:5)

**Best for:** Local businesses — restaurants, salons, retail stores, service providers.

A template designed for local conversion: prominent business photo or offer, clear location or service area indicator, "Directions," "Call," or "Book" CTA zone. The design maintains professionalism while feeling personal and approachable — appropriate for a business you might visit in person.

**Why it works:** Local ad viewers are often near a purchase decision. The design should make acting on that decision as easy as possible — clear location, clear action, clear value.

## Facebook & Instagram Ad Templates

Meta's ad platform rewards creative quality with higher relevance scores, lower CPMs, and better delivery. These templates are designed for the feed, Story, and Reels placements that drive the majority of Meta ad performance.

### Template F1: The Feed Ad — Benefit First

**Format:** Facebook/Instagram Feed (1:1 or 4:5)

**Best for:** Conversion campaigns, product sales, lead generation.

The workhorse Facebook ad template. A clear visual hierarchy: benefit statement or headline as the dominant element, supporting visual (product photo, lifestyle image, or illustration), and space for text overlay within Meta's recommended text-to-image ratio. The Story version adapts the layout for full-screen vertical.

**Why it works:** Users scroll past feed ads in under a second. The benefit needs to be readable in that window. The supporting visual provides context if they stop to engage. Strong CTA placement for those who read further.

---

### Template F2: The Carousel Ad — Story Arc

**Format:** Facebook/Instagram Carousel (1:1 cards)

**Best for:** Product collections, storytelling, step-by-step guides, before/after sequences.

A 3-5 card carousel template with a narrative arc. Card 1: hook or problem statement. Cards 2-4: solution, features, proof, or product showcase. Final card: strong CTA. Each card shares a consistent visual framework for a cohesive swipe experience, but each card's content is distinct.

**Why it works:** Carousel ads have 30-50% higher CTR than single-image ads. The narrative structure keeps users swiping. The final-card CTA captures intent built across the sequence.

---

### Template F3: The Story Ad — Full Immersion

**Format:** Instagram/Facebook Story (9:16)

**Best for:** Brand awareness, engagement, app installs, event promotion.

A full-screen Story template designed for the immersive, fast-paced Story environment. Motion-friendly layout (works as both static image and animated GIF/video template). Safe zones accounted for (UI elements top and bottom). Strong visual hook in the first 0.5 seconds — the time before someone taps to skip.

**Why it works:** Story ads are skippable. The viewer's thumb is hovering. The design has to earn every fraction of a second of attention. Bold, simple, and immediately interesting — every time.

---

### Template F4: The Social Proof Ad — UGC Style

**Format:** Facebook/Instagram Feed (1:1 or 4:5)

**Best for:** Brands with strong customer content, community-driven products, authenticity-positioned brands.

A template that frames user-generated content (customer photos, unboxing videos, review screenshots) in an ad format. The UGC is the hero; the brand elements (logo, CTA, offer) are subtle but present. The design makes the ad feel like a friend's recommendation, not a brand's promotion.

**Why it works:** UGC-style ads consistently outperform polished brand creative in authenticity-sensitive categories (beauty, fashion, wellness, food). People trust people more than brands. This template makes UGC look intentional rather than accidental.

---

### Template F5: The Lead Gen Ad — Value Exchange

**Format:** Facebook Lead Gen Ads (1:1 or 1.91:1)

**Best for:** Newsletter sign-ups, ebook downloads, webinar registrations, free trial sign-ups.

A template designed for the "give your email, get something valuable" exchange. The valuable asset (ebook cover, webinar screenshot, template preview, course module visual) is the hero. The value proposition is stated clearly: "Free download: [Asset name]." The CTA emphasizes the zero-cost exchange: "Get the Free Guide."

**Why it works:** Lead gen ads succeed or fail on how compelling the value exchange feels. The design needs to make the "something valuable" look genuinely valuable. A mockup of the actual ebook, guide, or resource converts better than abstract graphics.

## Ad Design Principles (Built Into Every Template)

These principles are baked into every template in this collection:

**Visual hierarchy for scan time.** Ad viewers scan, they do not read. Your value proposition must be the most visually prominent element. Everything else supports it.

**Platform-native feel.** Ads that look like ads get ignored. Ads that look like content get engaged. Templates are designed to feel native to their platform while remaining clearly branded.

**Text-to-image ratio discipline.** Meta's recommendation of under 20% text overlay exists for a reason — text-heavy ads perform worse. Templates respect this guideline without sacrificing message clarity.

**Mobile-first composition.** 85%+ of Facebook ad impressions and 70%+ of Google Display impressions are mobile. Every template is designed mobile-first, with desktop variants that adapt gracefully.

**Color psychology for conversion.** Warm CTA colors (orange, red, green buttons) outperform cool colors (blue, purple) in click-through. Templates use color strategically, and you can adjust via ChatCanvas: "Make the CTA button [color] for higher contrast."
