## Episode Overview

In 2024, James Park left his Creative Director role at a prestigious Madison Avenue agency to start DesignScale — a creative agency built entirely around AI-augmented workflows. Three years later, DesignScale has 50 employees, $4.2 million in annual recurring revenue, and a client list that includes three Fortune 500 companies. In this episode, James discusses the economics of AI-first agencies, how clients perceive AI-generated creative work, and why he believes traditional agencies that ignore AI will be obsolete by 2030.

## Full Transcript

### [00:00] Introduction

**Marcus Chen:** Welcome back to the Lovart Podcast. I'm Marcus Chen. In Episode 1, we talked about the future of AI design from the perspective of an experienced design director. Today, we're going deeper into the business side. My guest is James Park, founder and CEO of DesignScale, an agency that has grown from zero to over four million dollars in annual revenue in three years by being AI-first from day one. James, welcome.

**James Park:** Thanks for having me, Marcus. Excited to be here.

**Marcus:** Let's rewind. 2023. You're a Creative Director at a major traditional agency. What made you walk away?

**James:** I saw the writing on the wall. We had a client — a mid-size DTC brand — paying us $15,000 a month for creative services. They needed about 200 assets a month across social, email, and ads. We had three designers and a project manager on the account. Our margin was about 35%.

I did an experiment on my own time. I took the same creative brief and ran it through early AI tools — this was late 2023, so they were much rougher than what we have now. In a weekend, I produced 150 assets. They weren't all usable — maybe 60% were good enough with refinement. But I had just done 75% of a $15,000 monthly retainer's output in two days. Alone.

That math broke my brain. I realized the agency model I'd spent my career in was about to be disrupted, and I could either be disrupted or do the disrupting.

**Marcus:** So you chose disruption. What were those first six months like?

**James:** Brutal and exhilarating. I started DesignScale in January 2024 with $30,000 in savings, a laptop, and subscriptions to every AI tool I could find. My pitch to clients was: "Same quality creative, 40% faster, 30% cheaper." The "cheaper" part got meetings. The "faster" part closed deals.

Those first clients were small DTC brands who couldn't afford traditional agencies anyway. They didn't care how the work was made — they cared about results. And because we could iterate so much faster, our results were genuinely better. We'd test 20 ad variants in the time a traditional agency tested 3. More tests meant more wins. More wins meant more referrals.

By month six, we were at $45,000 MRR with three employees. By the end of 2024, $120,000 MRR with eight employees. The growth was entirely organic — client referrals drove everything.

### [14:45] The Economics of AI-First Agencies

**Marcus:** Let's get into the numbers. How do the unit economics of an AI-first agency compare to a traditional one?

**James:** Night and day. Here's a real comparison from our books:

**Traditional Agency Model (per $10K monthly retainer):**
- 3 designers at $70K/year each: ~$5,800/month allocated
- 1 PM at $85K/year: ~$2,400/month allocated
- Software/licenses: ~$600/month
- **Total cost:** ~$8,800/month
- **Margin:** 12%

**DesignScale Model (per $10K monthly retainer):**
- 1 senior designer/art director: ~$2,500/month allocated
- AI tools (Lovart, Midjourney, etc.): ~$400/month
- **Total cost:** ~$2,900/month
- **Margin:** 71%

Same $10,000 retainer. Same or better output quality. 71% margin versus 12%. That's not incremental improvement — that's a fundamentally different business.

**Marcus:** But you have 50 employees. That doesn't sound like a skeleton crew.

**James:** Right — because we didn't take the margin as profit. We reinvested it. Instead of 12% margin on $10K retainers, we kept margins at 40% and hired more senior creative talent. The traditional agency model forces you to hire junior designers and burn them out doing production work. We hire experienced creative directors and give them AI superpowers. Our people do the fun, strategic, high-value work. AI handles the production grind.

That's why our retention is so good — both client and employee. Clients stay because the work is better. Employees stay because they're not doing soul-crushing banner resizes all day.

### [20:30] Client Perceptions

**Marcus:** How do clients feel about AI-generated work? Has that changed over time?

**James:** Dramatically. In 2024, I had to hide it. I'd say things like "We use advanced automation tools to accelerate production." Couldn't say the A-word. One client found out we used AI for concept exploration and accused us of "not doing real creative work." We almost lost them.

Now? We lead with it. Our pitch explicitly says "AI-Augmented Creative." Clients ask about our AI workflow in the first meeting. The concern has completely inverted — they're worried if we're NOT using AI, because that means we're slower and more expensive than competitors who are.

**Marcus:** Do you charge differently than traditional agencies?

**James:** We charge on value, not time. Our pricing is based on the output and the business impact, not hours logged. If AI lets us deliver a rebrand in two weeks instead of eight, we don't discount — we deliver faster and move on to the next project. Speed is a premium feature, not a discount justification.

Some agencies are in a race to the bottom — "AI makes us cheaper so we'll charge less." That's a death spiral. We've gone the other direction: AI makes us better and faster, and clients pay for better and faster. Our average retainer has actually increased 40% since 2025.

### [26:00] The AI-First Creative Team

**Marcus:** What does your team structure look like? How is it different from a traditional agency?

**James:** We don't have junior designers. That's the biggest difference. In a traditional agency, you have a pyramid: a few creative directors at the top, a layer of senior designers, and a broad base of junior designers doing production. We've eliminated the base.

Our structure is more like:
- **Creative Strategists** (8 people) — Develop brand strategy, creative briefs, campaign concepts
- **AI Design Directors** (15 people) — Write prompts, curate AI outputs, refine designs, maintain brand quality
- **Creative Directors** (5 people) — Final creative sign-off, client relationships, creative vision
- **Motion & Video** (6 people) — AI-assisted video and animation (the one area AI still needs the most human help)
- **Client Services** (8 people) — Account management, client communication, project management
- **Operations & Tech** (8 people) — Build and maintain our AI workflows, tools, and automations

The key role is the "AI Design Director" — these are designers who've become expert prompt engineers. They have strong visual taste and strategic thinking, but their primary tool is Lovart's prompt interface, not a pen tool in Illustrator. They can generate and curate 200 design options in the time it takes a traditional designer to sketch 10.

### [31:40] The Tools Stack

**Marcus:** Walk us through your actual tools stack. What do you use and why?

**James:** We've settled on a core stack after experimenting with basically everything:

**Lovart** — Brand management and production design. This is our workhorse. We configure each client's Brand Kit once, then Lovart handles all the templated and brand-constrained design work. Batch generation for e-commerce clients is probably our highest-ROI feature. One designer, one CSV, 500 product images by lunchtime.

**Midjourney** — Pure inspiration and concept exploration. When we need wild, unexpected visual directions for a rebrand pitch, Midjourney is still unmatched for raw creativity. But it has zero brand consistency, so we use it upstream and feed the winning concepts into Lovart for production.

**Runway ML** — Video generation and editing. This is the newest addition to our stack and the most exciting. We're experimenting with AI-generated short-form video ads. The quality isn't flawless yet, but it's improving every quarter.

**ChatGPT Enterprise** — Copy and strategy support. Not a design tool per se, but our creative strategists use it heavily for audience research, positioning alternatives, and first-draft copy.

**Custom Lovart Skills** — We've built internal Lovart skills for our most repetitive workflows: "Client Onboarding Asset Pack," "Monthly Social Media Batch," "E-commerce Product Launch Suite." These save us roughly 40 hours per week across the team.

**Marcus:** What's your actual Lovart usage volume?

**James:** [Laughs] We're probably one of your largest customers. Last month we generated about 12,000 designs through Lovart. Our team does in a day what would take a traditional agency of 100 people a week.

### [36:15] Common Mistakes

**Marcus:** What mistakes do you see agencies making with AI?

**James:** Four big ones.

**One:** Treating AI like a junior designer. "Here's a vague brief, go make something." AI is a tool, not a colleague. It needs clear direction, specific constraints, and human judgment to produce work worth paying for.

**Two:** Not building brand systems first. Agencies jump straight to generating flashy one-off images without properly configuring brand parameters. Then every design looks like it's for a different company. Spend the time upfront building the Brand Kit. It pays back 100x.

**Three:** Hiding AI usage from clients. We tried this in 2024. It creates trust issues, and trust is everything in client relationships. Be transparent. Most clients respect the honesty, and the ones who don't probably aren't your ideal long-term partners anyway.

**Four:** Under-pricing. Just because AI reduces your costs doesn't mean you should reduce your prices. Price based on the value you deliver to the client. If AI lets you deliver more value faster, that should increase your effective rate, not decrease it.

### [40:10] Predictions for 2030

**Marcus:** Last question. Agency model in 2030 — what does it look like?

**James:** I think the traditional agency model — billable hours, large teams, slow processes — will be largely extinct by 2030. What replaces it? Three models, I think:

**The AI-Native Agency:** Like DesignScale. Small teams of highly senior people augmented by AI. High margins, fast turnaround, premium pricing. This is the model we're betting on.

**The Brand Strategy Firm:** Pure strategy consulting. These firms won't do production at all — they'll define the brand strategy and hand it to AI tools (or AI-native agencies) for execution. The value is entirely in thinking, not making.

**The In-House AI Team:** Large brands will bring creative in-house because AI makes it cost-effective. Why pay an agency $20K/month when you can hire one AI Design Director at $120K/year, give them a Lovart Enterprise subscription, and get equivalent output?

The losers will be the middle-ground agencies — the ones that do decent work but aren't truly strategic and aren't truly AI-native. They'll get squeezed from both ends.

**Marcus:** James Park, founder of DesignScale. Fascinating conversation. Thank you.

**James:** Thanks, Marcus. Appreciate the conversation.

**Marcus:** That's Episode 2 of the Lovart Podcast. Next episode, we're talking with an e-commerce founder who used AI design to scale from zero to $10 million in sales. Subscribe so you don't miss it. See you in two weeks.
