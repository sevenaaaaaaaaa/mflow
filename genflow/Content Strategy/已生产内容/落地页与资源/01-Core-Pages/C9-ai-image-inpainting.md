## What Is AI Image Inpainting?

Inpainting is the technique of reconstructing missing or removed portions of an image. The term originates from art restoration, where conservators would "inpaint" damaged areas of paintings to restore them to their original appearance.

In the AI context, inpainting means: you mark a region of an image for removal, and the AI fills that region with plausible content that matches the surrounding context — color, texture, lighting, perspective, and semantic content (what *should* be there).

AI inpainting goes beyond traditional content-aware fill by understanding *what the scene is*. Remove a person from a beach photo, and the AI doesn't just clone nearby sand — it reconstructs the beach, the waves, the shadows, as a coherent scene. Remove a car from a street, and it fills in the road, sidewalk, and buildings behind it.

| **Clone Stamp** | Slow | Depends on skill | High | Repetitive textures fail |
| **Healing Brush** | Moderate | Good | Medium | Struggles with complex edges |
| **Content-Aware Fill** | Fast | Moderate | Low | Leaves smudgy artifacts |

Content-aware fill was revolutionary in 2010. It's primitive by 2026 standards. AI inpainting doesn't just blend nearby pixels — it *understands* and *reconstructs* the scene. The difference is most visible on complex backgrounds: removing a person from a patterned tile floor, or a car from a street with lane markings. Content-aware fill smudges. AI inpainting reconstructs.
