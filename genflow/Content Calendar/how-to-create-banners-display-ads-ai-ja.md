## The Multi-Size Problem (and How AI Solves It)

A traditional **web banner generator** workflow means designing each size individually — or using Canva's Magic Resize and praying the layout survives. Neither scales.

A horizontal banner becomes a vertical story ad. A square feed post becomes a skyscraper. The message stays coherent; the layout adapts.

## Writing Prompts That Convert

A display ad has one job: get the click. Your prompt needs to encode everything that makes that happen:

**The Offer Must Lead.** Start every prompt with the value proposition: *"Display ad for 30% off annual plans — headline 'Save 30% Today' prominent — CTA button 'Claim Offer' in brand orange — hero image of product dashboard — clean white background, minimal text."*

**Size-Aware Prompting.** When requesting multiple sizes, guide the layout: *"For horizontal (728×90) — stacked layout with logo left, headline center, CTA right. For vertical (300×600) — logo top, hero image center, headline + CTA bottom. Maintain 60px safe zone on all edges."*

**Platform-Specific Constraints.** Google Ads has strict rules about text-on-image ratios and misleading CTAs. Meta penalizes ads with more than 20% text overlay. Write these constraints into your prompt and Lovart respects them: *"Facebook compliant — less than 20% text overlay, no fake urgency, no before/after claims."*

## Display Ad Design Principles (Built Into the AI)

Even with an **ai banner generator**, these principles separate winning ads from wasted spend:

- **Visual hierarchy:** The eye should land on the hero image first, then the headline, then the CTA. Lovart's layout engine enforces this by default.
- **Contrast for scannability:** Feeds are fast-scrolling environments. Your ad has 0.3 seconds to register. High contrast between background and text isn't optional — it's survival.
- **Motion consideration:** If you're running animated banners (GIF/HTML5), your prompt should specify: *"animated banner — frame 1: hook question, frame 2: product reveal, frame 3: CTA pulse."*
- **Brand consistency at scale:** When you're running 40+ banner variants across 4 platforms, brand drift is inevitable — unless your brand lives in a system that applies it automatically.

## The Zero-AI Trope: The Banner Someone Painted by Hand

Go to any local farmers' market and you'll see it: a wooden sign painted by the vendor's kid, spelling "STRAWBERRIES" with the S backward. People stop. They smile. They buy. That sign converts better than any programmatic banner ever will — not because it's well-designed, but because it's true. It telegraphs something no display ad can fake: a real person grew these, and they care enough to show up every Saturday. The best AI display ads steal this energy — not the crooked letters, but the honesty. Before you prompt, ask: *What's true about this offer?* Start there.

**Q: How many banner sizes can I generate at once with Lovart?**
There's no hard limit. You can batch-generate 10+ size variations from a single master design, with each variant intelligently adapted to its target dimensions.

**Q: Does Lovart support animated HTML5 banners?**
Lovart is primarily a static design agent. For animation, pair Lovart's exported PNG frames with a GIF assembler or HTML5 animation tool. Lovart provides the frame-perfect design assets.

**Q: Can I use AI banners for Google Ads?**
Yes. Lovart generates web-optimized images that meet Google Ads specifications. Just ensure your prompt includes any platform-specific constraints (text ratio, safe zones, file size limits).

**Q: How do I keep my brand consistent across 50+ banner variants?**
Use Lovart's Brand Kit feature. Upload your logo, set brand colors and fonts once. Every generation — regardless of size — automatically applies your brand guidelines.

**Q: What's the best format for display ad exports?**
For static banners, PNG (crisp text and logos). For photographic ads, JPG at 85%+ quality (smaller file size). For animated, export frame sequences as PNG, then assemble externally.

**Q: Can I A/B test different designs from the same prompt?**
Yes. Every generation in Lovart produces a unique output. Run the same prompt 3–4 times to get natural variants for split testing — or add deliberate variations like *"version B: dark background, white text, urgent tone."*
