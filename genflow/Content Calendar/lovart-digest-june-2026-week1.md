## Summer 2026: 3 Design Trends Worth Watching

[IMAGE 2 PLACEHOLDER — Conceptual Diagram]

### 1. Soft Brutalism — The Anti-Minimalist Summer

The clean, beige-on-beige aesthetic that dominated 2024-2025 is giving ground to something rougher. "Soft Brutalism" combines raw, unpolished visual elements (asymmetric layouts, visible grid lines, rough typography) with warm, approachable color palettes. Think: a concrete-textured background with a butter-yellow headline in a friendly sans-serif.

Designers describe it as "brutalism that doesn't want to scare your mom." It's showing up everywhere from indie brand packaging to Spotify playlist covers. The key to pulling it off: the contrast between *structural rawness* (the layout) and *emotional warmth* (the color and typography).

**Try it:** Prompt for "soft brutalist layout with warm terracotta and cream palette, visible grid structure, single bold headline" — and see what emerges.

### 2. Liquid Gradients and Glass Morphism Are Back (But Softer)

Apple's 2026 WWDC visual language leaned heavily into translucent, glass-like UI elements with fluid gradient transitions. The design world followed. But unlike the harsh glass morphism of 2020-2021, the 2026 version is softer — lower opacity, gentler background blur, gradients that melt into each other rather than creating sharp color boundaries.

This works especially well for brand assets that need to feel premium and contemporary without looking dated in six months. The trick: keep the gradient transitions subtle and pair glass elements with solid, grounded typography.

**Try it:** "Soft glass morphism card design, translucent overlays at 15% opacity, gentle lavender-to-sky gradient background, modern sans-serif text anchored on solid white."

### 3. The Return of Illustrated Typography

After years of clean, geometric sans-serif dominance, we're seeing a resurgence of illustrated, hand-drawn, and custom lettering integrated into brand identities. Not as a replacement for primary logotypes, but as a secondary element — illustrated type treatments for campaign headlines, social media covers, and hero sections.

This is one area where AI shines: generating custom illustrated lettering that would take a human illustrator days can be done in minutes. The results feel personal and craft-forward — the opposite of "AI-looking."

**Try it:** "Custom illustrated headline reading 'Summer Sale' — hand-drawn lettering style, botanical elements woven through the letters, warm peach and sage green, transparent background."
