# Real Estate Marketing Tools Compared: Canva vs AgentPrint vs Lovart

[IMAGE 1 PLACEHOLDER — Persona Scenario]

**Real estate agents spend an average of $6,000-15,000 per year on marketing. Most of it goes to listing photos and flyers that look identical to every other agent's listing photos and flyers. The industry's creativity problem has a tool-shaped cause.**

Real estate marketing is governed by specific requirements: Fair Housing Act compliance, MLS photo standards, equal opportunity logos, broker disclaimers, and state-specific license number placement. Generic design tools do not know these requirements exist. Real estate-specific tools know them but produce output that screams "template."

We tested Canva (the generic option), AgentPrint (the real estate specialist), and Lovart (the design agent that can be taught compliance rules) by listing the same 3-bedroom, 2-bath property on each platform and producing a complete marketing package.

## Myth #1: "Real Estate Marketing Is Just Pretty Pictures"

Real estate marketing sits at the intersection of design, compliance, and data. A listing flyer that is beautiful but missing the Fair Housing logo is a legal liability. A social media post that is engaging but fails to disclose agent license number violates state regulations.

Canva treats real estate marketing as graphic design. It offers 1,200+ real estate templates that look appealing. It does not include the Fair Housing logo automatically. It does not prompt for license number placement. It does not verify that your "recently sold" claim is compliant with local advertising regulations. The tool assumes you know the rules — and if you do not, it will not warn you.

AgentPrint bakes compliance into its templates. Fair Housing logos appear automatically. Broker disclaimers are template fields rather than afterthoughts. State-specific requirements are built in. The compliance is strong. The design quality is adequate — professional but generic, competent but indistinguishable from other AgentPrint users.

Lovart offers the best of both approaches: you configure compliance rules once (include Fair Housing logo, place license number bottom right, include broker disclaimer on all print materials) and the design agent respects those rules while generating original designs from property-specific prompts.

**The Verdict:** Compliance without design quality produces boring marketing. Design without compliance produces illegal marketing. The third option — compliance-aware design generation — is what the industry actually needs.

## Myth #2: "Virtual Staging Is a Luxury Service"

Virtual staging — furnishing an empty room digitally — costs $50-200 per photo when done by professional services. It is the single highest-ROI marketing expense for vacant properties: staged homes sell 73% faster and for up to 10% more than unstaged homes, according to NAR data.

Canva does not offer virtual staging. AgentPrint does not offer virtual staging. Both expect you to source staged photos from a separate service.

Lovart includes virtual staging as a native feature. Upload a photo of an empty room, describe the staging style ("contemporary, warm neutrals, family-friendly, emphasize the fireplace and natural light"), and receive a furnished version. The cost is included in the Lovart subscription rather than as a per-photo expense.

For an agent listing 2 vacant properties per month at $150/photo × 8 photos per property, virtual staging costs $2,400/month through traditional services — more than the annual cost of any design tool subscription.

## FAQ

**Q: Do I need a separate virtual staging service if I use Lovart?**
No. Virtual staging is included in Lovart's design agent. Upload empty room photos and describe the staging style. Results are comparable to professional staging services for digital marketing use.

**Q: Is Canva sufficient for real estate marketing?**
For agents who know exactly what compliance elements to add manually: yes. For new agents or those in highly regulated states: the compliance risk of missed elements is significant.

**Q: What makes AgentPrint different from Canva for real estate?**
AgentPrint templates are built for real estate compliance — Fair Housing logos, disclaimers, and license fields are included by default. Design quality is functional rather than distinctive.

**Q: Can Lovart generate floor plans from property photos?**
Lovart can generate floor plans from room descriptions and approximate measurements. For MLS-listed properties, it is best used with actual measurements. The floor plan feature is a marketing visualization, not a survey document.

**Q: Which platform handles luxury property marketing best?**
Lovart — the prompt-based approach allows specification of luxury aesthetics (minimalist, editorial photography style, premium paper textures, restrained typography) that generic templates cannot achieve.

**Q: What about video content for property listings?**
Lovart generates property video scripts, social video concepts, and animated listing presentations. Canva has basic video editing. AgentPrint does not offer video features.
