## Why Price List Design Directly Affects Revenue

[IMAGE 2 PLACEHOLDER — Conceptual Diagram]

Customers do not read price lists neutrally. They scan for anchors — the highest price sets the ceiling, the lowest sets the floor. A poorly designed price list buries your premium offerings and makes every price look like a random number. A well-designed price list uses visual hierarchy to frame your services in tiers, making the middle tier look like the obvious best value and the premium tier look aspirational.

This is not psychological manipulation. It is information design. The same way a well-organized restaurant menu leads customers toward the dishes with the best margins, a well-designed price list leads customers toward the services that deliver the best value — for them and for your business.

## The Chat-Generate Price List Workflow

### Step 1 — Define the Price List Format

Price lists come in formats optimized for different display contexts:

- **Wall display**: 11x17 or A3, large format, readable from 5 feet away. Common in salons, barber shops, spas.
- **Counter card**: 5x7 or A5, standing on a counter or in a holder. For quick reference at point of sale.
- **Brochure insert**: Trifold panel, part of a larger marketing piece. Common for full-service menus.
- **Digital display**: Screen-optimized, 1920x1080 or square 1080x1080. For websites, tablets, social media.
- **Takeaway card**: Business card size, 3.5x2 inches. For customers to take home.

Start your prompt with the format and display context:

```
Salon price list — wall display, 11x17 inches, portrait orientation. 
Designed to be readable from 5 feet away. Framed behind the reception desk.
```

```
cream (#FFF8F0), gold accent (#C8A84E). Font pairing: Cormorant Garamond (headings) +

LAYOUT:
- Top: Salon logo centered, with "Service Menu" in elegant serif below, gold.
- Below: Three columns of equal width, each representing a service category.

with a thin gold rule line below. Services listed vertically, each on one line: 
service name (Montserrat Light, 14pt, charcoal) on the left of each line, 
price (Montserrat SemiBold, 16pt, charcoal) on the right. 
A subtle dotted leader line connecting name and price. 0.3-inch spacing between items.

COLUMN 2 (center): "Color Services" — same heading and layout treatment. 
Premium service (Balayage, Color Correction) get a subtle gold star icon next to the price.

COLUMN 3 (right): "Treatments" — same heading and layout treatment.

BOTTOM SECTION: A subtle footer — "Prices subject to change. Gratuity not included." 
— 9pt, Montserrat Light, 50% opacity charcoal, centered. 
Small salon logo mark in the bottom center.

BACKGROUND: Soft cream (#FFF8F0) with a subtle watermark pattern (thin geometric lines 
in warm rose at 5% opacity). Clean, elegant, editorial beauty industry aesthetic.

TYPOGRAPHY RULES:
- Service names: left-aligned, 14pt, Montserrat Light.
- Prices: right-aligned, 16pt, Montserrat SemiBold.
- Category headings: 24pt, Cormorant Garamond Bold, with gold rule line (1pt, 80% width of column).
- Minimum text size: 12pt — ensures wall-display readability.
```

Key price list design principles embedded in this prompt:
- **Clear category separation** (three columns) — prevents the wall-of-text problem.
- **Dotted leader lines** between service and price — guides the eye across the gap, preventing price mismatches.
- **Premium indicators** (gold stars) — draw attention to high-value services without being pushy.
- **Readable minimum sizes in context** — 12pt minimum for wall display, where the customer is 3-5 feet away.
- **Footer with disclaimers** — legally prudent and industry-standard.

## Price List Design Psychology

[IMAGE 3 PLACEHOLDER — Real UI Screenshot]

| Principle | Application |
|-----------|-------------|
| Price anchoring | Place your highest-price service near the top of a category — it makes everything below it look reasonable by comparison |
| Decoy pricing | If you want to sell more of Service B at $95, list Service A at $65 and Service C at $150. B becomes the "best value" middle option |
| Remove currency symbols | "$65" triggers price sensitivity. "65" is more neutral. In luxury contexts, dropping the dollar sign entirely can increase perceived value |
| Tier naming | Not "Cheap / Medium / Expensive." Try "Essential / Signature / Premium" or "Classic / Deluxe / Master." Names frame the purchase decision |
| White space around prices | Prices crowded by text feel expensive and stressful. Prices surrounded by breathing room feel fair and considered |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I handle services with variable pricing ("starting at" or "from $X")?**

List the base price with a note. In your prompt: "Balayage — from $180 (price varies by length)." The agent will format the price range clearly without cluttering the layout. Use a smaller italic note for the variable clause.

**Can I generate a price list with images of each service?**

Yes. Prompt: "Each category column includes a small 1x1-inch circular image next to the category heading — a photo representing that service type (haircut, color, treatment)." The agent will comp images into the layout. For real salon photos, plan to swap in actual photos during post-processing.

**How do I make my price list look premium rather than discount-oriented?**

Three techniques: (1) Remove currency symbols. (2) Use elegant serif fonts. (3) Add generous white space between categories and items — crowded layouts signal "budget," airy layouts signal "premium." (4) Use a restrained color palette — 2-3 colors max, no bright red sale tags.

**Can I A/B test my price list layout?**

While you cannot A/B test a printed wall display the way you test a web page, you can generate two layout options, print one copy of each, and show both to staff or a few trusted clients for feedback. The agent makes this exploration cost-free.

**What if my price list needs to comply with industry regulations?**

Specify regulatory requirements in your prompt: "Include license number #MM12345 in the footer, 8pt. Include 'Prices inclusive of GST' notation. No 'Best Price' or 'Lowest Price' claims — regulatory compliance for [industry]." The agent will incorporate compliance elements into the design.

**Can I generate multilingual price lists?**

Yes. Prompt: "Generate this same price list with content in French. Same design, same layout, French copy. Adjust typography for French accent characters. Include both languages? Create a dual-column layout with English on the left and French on the right."
