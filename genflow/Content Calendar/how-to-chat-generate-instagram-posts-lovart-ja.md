## The Instagram Post Generation Workflow

### Step 1 — Decide What Kind of Post You Need

Instagram posts fall into predictable categories. Identifying which one you are making focuses your prompt and produces better output:

- **Single-image promotional post**: A product, an offer, a launch announcement.
- **Carousel slide set**: Multiple images or text slides for educational, storytelling, or showcase content.
- **Quote or typography post**: A statement, a testimonial, a data point — visually designed.
- **Lifestyle or brand image**: A scene or mood shot that builds brand identity without selling directly.
- **Before-and-after or comparison**: Side-by-side visuals showing transformation or product benefit.

Start your prompt by naming the format. "I need a square Instagram post for..." primes the agent on aspect ratio and canvas constraints before it starts composing.

### Step 2 — Write a Scene Prompt, Not a Design Brief

The most common mistake first-time users make is writing a design brief instead of a scene description. Compare these two prompts:

*Weak prompt*: "Make an Instagram post for my skincare brand. It should look nice and clean."

*Strong prompt*: "Square Instagram post. A frosted glass jar of moisturizer on a wet marble countertop, morning light from a window casting soft shadows, a sprig of rosemary and a linen cloth in the background. Clean, editorial beauty photography style. Cream and sage green color palette. Space at the top for a short headline."

The weak prompt leaves everything to the agent's defaults. The strong prompt specifies format, subject, setting, lighting, props, style reference, and practical layout requirements. The agent has more information to work with, and the output will be correspondingly more controlled.

### Step 3 — Request Your First Iteration

Send the prompt. Review the output. Do not expect perfection on the first attempt — the same way you would not expect a human designer to nail a project brief on the first comp. Approaching the agent with an iteration mindset, not a one-shot expectation, is the single biggest predictor of output quality.

### Step 4 — Refine with Specific, Actionable Feedback

When you request changes, be as concrete as you would with a junior designer sitting next to you:

- "Make the product 20% larger in the frame"
- "Shift the lighting — more dramatic, deeper shadows, less flat"
- "Replace the background with a darker wood texture"
- "Add more negative space on the left side for text overlay"
- "The color feels too warm — cool it down, more blue undertones"

Avoid vague feedback like "make it pop" or "something is off." The agent responds to concrete visual instructions, not emotional reactions.

### Step 5 — Export in the Right Format

Instagram posts should be exported at 1080x1080 pixels (square), 1080x1350 (portrait), or 1080x566 (landscape). JPEG at quality 85-100 is standard. The agent handles format and resolution; you just specify your target.

For carousel posts, generate all slides in sequence using a consistent visual template — same background treatment, same font style, same color accents. The agent can generate the first slide, and you can prompt subsequent slides with "same style, slide 2 of 5, content changed to..."

## Optimizing Your Prompt Over Time

Your first ten prompts will be exploratory. Your next fifty will be refined. Here is what improves with practice:

- **Style vocabulary**: You will learn which descriptors produce which aesthetic outcomes. "Editorial" + "warm tones" gives you one thing. "Flat lay" + "bright white" gives you another. "Moody" + "backlit" gives you a third.
- **Composition shorthand**: Instead of "put the product in the middle with space around it," you will learn to say "centered hero, generous negative space."
- **Iteration efficiency**: You will learn to batch feedback — giving three specific changes in one message rather than stretching the same adjustments across three rounds.
- **Brand consistency**: You will save your color codes, font preferences, and composition formulas in the agent's brand kit so every post pulls from the same visual rulebook without you needing to restate it.

## Avoiding "Generic AI" Output

The internet is filling with AI-generated content that all shares a family resemblance — over-saturated colors, unnaturally perfect compositions, a certain plastic smoothness to textures. Your posts need to stand apart from that slurry.

Ways to differentiate:

- **Use specific, grounded references**: "Like a Kinfolk magazine spread" or "1970s Polaroid aesthetic" or "Wes Anderson symmetry" — cultural references that the agent can interpret compositionally.
- **Introduce imperfection**: "Include some film grain" or "natural, unposed, iPhone-candid feel" or "slightly underexposed." Counterintuitively, asking for imperfection often produces more authentic-looking results than asking for perfection.
- **Iterate toward authenticity**: If the first output looks too sterile, ask for "more texture, more shadow depth, less studio — more real life."
- **Combine AI imagery with your own photos**: The most effective Instagram accounts blend AI-generated lifestyle scenes with real behind-the-scenes phone shots. The mix of production values signals authenticity better than either extreme alone.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many iterations does a typical post take?**

Most users land on a usable post in 2-4 iterations. The first draft rarely survives unchanged, but the second or third revision is typically the keeper.

**Can I generate carousel posts with consistent styling across slides?**

Yes. Generate the first slide to establish the visual template, then prompt each subsequent slide with "same style, new content." The agent references the previous slide for visual consistency.

**What if I need to add my logo to every post?**

Set your logo as a brand kit element. The agent will place it consistently — same size, same position, same margins — on every generated post without you needing to specify it each time.

**Can the agent generate Instagram Story graphics?**

Yes. Specify "vertical 9:16 Instagram Story" at the start of your prompt. The agent adjusts the canvas and composition for the taller format. Story graphics typically benefit from more centralized composition since they fill the entire phone screen.

**How do I prevent my feed from looking repetitive?**

Vary your post categories and scene descriptors. If you use "morning light on a marble surface" for every post, your feed will feel monotonous. Rotate through 3-5 distinct visual themes — product close-ups, lifestyle scenes, typography posts, behind-the-scenes candids.

**Does the agent work in languages other than English?**

Prompts can be written in most major languages. The generated image content is language-agnostic. For text overlay in non-Latin scripts, test the type rendering carefully — font glyph support varies.

**Can I plan an entire week of posts in one session?**

Yes. Many users batch-generate 5-7 posts in a single sitting, saving them to schedule throughout the week. This "content day" approach is more efficient than generating one post daily and produces a more cohesive weekly grid.
