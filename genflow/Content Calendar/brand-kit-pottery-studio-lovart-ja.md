# How to Create a Brand Kit for Your Pottery Studio

## The Scene: Earth, Wheel, Fire

[IMAGE 2 PLACEHOLDER — Conceptual Diagram]

Pottery is the most tactile of all crafts — your customers literally hold your brand in their hands. The weight of a mug, the texture of a glaze, the way a bowl fits in two palms. Your brand kit must be as grounded and elemental as the craft itself. Whether you sell functional stoneware or sculptural ceramics, your visual identity should feel like it was pulled from the earth.

- **Body Text:** Lora — organic serif for maker stories and process descriptions

- **Digital:** Work Sans — clean, modern for website and e-commerce

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q1: How do I brand an inherently imperfect product?**
Embrace wabi-sabi — the Japanese aesthetic of beauty in imperfection. Your branding can celebrate variation rather than hide it. A tag like each piece is unique or variations in glaze are intentional frames inconsistency as a feature.

**Q2: What is the best way to photograph pottery?**
Natural window light, neutral backgrounds (concrete, linen, raw wood), and lifestyle context (the mug with coffee, the bowl with soup). Show the piece being held so customers understand scale. Avoid studio lighting that flattens glaze textures.

**Q3: Should I sign or stamp every piece?**
Yes — a maker's mark is the original brand logo. Design a consistent stamp or signature style. It should be recognizable but not overwhelming. Lovart can design your maker's mark as part of the brand kit.

**Q4: How do I brand my glaze colors consistently?**
Name your glazes and photograph them under consistent lighting. Create a glaze swatch guide — like paint chips — showing each color on your clay body. Lovart's glaze catalog template standardizes this presentation.

**Q5: What packaging is right for ceramic goods?**
Protection first — ceramics break. Branded tissue, recycled kraft paper cushioning, and a thank-you note. The unboxing should feel like unwrapping a gift. Include care instructions inside the box, not on it (recyclable packaging is better).

**Q6: How do I price my work visually in a way that communicates value?**
Never apologize for pricing. Display prices cleanly without explanations. Group pieces by collection rather than by price. High-end pieces should get more white space and larger photos in your catalog — visual hierarchy implies value hierarchy.
