# Eye Contact with AI: Touch Edit Characters to Look at Camera (2026)

AI characters that look past the camera feel like mannequins in a Zoom call. Marketing wants connection; the model delivers a cheekbone tour. The wrong fix is regenerating the whole person until identity drifts. The right fix is usually local: Touch Edit the gaze, protect catchlights, and keep Identity Lock honest.

This slug had a broken Title Case title and a padded clone body. The query still earns impressions near position seven. This rewrite is the operator manual for look-at-camera repairs without vinyl eyes or a new stranger’s face.

## Who this is for

Campaign teams with recurring characters, thumbnail makers, brand mascot owners, anyone shipping portraits that must meet the viewer’s eyes.

## What “eye contact” actually is

- Gaze vector toward lens (not toward a shoulder ghost)
- Symmetry of iris/pupil read at publish size
- Catchlights consistent with scene light
- Eyelid shape that matches the identity
- No horror micro-asymmetry after edit

## Decision tree

1. Wrong person entirely? → Identity Lock / regen casting—not eye poke  
2. Pose OK, gaze off by a little? → Touch Edit eyes  
3. Head turned too far for honest eye contact? → New take or accept three-quarter gaze  
4. Eyes sharp but dead? → Catchlight + micro-contrast, not megasharpen  
5. Series must match? → Lock identity first, then edit gaze per frame  

## Preflight

Archive master. Confirm Identity Lock / refs. Note publish size (thumbnail vs hero). Note whether both eyes are visible. Note light direction.

## ChatCanvas sequence

3. Touch Edit iris/gaze region.  
4. Soft-proof at publish size.

6. Sibling frames: match catchlight story.  

## Touch Edit recipe: look at camera

Select eye region (both if visible). Instruct: rotate gaze to lens, keep iris color/identity, preserve catchlight side, forbid enlarging eyes into anime unless brand. Strength mild. One pass. Stop when phone-size reads as contact.

## Catchlights

One primary catchlight usually beats disco eyes. Match scene key. After edit, check both eyes agree.

## Identity Lock interactions

If lock is weak, eye edits can “loosen” the face into a cousin. Strengthen refs before surgery. See face retouch cluster: [how to edit faces](/blog/how-to-edit-faces-retouch-portraits-ai).

## Thumbnail vs hero

Thumbnails forgive less dead gaze and more contrast. Heroes forgive less anatomical weirdness. Soft-proof both if you ship both.

## Flop: I stared them into horror

I pushed gaze too far on a profile-ish head. The whites geometry lied. Fix: new three-quarter take, not more Touch Edit. Physics of orbits matter.

## FAQ

### How do I make an AI character look at the camera?
Prefer Touch Edit on gaze when pose allows; otherwise regenerate pose with Identity Lock.

### Why do AI eyes look dead?
Missing/wrong catchlights, over-smooth iris, mismatched gaze. Fix locally; avoid beauty mush.

### Can Touch Edit break identity?
Yes if aggressive. Mild passes + lock refs.

### Should both eyes always stare?
If head angle forbids honesty, prefer credible three-quarter engagement.

### What about animals / mascots?
Same locality principle; stylized brands may want graphic eyes—document the style.
