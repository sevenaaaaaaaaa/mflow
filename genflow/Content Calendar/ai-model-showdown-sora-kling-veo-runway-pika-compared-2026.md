## The Honest 100-Word Review of Each Model

### Sora (OpenAI)
Sora produces the most beautiful AI video in existence — when it works. Its understanding of light, atmosphere, and cinematic language is unmatched. The problem: availability is gated behind a $200/month ChatGPT Pro subscription, generation is slow, and output consistency swings wildly. You will get one masterpiece and five unusable clips from the same prompt. Sora is the model you use when you need something *beautiful*, not when you need something *reliable*. For creative filmmaking, it is peerless. For commercial production, it is unpredictable.

### Kling (Kuaishou)
Kling's motion realism is the best in the industry. Characters walk like real people. Fabric moves convincingly. Facial micro-expressions are nuanced and natural. If your content involves human subjects doing human things, Kling delivers more consistently than any competitor. The downside: try to generate anything abstract, surreal, or heavily stylized and Kling falls flat. It is optimized for photorealism and everyday scenes. The stylistic range is narrow. If you live in that range, Kling is your best option. If you want to experiment, look elsewhere.

### Veo (Google)
Veo is what happens when Google builds a video model: technically excellent, reliably consistent, creatively unremarkable. Temporal coherence — the "no flickering or morphing between frames" problem — is the best in class. Enterprise teams love Veo because the output is safe and predictable. Creators find it sterile. Veo is the Honda Accord of AI video: it will never break down on you, and it will never thrill you either. Deep Google ecosystem integration (YouTube, Ads, Cloud) makes it a natural choice for organizations already committed to Google infrastructure.

### Runway (RunwayML)
Runway's Gen-3 Alpha model is solid, but Runway's real strength is not generation — it is *editing*. Video-to-video transformation, inpainting, background removal, motion brush. Runway treats AI video as a creative editing medium, not just a text-to-video vending machine. If your workflow involves taking existing footage and transforming it, Runway is the best tool available. If you want to generate video purely from text prompts, Runway's generation quality trails Sora and Kling. Pricing at volume gets expensive fast.

### Pika
Pika is the Instagram of AI video: fun, social, aesthetically pleasing, and shallow. The interface is dead simple. The generation is fast. The social features (remixing, sharing, community prompts) are genuinely fun. But clip lengths are short, creative control is limited, and the output quality caps out below professional needs. Pika is perfect for social media managers who need quick, eye-catching short clips and do not need fine control. For professional video production, it is a toy.

### Hailuo (MiniMax)
Hailuo is the model most people have not heard of but should pay attention to. MiniMax has quietly built a video model that competes with Kling on realism and beats most competitors on generation speed. Character consistency across multiple generations is surprisingly strong. The limitation: Hailuo is heavily optimized for Asian market content and visual styles. Global distribution and English-language prompt understanding lag behind the Western-focused models. Watch this one — it is improving fast.

### Luma (Dream Machine)
Luma's differentiator is camera movement. If you want sweeping crane shots, dolly zooms, orbiting camera paths, or tracking shots, Luma executes them more convincingly than anyone else. The model understands 3D space and perspective shifts in a way that feels intentional rather than hallucinated. The trade-off: generation is slower than competitors, maximum clip length is limited, and the "dreamlike" aesthetic that gives the model its name can feel floaty and imprecise when you wanted sharp realism.

### CapCut
CapCut is not really an AI video generator — it is a video editor with AI features bolted on. The template library is enormous and platform-optimized (TikTok, Reels, Shorts). If you need social media video content *fast*, CapCut's template + AI enhancement combination is hard to beat. But you are working within templates, not creating from scratch. The creative ceiling is low. For original, prompt-driven AI video creation, CapCut is the wrong tool. For template-based social content at speed, it is the right one.

### Hedra
Hedra does one thing: character-driven video with talking, expressive faces. Lip sync quality is excellent. Facial animation is nuanced. If your content involves a character speaking to camera — an AI avatar, a virtual spokesperson, a digital host — Hedra is purpose-built for that. Outside of that narrow lane, it does almost nothing. Do not try to generate a landscape video or an abstract visual piece in Hedra. It is a specialist, not a generalist.

### Pollo AI
Pollo sits at the budget end of the market, and it knows it. The quality ceiling is lower than every other model on this list. But it is cheap or free, the interface is approachable, and it is perfectly adequate for someone who wants to experiment with AI video without spending money or learning complex tools. Pollo is a starter tool — use it to understand what AI video can do, then graduate to something more capable when you outgrow it.

### OpenArt
OpenArt is primarily an AI image generation platform with some video capabilities tacked on. Its image generation (via multiple underlying models) is genuinely good. The video features exist but are not competitive with dedicated video models. If you need still images with occasional simple motion, OpenArt works. If you need serious AI video generation, use a tool built for that purpose.

### Pictory
Pictory is in a different category entirely. It does not generate video from text prompts. It takes existing long-form content (blog posts, podcast episodes, webinar recordings) and automatically extracts highlights, adds captions, and reformats for social media. It is a content repurposing tool, not a generative AI tool. Useful for content marketers who want to turn one long video into ten short clips. Not useful for anyone who wants AI to create video from scratch.
