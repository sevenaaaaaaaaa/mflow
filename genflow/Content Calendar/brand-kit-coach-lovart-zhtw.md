## Warm, Inspiring, Trustworthy: The Coach Color Palette

[IMAGE 2 PLACEHOLDER — Conceptual Diagram]

Coaching palettes must balance warmth (you're inviting vulnerability) with professionalism (you're charging professional fees). Lovart's **Coach Palette Engine**:

**Life / Transformational Coach:** Warm terracotta (#C67B4B), golden hour yellow (#D4A843), cream (#FDF6EC), sage (#9CAF88), and deep brown (#4A3728). These colors evoke sunrise — new beginnings, growth, transformation. The terracotta and gold provide warmth; the sage and cream keep it grounded. No harsh contrasts — this palette reassures, which is the emotional prerequisite for coaching work.

**Executive / Leadership Coach:** Deep navy (#1B2A4A), warm gray (#6B7280), ivory (#FDFBF7), copper accent (#B87333), and slate (#3A4A5A). More structured than the life coach palette, reflecting the corporate context. Navy and gray signal business credibility; copper adds warmth without sacrificing professionalism (unlike orange or yellow, which can feel too casual for executive contexts).

**Health / Wellness Coach:** Eucalyptus (#A3B5A6), warm sand (#D4C4A8), aloe (#C5D8C5), sunstone (#D4A574), and cream (#F5F0E8). Nature-derived colors that signal vitality and well-being. The palette feels clean, alive, and nurturing — the visual equivalent of "I will help you feel better in your body."

Lovart's coach palettes include **Emotional Impact Notes** — how each color psychologically affects the coaching client. Terracotta: warmth, safety, grounding. Gold: possibility, optimism, transformation. Navy: trust, structure, authority. Sage: growth, calm, balance. Coaches select palettes not just for aesthetics but for the emotional state they want prospects to enter.

## Typography: Approachable Authority

**Coach Name Font** — A warm serif (Lora, Cormorant Garamond) for life/wellness coaches; a clean sans-serif (Raleway, Montserrat) for executive/career coaches. The name font sets the relationship tone: serif says "I'll listen deeply;" sans-serif says "I'll get you results." Both are true — your font emphasizes which promise leads.

**Program/Body Font** — For sales pages, program guides, and client materials. Inter or DM Sans at 16–18pt for digital reading. Coaching clients range from 25 to 65 — the body font must accommodate presbyopia (age-related reading difficulty) with generous sizing and spacing.

**Inspiration/Quote Font** — For social media quotes and program mantras. A lighter-weight italic of the name font (Lora Italic, Cormorant Light Italic). Inspirational content is the highest-volume social media output for most coaches — the quote font must feel elevated, not generic.

**Client Workbook Font** — For the PDF worksheets and exercises clients complete between sessions. Clean sans-serif at 12–13pt with generous line spacing (1.6x) and form fields that are easy to fill digitally or by hand. Workbook design affects completion rates — beautiful workbooks get completed.

## Program Materials: Where Transformation Becomes Tangible

[IMAGE 3 PLACEHOLDER — Real UI Screenshot]

**Program One-Pager** — The most important sales document in coaching. A single page with: program name, transformation promise (what they'll achieve), program structure (number of sessions, duration, format), what's included (coaching sessions, assessments, resources, support between sessions), investment (price presentation — see below), and testimonial snippet (one powerful quote with client name and result). Designed to be read in under 90 seconds.

**Welcome Packet** — Sent after enrollment. Includes: welcome letter, program overview and timeline, session preparation guide (how to prepare for coaching sessions), communication guidelines (response times, between-session support), assessment links, and a "Your First Session" checklist. The welcome packet transforms buyer's remorse into excitement.

**Client Session Summary Template** — For post-session recaps. Includes: session date, key insights, action items (with checkboxes), and "Before Next Session" preparation. Branded session summaries reinforce professionalism and increase accountability completion rates.

**Assessment/Self-Discovery Guide** — Branded worksheets for values clarification, strengths assessment, goal setting, and habit tracking. Lovart generates these as fillable PDFs. Coaches who use branded assessments are perceived as having a "proprietary methodology" rather than delivering generic coaching.

## Pricing Presentation: The Psychology of Coaching Fees

Coaching is an intangible service — pricing presentation must make the value tangible. Lovart's **Coach Pricing Guide** applies: value-first pricing language (list everything included before showing the price — the price should feel like an obvious yes after reading the value), tiered program options (3 levels — the Goldilocks strategy), and "Investment" framing (coaching prices presented as an investment, not a cost). Visual treatment: prices in the brand's accent color, slightly larger than body text, with a payment plan option visibly presented (reduces sticker shock for premium programs).

## FAQ

**Q: Should my coaching brand feel like me or like a company?**
A: Solo coaches: 100% personal brand. Your face, your name, your story. Clients hire YOU, not a coaching methodology. Even if you have a program name, the brand should feel like an extension of your personality. Lovart's brand kit emphasizes personal brand over corporate brand for solo coaches.

**Q: How do I make my coaching brand look premium without looking unapproachable?**
A: Premium comes from consistency and detail, not luxury signals. Clean typography, generous white space, professional photography, and flawless grammar. What makes a coaching brand feel unapproachable: ultra-luxury imagery (private jets, designer clothes), jargon-heavy language, and absence of real client results. Lovart's kit balances premium presentation with approachable warmth.

**Q: Can Lovart design coaching certification program branding?**
A: Yes. On the $49 Pro plan, Lovart generates a **Certification Program Brand**: program logo, curriculum guide (module-by-module breakdown), student workbook template, graduation certificate design, and alumni badge. The certification brand sits within your main coach brand system.

**Q: How does Lovart handle group coaching program branding vs. 1:1 coaching?**
A: Lovart generates **Program-Specific Sub-Brands**: your main coach brand (name, photo, core palette) remains constant. Individual programs get color-coded identifiers — "The Leadership Accelerator" in copper, "Executive Presence Intensive" in teal. Programs feel distinct but clearly belong to the same coach.

**Q: Does Lovart include a sales page template for coaching programs?**
A: Yes. The **Program Sales Page Template** includes sections for: hero with transformation headline, pain-point identification, program overview (timeline, structure, inclusions), curriculum/modules breakdown, about the coach, testimonials with photos, investment/pricing, FAQ, and CTA. Designed as a wireframe — coaches add their copy and launch.

**Q: What's the biggest coaching branding mistake Lovart helps avoid?**
A: Generic inspirational branding. Stock photos of sunsets and mountains, Unsplash images of people meditating, gradient backgrounds with motivational quotes. This visual language is used by thousands of coaches and signals "I haven't invested in my brand." Lovart's toolkit emphasizes: real photos of YOU, original visual identity, and specific language about YOUR methodology — not generic coaching clichés.
