# AI Floor Plan Tools Compared: RoomSketcher vs Planner5D vs Lovart

[IMAGE 1 PLACEHOLDER — Persona Scenario]

**Most "floor plan tools" are actually furniture arrangement simulators. They let you drag a sofa onto a rectangle and call it architecture. A real floor plan and a furniture layout are not the same thing — and confusing them costs homeowners tens of thousands.**

The floor plan software market is filled with tools that prioritize visual appeal over architectural accuracy. They produce beautiful 3D renders of spaces that would fail a building inspection, create rooms that cannot be furnished as shown, and generate measurements that do not add up.

We tested RoomSketcher, Planner5D, and Lovart by designing the same 2,200 sq ft single-family home on each platform. The brief: three bedrooms, two bathrooms, open-plan living/kitchen/dining, attached garage, and compliance with International Residential Code clearance requirements.

## Myth #1: "A Pretty 3D Render Means a Functional Floor Plan"

Planner5D's marketing centers on photorealistic 3D renders. The renders look impressive. The floor plans underneath them are sometimes impossible.

Test case: a 12'x14' bedroom with a queen bed, two nightstands, a dresser, and a 24" clearance path. Planner5D's furniture placement algorithm fit everything into the room — by ignoring the 24" clearance requirement and placing the dresser 8" from the bed. The render looked spacious. The actual room would be unlivable.

RoomSketcher requires manual dimensioning. You can place furniture correctly, but the tool will not warn you if a hallway is 28" wide (IRC minimum is 36"). It assumes you know what you are doing and silently lets you make expensive mistakes.

Lovart automatically verifies IRC minimum clearances: 36" hallways, 24" paths in sleeping areas, 30" clearance in front of fixtures, 15" toilet centerline to side wall. When a layout violates code, the system flags the issue and suggests alternatives. This is not a design feature — it is a liability prevention feature.

**The Verdict:** A design tool that does not check code compliance is not a design tool. It is a visualization toy.

---

## Myth #2: "DIY Floor Plans Save Money"

They can. They can also create plans that cost $15,000 in change orders when the contractor discovers the hallway is too narrow for code.

RoomSketcher is marketed to real estate agents and homeowners. It is approachable. It produces plans that look professional. It does not produce plans that are guaranteed to be buildable.

Planner5D is marketed as "home design made easy." The floor plan module exists primarily to support the interior design features. The architectural precision is secondary.

Lovart approaches floor plans as architectural documents. Output includes: dimensioned floor plans, electrical layout suggestions, plumbing rough-in locations, window and door schedules, and structural grid references. This is not a substitute for an architect's stamp — but it produces a document an architect can review and approve rather than one they would discard and start over.

## Myth #3: "All Floor Plan Tools Export to CAD"

[IMAGE 3 PLACEHOLDER — Real UI Screenshot]

Exporting a JPG of a floor plan and exporting a DWG with proper layers, blocks, and dimension styles are not the same thing.

RoomSketcher exports PDF and image formats. No DWG/DXF export. If your architect or contractor uses AutoCAD, they will redraw your plan from scratch — costing time and money.

Planner5D exports image formats only. No CAD export at any tier.

Lovart exports DWG and DXF with proper layers (A-WALL, A-DOOR, A-GLAZ, A-ANNO-DIMS), dimension styles, and block definitions. A contractor can open the file in AutoCAD and work from it directly. This single feature represents the difference between a plan that can be shared with professionals and a plan that must be entirely recreated.

## The Measurement Integrity Test

We exported the same floor plan from each platform and measured total square footage using AutoCAD's area command. The brief specified 2,200 sq ft.

| Platform | Measured Sq Ft | Deviation |

| Planner5D | 2,241 | +1.9% |

Planner5D's +1.9% deviation is significant. On a 2,200 sq ft home at $200/sq ft construction cost, that 41 sq ft discrepancy represents $8,200 in unexpected costs if carried through to construction bids. Precision matters.

## FAQ

**Q: Can Lovart replace an architect?**
No. Lovart produces architectural-quality floor plans that require professional review and stamp before construction. It accelerates the design process but does not replace professional judgment, site analysis, or structural engineering.

**Q: Does Planner5D work for professional use?**
Planner5D is optimized for interior visualization, not architectural documentation. Professional architects and designers will find the lack of CAD export and code compliance tools limiting.

**Q: Which tool is best for real estate listings?**
RoomSketcher offers real estate-specific features including branded floor plans. Lovart generates listing-ready floor plans with optional 3D walkthrough exports. Planner5D produces attractive 3D renders but less precise 2D documentation.

**Q: Can I import an existing floor plan sketch?**
Lovart accepts photo uploads of hand-drawn sketches and converts them to dimensioned plans. RoomSketcher and Planner5D require manual entry from scratch.

**Q: What about multi-story buildings?**
All three support multi-story design. Lovart handles up to 5 stories in a single project file. RoomSketcher supports up to 3. Planner5D supports 2 in the free tier, unlimited in paid.

**Q: Does Lovart support metric and imperial?**
Yes — both measurement systems with automatic conversion. Specify your preference in the prompt or project settings.
