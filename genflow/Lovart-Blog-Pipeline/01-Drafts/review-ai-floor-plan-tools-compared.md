# AI Floor Plan Tools Compared 2026: RoomSketcher vs Planner 5D vs Lovart

People search “ai floor plan tools compared” wanting one winner. They usually need two different jobs separated: a measure-aware plan that can survive a contractor conversation, and a brand-coherent visual that can survive a listing page or campaign. Those jobs do not always live in the same subscription.

RoomSketcher and Planner 5D sit closer to the planning / home-design software tradition. Lovart sits closer to the Design Agent tradition: ChatCanvas briefs, Brand Kit memory, and marketing-grade stills that may include floor-plan-like visuals without pretending to be CAD. Confusing these lanes is how you get pretty wrong walls.

This rewrite replaces a padded clone on the slug. Impressions near position nine deserve a real comparison: jobs, limits, bake-off method, and when to hybrid.

## RoomSketcher: where it earns keep

RoomSketcher is often chosen when the center of gravity is the plan itself: walls, rooms, 2D/3D toggles, and presentation to clients who think in rooms. It behaves like home design software with AI-assisted acceleration depending on current features—not like a campaign OS.

### Strengths (typical)

- Plan-first mental model
- Familiar path from 2D to 3D visualization
- Useful for realtor / renovator conversations when used honestly

### Limits

- Brand systems across ads, blogs, and social still need a marketing desk
- AI features do not erase the need for accurate inputs
- Treating a quick plan as contractor gospel is still on you

### Fit score prompts

Choose RoomSketcher-like tools when the weekly artifact is “updated floor plan for client review,” not “30 listing ads in brand kit.”

## Planner 5D: where it earns keep

Planner 5D often wins DIY energy: furniture placement play, interior look exploration, approachable UI for non-pros. It is a strong “what if the sofa were here” machine when expectations are exploration-grade.

### Strengths (typical)

- Friendly interior sandbox
- Fast layout ideation for homeowners
- Visual inspiration loops

### Limits

- Accuracy folklore—“AI said it fits” is not a tape measure
- Brand marketing continuity across a portfolio still needs another system
- Professional deliverables may need export + human cleanup

### Fit score prompts

Choose Planner 5D-like tools when the buyer is exploring lifestyle layouts, not when you are shipping a regulated proptech brand system.

## Lovart: where it earns keep

Lovart is a Design Agent. You brief on ChatCanvas, plan with MCoT, lock brand with Brand Kit, keep characters/mascots consistent with Identity Lock when needed, and repair with Touch Edit / Edit Elements. Floor-plan-like visuals are one asset class among many—heroes, listing graphics, ads, carousels—not a CAD replacement.

### Strengths

- Brand-coherent sets across sizes
- Fast variants for campaigns without five tabs of glue
- Regional repair without regenerating entire boards
- Better when the plan graphic must live beside product UI, lifestyle, and ads

### Limits

- Do not use marketing plan art as construction truth
- Complex measured renovations still need proper plan tools / architects
- Garbage briefs produce pretty wrong rooms

### Fit score prompts

Choose Lovart when the weekly artifact is “on-brand listing + ads + landing visuals,” possibly incorporating plan diagrams as designed graphics.

## Kill-shot differences

1. **Truth vs story:** RoomSketcher/Planner lanes lean spatial planning story; Lovart leans brand story.  
2. **System vs project:** Design Agent memory across assets vs project-by-project home design files.  
3. **Repair loop:** Touch Edit/Edit Elements for campaign surgery vs re-export from plan software.  
4. **Liability posture:** All tools can be misused; marketing AI misuse shows up as wrong square footage claims—forbid them unless verified.

## Bake-off checklist (run before you subscribe)

Use the same brief for each tool:

1. 2-bed apartment, known dimensions sheet (you supply numbers)  
2. Produce 2D plan graphic  
3. Produce one furnished view or diagram  
4. Produce one marketing variant (social size)  
5. Time to first usable export  
6. Dimension honesty check against your sheet  
7. Brand continuity if you already have colors/logo  
8. Repair a small label mistake without full redraw  

Score 0–2 per row. Pay for the winner of your job, not the prettiest blog hero from a vendor.

## The flop: I marketed AI plans as “accurate to the inch”

A listing campaign used AI-furnished plans with confident dimension callouts that were never field-verified. Buyers showed up angry. The fix was language: “illustrative layout” + verified stats from the listing sheet only. Tools were not the villain; claim posture was.

## Claim language that keeps you out of trouble

Safe: illustrative layout, concept plan, furniture ideas subject to verification.  
Unsafe: exact sqft from an AI guess, structural assurances, permit-ready claims without pros.

## ChatCanvas recipe for marketing plan graphics (Lovart)

Job: listing plan graphic for ICP. Subject: 2D plan from provided reference export, north label, empty type bands for room names in layout tool. Constraints: Brand Kit, high legibility, no fake dimensions. Forbid: inventing doors/windows not in reference, fake stamps, unreadable micro labels.

Then Touch Edit dust/artifacts. Use Edit Elements for background plates in lifestyle-adjacent graphics—not for inventing rooms.

## RoomSketcher deep notes for operators

Use it when clients already think in rooms and walls. Keep a “measurement owner.” Export clean 2D for marketing rather than screenshotting cluttered UI. Build a house style for line weights so exports look related even before Lovart framing. When AI features suggest furniture, separate structure layers from décor layers mentally—even if the UI makes it easy to mush them.

Common failure: realtor updates marketing copy sqft from an unverified AI plan. Policy: listing sheet wins; graphics follow.

## Planner 5D deep notes for operators

Lean into exploration workshops. Timebox client play sessions. Export chosen options with names like `explore_A/B/C`, not `final`. When a client falls in love with an impossible layout (door clashes, code issues), you need a professional checkpoint before ads spend. Planner energy is a feature; unchecked commitment is the bug.

## Lovart deep notes for operators

Build a “plan graphic kit”: north arrow style, line weight, room label typography rules (prefer HTML/vector), Brand Kit colors for fills, forbidden fake stamps. Store verified reference exports in the board. Generate marketing variants with empty bands. Touch Edit collisions. Edit Elements for seasonal frames around a locked plan graphic—not for moving structural walls.

If someone asks Lovart to “make the apartment bigger,” refuse. That is not a creative request; it is a compliance request in costume.

## Dimension honesty protocol

2. Numbers copied, not vibes

## Furniture realism protocol

If a SKU is sold, prefer true models or clear “style approximate” labels. AI sofas that imply inventory cause returns.

## Lighting in dollhouse views

Pretty sun sells. It also hides small rooms’ gloom. For serious renovations, include a neutral view. For lifestyle marketing, dollhouse is fine with honest size stats elsewhere.

## Mobile legend design

Most plans die on mobile because legends become microtype. Design a mobile-first plan: fewer labels in-pixels, more in HTML accordion. AI should leave empty space for that system.

## Portal compression QA

Upload tests to private listing drafts. Line weights that look fine on desktop can vanish. Adjust before spending boost budget.

## Collaboration with photographers

Photos show height and finish; plans show relationships. Do not let AI plan art contradict photo evidence (windows, fireplace). Cross-check.

## Collaboration with architects

Marketing can start earlier with illustrative graphics, but architects own construction truth. Clear handoff language prevents ego wars.

## International units and norms

US sqft vs EU m²; door swing conventions; bed counts marketing vs legal definitions. Localize the system of record, then graphics.

## Accessibility expanded

Provide text descriptions of circulation for complex plans. Do not convey essential info only via pastel fills.

## Security for unreleased developments

Unreleased floor plans are confidential. Permissioned boards. Watermark previews. AI does not reduce secrecy needs.

## Student / education use

Great for teaching spatial literacy. Bad for pretending students produced permit sets. Label coursework illustrative.

## Enterprise governance

Approve tool lanes in writing. Approve claim language. Log bake-off results. Retire shadow IT floorplan apps that invent metrics.

## Color and line-weight constitution for plan graphics

Pick:

- Wall line weight for web  
- Wall line weight for print  
- Fill opacity for rooms (pastel, accessible)  
- Accent for circulation arrows if used  
- North arrow style  
- Scale bar policy (only if true)  

Store in Brand Kit notes. AI prompts must cite the constitution. Without it, every freelancer invents a new cartography.

## Typography rules for room labels

Prefer outside-the-image HTML labels for multilingual listings. If burned-in labels are required for portal constraints, use large type, few words, and Touch Edit readiness. Never rely on 6pt furniture SKU labels as the only legend.

## Scale bars: when to show them

Show only when the graphic is dimensionally trustworthy. A decorative scale bar on an illustrative sketch is a lie costume. If unsure, omit scale bar and keep “illustrative” in caption.

## Door swings and code literacy (marketing level)

Marketers need enough literacy to spot impossible swings into cabinets. You do not need to be an architect to ask “does this door hit the island?” Build a one-page “red flags” sheet for juniors.

## Stairs and level changes

AI loves pretty staircases that ignore headroom. Multi-level listings need human checks. When unsure, simplify the marketing graphic rather than inventing elegant but false stairs.

## Outdoor spaces and property lines

Balconies, yards, and parking are lawsuit magnets when invented. Only draw what the listing sheet and photos support. If AI adds a hot tub, delete it.

## Parking and storage

Garages get hallucinated. Storage closets vanish. Cross-check with listing amenities list every time.

## Commercial vs residential plans

Commercial circulation and exits are specialized. Do not casually AI-generate restaurant seating plans for marketing as if code-complete. Label concepts heavily.

## HOA / building rule overlays

Some buildings forbid certain layouts in marketing if they imply unapproved renovations. Compliance review is not optional in those portfolios.

## Photography alignment grid

Create a checklist: window count per wall photo vs plan; kitchen island presence; fireplace; balcony door. Mismatches trigger hold.

## 3D material honesty

Marble that is actually laminate, in dollhouse view, can create buyer bitterness. Prefer honest finish callouts in text; keep dollhouse materials approximate unless staged with real finishes.

## Deep dive: what “AI” means in each lane

In plan software, AI may mean auto-furnish, smart room detection from photos/scans (when available), or layout suggestions. In a Design Agent, AI means multimodal generation/editing under brand constraints. Same buzzword, different contracts with truth.

Ask vendors: what is automated, what is generative, what is measured, what is illustrative.

## Deep dive: photo-to-plan features

Photo-to-plan is magical in demos and messy in hallways with mirrors. If you use it, treat output as draft until measured. Marketing teams should not scrape phone photos into “accurate” listing plans without ops discipline.

## Deep dive: 3D dollhouse views

Dollhouse views sell emotion. They also hide measurement sins with pretty sunlight. Use them for feeling; keep 2D plans for clarity; keep verified stats in text.

## Deep dive: furniture libraries

Libraries speed ideation and create sameness. Brand furniture retailers may need custom SKUs modeled elsewhere. AI-generated sofas that approximate a SKU can create returns—same honesty rules as product photography.

## Deep dive: annotations and stamps

Fake architect stamps are malpractice cosplay. Empty bands + real review process only.
