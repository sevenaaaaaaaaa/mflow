## Business Card Types

### Classic Professional

Clean, structured, timeless. White or cream stock, serif or clean sans-serif typography, logo prominent but not dominant. The standard for law firms, consulting, finance, and traditional professional services.

```
@businesscard style:classic name: "Dr. Elena Vasquez" title: "Partner" company: "Meridian Consulting" contact: phone, email, LinkedIn
```

### Creative Industry

Bold colors, distinctive typography, memorable layouts. For designers, photographers, artists, agencies, and anyone whose card should communicate creativity before the recipient reads a word.

```
@businesscard style:creative name: "Marcus Chen" title: "Creative Director" company: "Studio M" palette: bold, warm accent: illustration element
```

### Minimalist

The design equivalent of a firm handshake and direct eye contact. Minimalist cards use negative space as a design element — one side logo, one side contact info, nothing extraneous. Popular with tech startups, architects, and modern brands.

```
@businesscard style:minimalist name: "Aiko Tanaka" title: "Product Designer" company: "Linear" accent: single color stripe
```

### Tech and Startup

Modern, often with a dark background, gradient accent, or subtle tech-industry visual cues. Designed to feel current — these are the cards people turn over in their hand at a networking event.

```
@businesscard style:tech name: "Jordan Park" title: "CTO & Co-Founder" company: "Veridian AI" palette: dark, gradient accent
```

### Double-Sided

Front: logo and name. Back: full contact details, QR code, and brand tagline. Double-sided cards carry more information without feeling crowded. This is the most common premium card format.

```
@businesscard style:double-sided name: "Sarah Okafor" title: "Real Estate Agent" company: "Compass" back: QR to listings, photo, testimonials snippet
```

### Digital Business Card

Not a physical card — a screen-optimized card for email signatures, virtual meeting backgrounds, LinkedIn profile headers, and digital sharing via Apple/Google Wallet. Includes tap-to-save functionality.

```
@businesscard style:digital name: "Carlos Mendez" title: "Sales Director" company: "Nexus Solutions" format: email-signature, wallet-pass
```

## Business Card Design Rules Lovart Follows

**Readable typography above all.** A business card is functionally useless if the phone number is illegible. Lovart enforces minimum type sizes for contact information regardless of the design style selected.

**Logo-first hierarchy.** The brand mark or company name should be the first thing the eye registers. Lovart structures layouts so the brand element anchors the visual flow, with personal details following.

**QR codes must scan.** Lovart generates QR codes at sufficient size and contrast to scan reliably from a held card — tested at typical hand-to-hand distance.

**White space is not wasted space.** Cards that try to say everything say nothing. Lovart designs include breathing room. A clean card on quality paper stock makes a better impression than a crowded card.
