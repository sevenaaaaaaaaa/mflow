### 1. Recipe Card Design: The Asset That Makes or Breaks a Recipe Post

The recipe card at the bottom of your blog post is the single most important piece of graphic design in your entire business. It's what gets printed (and stuck on fridges), what gets pinned (driving Pinterest traffic for years), what gets screenshotted (and shared in group chats), and what determines whether a reader who cooked your recipe comes back for more. A distinctive recipe card is a brand-building asset. A generic recipe card is an invisible one.

Lovart creates recipe card designs that readers recognize at a glance. "Design a recipe card template system for 'Saffron & Salt' — our Mediterranean-inspired food blog. Three card formats: 1) Full recipe card (for blog posts) — ingredients list left column, step-by-step instructions right column, hero photo of finished dish at top, prep time/cook time/serves icons, 'Notes from My Kitchen' tip box, nutritional info collapsed section, print-friendly version. 2) Pinterest-optimized pin version — 2:3 ratio, vertical, hero photo dominant, recipe title in our brand typography, 'Get the Full Recipe' CTA with our URL. 3) Quick recipe card (for Instagram carousel slides) — simplified, swipe-friendly, one step per slide. The card system should feel like a cookbook page — publishable quality, consistent across every recipe on our site." The AI generates a cohesive card system where the full blog card, the Pinterest pin, and the Instagram carousel all feel like they belong to the same brand — a reader who sees your pin and then visits your blog experiences visual continuity, which signals professionalism and builds trust.

For SEO: "Our recipe cards need to satisfy Google's recipe structured data requirements — ingredients list, cook time, prep time, nutrition, and reviews all visually present and properly formatted. The design should make Google happy AND readers happy."

### 2. Step-by-Step Process Photo Layouts: Teaching Through Design

Process shots are where food blogging separates itself from recipe websites. Showing the dough at each rise stage, demonstrating the knife cut for julienne, illustrating "until golden brown" — these are teaching moments that build reader confidence and reduce comment-section questions ("How golden is golden brown??").

Lovart creates process-shot layout templates that make your teaching visual. "Design a 'Process Steps' layout template for blog posts — up to 10 step-by-step photos. Each step gets: a numbered circle overlay (1-10), a large process photo showing the technique/state, and a 2-3 line caption below or to the side. Design: clean grid or alternating left/right layout for visual rhythm. Our brand — the numbers match our brand color, the typography is our font, the layout framing ties to our recipe card aesthetic. For a recipe like homemade pasta, the 10 steps walk the reader from flour well to finished fettuccine — the visual layout should tell the story even if someone scrolls without reading captions."

For Instagram carousel teaching posts: "Create an Instagram carousel template for 'How to Make Fresh Mozzarella in 30 Minutes' — 8 slides, one step per slide. Slide 1: title card with finished dish photo, Slides 2-7: each a critical step with a single instruction ('Heat the milk to 90°F,' 'Add rennet and let rest 5 min'), Slide 8: 'Save this for your next cheese-making day' + CTA. Our brand, educational, high-save potential."
