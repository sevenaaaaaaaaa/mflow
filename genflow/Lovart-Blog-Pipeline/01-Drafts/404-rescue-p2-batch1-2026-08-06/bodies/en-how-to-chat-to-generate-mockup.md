# How to Chat-Generate a Product Mockup in Lovart (Without Photoshop)

Product mockups used to mean hours in Photoshop: smart objects, vanishing points, layer masks, and a library of angle photos you hope still match the SKU. Teams still search **how to chat generate mockup** because the old workflow breaks whenever marketing swaps packaging, colorway, or marketplace aspect ratio the night before launch. This page restores a missing blog URL with a practical Lovart path: describe the shot in ChatCanvas, let the Design Agent compose the scene, refine with Touch Edit, and export sizes your channel actually uses.

## What “chat-generate” means in Lovart

Chat-generating a mockup is not typing “make it cool” and hoping. In Lovart, the **Design Agent** reads your brief on **ChatCanvas**—the conversational surface documented in Lovart’s product knowledge base—and turns it into layout decisions: camera angle, surface, lighting, shadow density, and label placement. You stay in language. The canvas stays editable. That pairing matters when the PM replies, “Can we try navy instead of black?” and you need a answer in minutes, not a new PSD.

Before you prompt, load **Brand Kit** if the product already has rules: primary colors, type roles, logo clear space. Brand Kit is the memory layer that stops every mockup from drifting into a one-off palette. If you skip it, you will still get a picture; you will not get a system.

## Step one: Write a brief the agent can execute

Open ChatCanvas and write like a producer, not a poet. Name the product category, the surface (marble counter, matte paper, handheld), the camera (top-down, three-quarter, eye-level), the lighting (soft window, studio rim, outdoor shade), and the export intent (Amazon main image, Instagram story, pitch deck slide). Mention what must remain readable: logo, flavor text, regulatory line.

A weak brief sounds like “premium mockup for skincare.” A strong brief sounds like “three-quarter hero of a 50ml serum bottle on frosted glass, soft window light from left, label text legible, shadow falloff to right, export 2000×2000 for marketplace, leave top 15% empty for later headline overlay.” The second version gives the agent constraints it can satisfy and gives you criteria to judge the first result.

## Step two: Generate the hero frame

Send the brief. When the first frame lands on canvas, resist the urge to re-roll immediately. Zoom to label edges and check geometry. Mockups fail in retail when typography warps or caps look melted. If the bottle shape is right but the label is soft, use **Touch Edit** on the label region with a precise instruction: “Sharpen label type, keep bottle curvature, do not change background blur.” Touch Edit is for surgical edits; full re-prompts are for structural changes like angle or surface.

If the product photo reference exists, upload it in the agent flow described in Lovart docs for file upload on ChatCanvas. Reference anchoring reduces fantasy labels. For conceptual packaging not yet photographed, generate once, then attach the approved label PDF on the next pass so the agent aligns art to real copy.

## Step three: Build the campaign variants without restarting

One mockup rarely ships alone. Marketplace needs a white-background hero; social needs a lifestyle scene; email needs a square with CTA space. Stay in the same ChatCanvas thread and ask for derivative scenes that preserve the product mesh while changing context: “Same bottle, replace marble with light oak desk, add soft plant bokeh background, keep label unchanged.” The Design Agent reuses composition logic instead of treating each size as a unrelated image.

When your media plan lists six formats, ask for explicit dimensions per channel rather than scaling one PNG in a separate tool. Lovart’s resize workflows work best when you name targets up front: “Also export 1080×1920 story and 1200×628 link preview with safe margins.” Naming targets early prevents cropped CTAs later.

## Step four: QA like a merchandiser, not a fan

Walk through a short QA pass before you mark the mockup approved. Is the label copy exactly what legal signed off on? Does the shadow direction match other assets in the launch set? Are highlights blowing out on metallic caps? Does the file size meet channel limits without hidden compression surprises?

If something fails, fix in Touch Edit first; re-prompt second. Teams burn credits and time when they restart from zero for a one-line fix. Document the approved prompt snippet in your internal brief so the next SKU in the line inherits the same lighting recipe.

## Common mistakes I see in mockup chats

Operators ask for “realistic” without defining surface or light, then reject three generations for feeling random. They omit negative space for ad copy, then fight cropping in the ad manager. They generate before Brand Kit exists, then manually recolor six exports. They treat mockups as final photography and skip the legal read on label claims.

Another mistake is ignoring companion assets. A mockup page that 404’d often sat in a cluster with banner and email modules. ChatCanvas can orchestrate those relatives in one session if you tell the agent the cluster upfront: hero mockup plus email header plus square ad using the same bottle angle.

## Marketplace and pitch contexts

Amazon and Shopify heroes punish different sins. Marketplaces want clean edges, readable labels, and backgrounds that do not fight the zoom tile. Pitch decks want context that tells a story—desk scenes, hands, packaging stacks—without obscuring the SKU. Tell ChatCanvas which gate you are hitting. The agent chooses different shadow hardness and prop density when you name the channel.

For investor slides, I also ask for a “clean plate” export: product only plus optional alpha-friendly background for drop-in on slide masters. That second export costs one more pass but saves the CFO from seeing a lifestyle plant photo behind financial tables.

## After approval: handoff to ads and web

Once merchandising signs off, export named files (`sku-hero-marketplace.png`, `sku-story-lifestyle.png`) and paste the final prompt snippet into your ticket. Media buyers should not reverse-engineer lighting from PNGs alone. If you run paid social the same week, generate the ad-safe crop in the same session so color temperature matches the product detail page. Broken mockup URLs in sitemaps often trace back to campaigns that never received companion sizes; fixing the page is step one, fixing the cluster is step two.

When legal updates label copy post-approval, return to Touch Edit on the label layer instead of rebuilding the scene. That discipline keeps mockup libraries consistent across seasons and makes this restored how-to page useful beyond a one-off tutorial.

Teams migrating off Photoshop often ask whether chat mockups are “real design.” They are real when briefs, Brand Kit, and QA gates are real. The canvas is a production surface, not a toy, once approvals attach to named exports and revision history lives in the thread instead of orphaned PNGs in a downloads folder.

## FAQ

**Do I need Photoshop at all?**  
Not for many ecommerce and pitch workflows. You may still export to Photoshop for exotic retouching, but the Lovart path covers most launch mockups.

**How long should the first mockup take?**  
With a tight brief and Brand Kit loaded, my first usable hero often lands under fifteen minutes including one Touch Edit pass.

**Can I match an existing photo shoot?**  
Upload reference stills and specify matching light direction and surface. Perfect parity is not guaranteed, but directionally matched sets are realistic.
