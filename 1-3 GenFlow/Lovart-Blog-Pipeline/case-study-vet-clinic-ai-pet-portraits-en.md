# How Vet Clinic Owner Dr. Hanna Bergstrom Created 300 Pet Portraits — And Became the Most-Recommended Vet in Stockholm

Dr. Hanna Bergstrom's veterinary clinic was good at medicine. It was mediocre at marketing.

Djurkliniken Söder had served Stockholm's Södermalm neighborhood for 11 years — a 4-vet practice with loyal clients, competent care, and a social media presence that consisted of infrequent Facebook posts about vaccination schedules. The clinic was profitable but stagnant. New client registrations had been flat for three years.

"We were the clinic people trusted once they found us. The problem was finding us. Our marketing was clinical, impersonal, and frankly boring. 'Remember to vaccinate your cat.' Who engages with that?"

Hanna needed something her clients would actually share — something that made the clinic memorable, shareable, and worth talking about at the dog park. She found it in the most unlikely place: AI-generated pet portraits.

In six months, Djurkliniken Söder created 300 personalized AI pet portraits for clients. The portraits were shared thousands of times on social media. New client registrations jumped 40%. And the clinic became, as one client put it, "the vet that makes your dog look like royalty."

---

## The Background

Djurkliniken Söder is a well-regarded but under-marketed veterinary practice. Hanna, 42, is an excellent veterinarian and an indifferent marketer. She'd tried the standard playbook — social media posts about pet health, seasonal reminders, occasional client photos — and the results were exactly what the playbook promised: modest engagement from existing clients, zero viral reach, no new-client acquisition.

"The problem with pet-health content is that it's important but not shareable. People read it, nod, and move on. Nobody tags their friends in a post about feline dental hygiene. I needed something that made people want to share — something that made my clients proud of their pets and proud of their vet."

The idea came from an unexpected source: a client who brought in a beautifully framed portrait of her Maine Coon cat, painted by a local artist for 2,500 kronor ($240). Hanna looked at the portrait and thought: "Every pet owner would love one of these. Almost none can afford it."

What if the clinic could create something similar — not hand-painted, but beautiful, personalized, and delivered as part of the veterinary experience — at a cost approaching zero?

---

## The Pain Points

### 1. The Engagement Desert

Veterinary marketing faces a structural engagement problem. The content that's clinically important (vaccination schedules, parasite prevention, dental care) generates minimal social engagement. The content that generates engagement (cute pet photos, funny animal videos) has no connection to the veterinary practice. The clinic's social media existed in a no-man's-land between useful and shareable — achieving neither.

### 2. The Referral Plateau

Veterinary practices grow primarily through word of mouth. But "word of mouth" in the social media era isn't a conversation at the dog park — it's a share, a tag, a story repost. Hanna's clinic generated plenty of satisfied clients but very few social shares — because there was nothing visual, remarkable, or gift-like to share. The referral engine was running at 10% of its potential because there was no fuel.

### 3. The Emotional Connection Gap

Clients love their pets. Veterinary practices provide medical care. The emotional intensity of the client-pet relationship and the clinical nature of the client-vet relationship are mismatched. Hanna needed something that bridged the gap — something that acknowledged the emotional centrality of the pet in the client's life while coming from the veterinary practice. A medical reminder card says "we care about your pet's health." A beautiful portrait says "we understand how much you love this animal."

## The Solution: Hanna's Pet Portrait Program

### Portrait as Client Gift

The program was simple: at every annual checkup, clients received a personalized AI pet portrait — generated by Hanna or her staff using a photo taken during the visit, printed on quality photo paper, and presented in a branded Djurkliniken Söder folder.

The portrait generation process:
1. Staff member takes a photo of the pet during the consultation (the pet is already there, the lighting is good, the animal is usually calm post-examination)
2. Staff member inputs the photo and a style prompt into Lovart: "Regal pet portrait of a [breed], oil painting style, warm lighting, classical composition, museum quality"
3. AI generates the portrait in 15-30 seconds
4. Staff member prints on the clinic's photo printer, inserts into branded folder, presents to client at checkout

Total added time per appointment: 2-3 minutes. Cost per portrait: paper + ink + folder ≈ 8 kronor ($0.75).

### The Social Sharing Engine

The genius of the program was the social mechanism. Each portrait folder included a small card: "Love your pet's portrait? Share it and tag @DjurklinikenSoder — we'd love to see it in your home!" Clients shared enthusiastically — not because the clinic asked them to, but because the portrait was a genuinely delightful object they wanted to show off.

The portraits generated an average of 3-4 social shares per client (Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp status, sometimes all three). Each share reached 150-400 people. With 50-60 portraits distributed per month, the organic reach was 25,000-90,000 impressions — from a clinic that previously reached 500-800 people per month through its own social media.

### Themed Portraits for Seasonal Engagement

Beyond the standard checkup portrait, Hanna introduced themed portrait events:
- **Halloween:** "Spooky pet portraits" — pets rendered in Gothic illustration style against haunted-house backgrounds
- **Christmas:** "Holiday card portraits" — pets in winter scenes, designed for clients to use as actual holiday cards
- **Valentine's Day:** "Pet love stories" — portraits of bonded pairs (dogs and cats who live together) in romantic artistic styles

These themed events generated spikes in social sharing, media interest (a local newspaper covered the Halloween portraits), and new-client inquiries from people who wanted their pets to participate.

## FAQ

### Can AI generate accurate portraits of specific pets, or are they generic?

AI pet portraits using photo input are remarkably specific — they capture breed characteristics, coat patterns, ear shapes, and facial structure. They are recognizably portraits of the specific animal, not generic breed illustrations. The quality depends on the input photo: clear, well-lit, forward-facing photos produce the best results. Hanna's staff take photos during consultations specifically for portrait generation.

### How do I choose the right artistic style for pet portraits?

Match the style to the occasion and the pet's personality. Hanna's standard style is "regal oil painting" — it's universally flattering and suits the "dignified pet" framing. Themed portraits use different styles: "whimsical watercolor" for puppies and kittens, "heroic action painting" for active dogs, "Renaissance chiaroscuro" for elderly pets (the dramatic lighting is surprisingly flattering). The style choice is a creative decision — experiment with 3-4 styles per pet and select the best output.

### What print quality should I use for client-gift pet portraits?

For a gift-quality portrait, print on matte photo paper (not glossy — it looks more like art and less like a snapshot) at a minimum of 200 DPI. Hanna uses a Canon SELPHY photo printer (compact, fast, excellent color reproduction) with 4x6-inch prints in inexpensive frames from IKEA. Total cost per framed portrait: roughly 35 kronor ($3.30). The perceived value far exceeds the production cost — clients treat these as keepsakes, not disposable prints.

### How can veterinary practices use AI pet portraits for client retention?

The portrait-at-checkup model creates an annual ritual — clients book their checkup partly to get the next year's portrait. Hanna's 89% annual-checkup booking rate (vs 71% before the program) demonstrates the retention power of a delightful, low-cost client experience. Additional retention applications: "gotcha day" anniversary portraits, post-surgery recovery portraits ("brave patient" themed), senior-pet milestone portraits. Each is a reason for the client to visit and a memory to associate with the practice.

### Is this program scalable for multi-vet practices or chains?

Yes. Hanna's 4-vet practice generates 50-60 portraits per month with minimal staff time impact (2-3 minutes per appointment). A 10-vet practice could scale to 120-150 portraits per month using the same workflow. The bottleneck is staff training on photo-taking and prompt generation — both trainable in a single 30-minute session. Print costs are the only scaling cost, and they're negligible at volume (bulk paper and ink purchasing reduces per-portrait cost).

### Can pet portraits work for other animal-care businesses?

The model extends to any pet-adjacent business: groomers (before/after portraits — "your dog, freshly groomed and immortalized"), pet stores (purchase-milestone portraits for new pet owners), dog daycares (end-of-day portraits — "here's what your dog looked like having the time of their life"), pet shelters (adoption portraits that make animals more adoptable — already being used by several shelters with measurable adoption-rate improvement). The core principle — a personalized, beautiful, shareable image of a beloved pet — works anywhere the pet-owner relationship exists.

### What's the ethical boundary with AI pet portraits — should they be disclosed as AI-generated?

Hanna's approach: transparency without apology. The portrait folder includes a small note: "Created with AI and love by your friends at Djurkliniken Söder." This acknowledges the production method while keeping the emotional focus on the gift. Clients don't care about the production method — they care that someone created a beautiful image of their pet. The note is about honesty, not explanation. Zero clients have expressed concern about AI involvement; several have asked for the prompt to generate additional portraits at home.

## Consent and Ethics First

Before any generation:

1. Client opt-in form (photo use + portrait delivery)
2. No uploading client photos to random public tools without policy review
3. Soft illustration style for young kids' viewing; avoid uncanny faces
4. Clinic logo/clearspace rules
5. Human veto: front desk reviews before send

## Weekly Portrait Ritual

Monday: collect consented phone photos from visits.
Tuesday: ChatCanvas under clinic Identity Lock (palette, frame, soft light).
Wednesday: Touch Edit whiskers/eyes/text.
Thursday: deliver via email/SMS + print option.
Friday: pick 3 shareable posts (with permission) for Instagram.

300 portraits came from ritual, not a heroic weekend myth every week.

## Failure Modes

1. **Creepy realism** — owners hated almost-photo faces. Fix: illustration lock.
2. **Breed drift** — corgi became generic fluff. Fix: stronger reference + Identity Lock notes.
3. **Consent gaps** — staff posted without asking. Fix: checkbox + Sheets log.
4. **Ink/print disasters** — muddy backgrounds. Fix: print-safe templates.
5. **Promo overclaim** — "most recommended" vanity without tracking. Fix: track referral source codes and Google review velocity, not slogans alone.
