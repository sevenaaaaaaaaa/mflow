## Choosing Your Color Palette

[IMAGE 2 PLACEHOLDER — Conceptual Diagram]

Acupuncture branding must communicate calm, clinical competence, and a connection to tradition without veering into cliché "spa green" or Orientalist tropes.

### Primary Colors
- **#2D6A4F (Jade Green)** — Deep, grounding, and universally associated with healing and renewal across both Eastern and Western traditions. Use as the primary logo color and on clinic signage.
- **#E8D5B7 (Warm Sand)** — The color of bamboo mats and natural linens. Essential as a background: it warms up clinical spaces and photographs beautifully in treatment-room shots.
- **#3D405B (Quiet Indigo)** — A sophisticated near-navy that anchors the palette. Use for text elements, website headers, and appointment-card backgrounds.

### Secondary/Accent Colors
- **#E07A5F (Terracotta Touch)** — A warm, human accent for call-to-action buttons, "Book Now" elements, and seasonal wellness promotions.
- **#F4F1DE (Rice Paper)** — The primary background color for business cards, intake forms, and website pages. Warmer and more intentional than pure white.
- **#81B29A (Sage Light)** — A soft supporting green for secondary elements, illustration backgrounds, and treatment-room wall colors.

## Typography Recommendations

### Logo/Display Font
**Lora** — A warm, readable serif with organic curves that feel handcrafted yet professional. Ideal for clinic names, treatment-menu headers, and window lettering. For a more modern, minimalist approach, **Raleway** offers elegant thin weights.

### Body/Text Font
**Nunito Sans** — A friendly, highly legible sans-serif that handles everything from intake forms to blog posts about meridian theory. Pair with **Crimson Text** for practices that publish longform wellness content.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q1: Should my acupuncture brand avoid red because it's "too intense" for healing?
Not necessarily. Red is a significant color in TCM (fire element, vitality). Use it sparingly — as an accent on appointment confirmations or seasonal promotions — rather than as a primary color.

### Q2: How do I brand a practice that offers both acupuncture and Western modalities?
Use a split-palette approach: your core brand uses neutral, professional colors (#3D405B, #F4F1DE), while modality-specific sub-pages or brochures introduce supporting accent colors (jade for acupuncture, warm blue for physical therapy).

### Q3: What imagery works best for an acupuncture brand kit?
Authentic treatment-room photography (your actual space, your actual hands) outperforms stock imagery by a wide margin. Avoid needles in isolation — show the environment, the linens, the calm lighting. Lovart's photo enhancer can style your iPhone shots into brand-kit quality.

### Q4: Should my intake forms match my brand kit?
Yes — intake forms are often a patient's first physical interaction with your brand. Branded forms with your logo, palette, and typography signal professionalism before treatment begins. Lovart generates form templates from your brand kit.

### Q5: How important is website color for an acupuncturist?
Critical. 78% of new acupuncture patients find their practitioner via Google search (NCBI patient-behavior study, 2024). A website using your brand kit's palette with proper contrast ratios (WCAG 2.1 AA minimum) keeps potential patients on the page long enough to book.
