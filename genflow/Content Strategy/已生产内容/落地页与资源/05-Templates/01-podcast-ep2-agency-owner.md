# Podcast #2: Building an AI-Native Creative Agency — An Interview with Studio Synth Founder

## Episode Metadata

- **Episode Number**: 2
- **Duration**: 42 minutes
- **Host**: Maya Chen, Head of Community at Lovart
- **Guest**: Jordan Reeves, Founder and Creative Director, Studio Synth

## Show Notes

In the second episode of the Lovart Design Podcast, Maya Chen interviews Jordan Reeves, who in 2026 founded Studio Synth, arguably the first creative agency built from the ground up on an AI-native workflow. Unlike traditional agencies that added AI tools to existing processes, Studio Synth designed every role, every client deliverable, and every pricing model around Lovart's capabilities. The result: a five-person agency that competes with fifty-person shops on output while maintaining the margins of a SaaS company.

## Episode Structure

### 0:00 — 6:00 | The Origin Story

Jordan describes the moment that led to Studio Synth. In late 2025, he was Creative Director at a seventy-person agency where he was spending sixty percent of his time on production management rather than creative direction. A client needed two hundred localized ad variants in two weeks. The production team projected a three-week timeline. Jordan ran a test batch in Lovart over a weekend and delivered all two hundred variants by Monday morning.

"That Monday was the moment I realized the agency model was about to be unbundled," Jordan says. "Not by AI replacing creativity, but by AI eliminating the production tax that makes agencies need so many people."

He left the agency in January 2026 and founded Studio Synth the following month with two collaborators: a brand strategist and a motion designer. The founding thesis: a lean team armed with Lovart could deliver agency-quality work at speed and price points that traditional agencies could not match.

### 6:00 — 16:00 | The Studio Model

Studio Synth operates on three principles that diverge from traditional agency practice. First, they charge by project, not by hour. This aligns incentives around speed and quality rather than billable time. Jordan explains: "When you charge by the hour, efficiency is punished. When you charge by the project, efficiency is rewarded. AI makes efficiency possible at a scale that was previously unimaginable."

Second, they standardize on Lovart's Brand Kit for every client engagement. There is no "let me mock that up in Illustrator while the brand guidelines are in a PDF somewhere." Every client's brand lives in a Lovart workspace, and every team member works from the same source of truth. This eliminates the version-control chaos that Jordan estimates consumed fifteen percent of his previous agency's billable hours.

Third, they practice radical transparency with clients about AI usage. Their proposals explicitly state which deliverables are AI-generated, which are AI-assisted, and which are fully human-crafted. "Clients appreciate the honesty," Jordan says. "And it usually leads to a deeper conversation about what they actually value. Spoiler: it is rarely the production work they thought they were paying for."

### 16:00 — 26:00 | Economics and Margins

This segment is the most detailed public breakdown of an AI-native agency's economics that has been released. Jordan shares actual numbers, anonymized by client.

Studio Synth's average project size is eighteen thousand dollars, with a range from five thousand for a basic brand identity to sixty thousand for a full campaign suite. Their gross margin is seventy-two percent, compared to an industry average of thirty to forty percent for traditional agencies of comparable project size. The difference comes from lower headcount, near-zero tooling costs because Lovart replaces a suite of Adobe licenses that would cost thousands per seat monthly, and dramatically reduced revision cycles because Lovart's real-time collaboration eliminates the back-and-forth email chains that bloat project timelines.

The five-person team serves twelve active clients, a ratio that would require at least twenty people in a traditional agency. Jordan explains the math: "In a traditional agency, a creative team of five might handle three or four active clients because each client requires production designers, QA, version control management. In our model, Lovart handles production. Our five people handle strategy, creative direction, and client relationships. The ratio flips from twenty percent strategic work to eighty percent strategic work."

### 26:00 — 36:00 | The Talent Question

Maya asks the question many listeners will be thinking: "If you only need five people, are you part of the trend that is eliminating creative jobs?"

Jordan's answer is nuanced. "The production designer role as it existed in 2020 is largely gone. I do not think that is reversible. But we have hired two people in the last six months for roles that did not exist at my previous agency: an AI Workflow Designer who builds and maintains our Lovart Custom Skills, and a Creative Quality Director whose entire job is reviewing AI outputs and ensuring they meet our standards before they reach clients."

He argues that the creative industry is not shrinking but transforming. "The number of brands that need design work is exploding. DTC brands, creator businesses, Web3 projects, local businesses that could never afford agency rates before. AI makes it possible to serve them profitably. The total volume of design work in the economy is growing. It is just the composition of that work that is changing."

Jordan also shares his hiring philosophy. Studio Synth looks for three traits that he believes are AI-resistant: taste, which he defines as the ability to distinguish good from great across a wide range of aesthetics; communication, the ability to translate between what a client says and what a client means; and systems thinking, the ability to design workflows and automations rather than just individual assets.

### 36:00 — 40:00 | Advice for Aspiring AI-Native Founders

Jordan offers five pieces of advice for designers or strategists considering an AI-native agency. First, start now. The window for building a differentiated AI-native brand is open but closing. Second, invest deeply in learning one platform rather than dabbling in ten. Studio Synth standardized on Lovart and built competitive advantage on depth, not breadth. Third, price on value, not cost. Do not discount just because your costs are lower. Fourth, build Custom Skills obsessively. Over time, your skill library becomes a proprietary asset that competitors cannot replicate quickly. Fifth, over-communicate your AI usage. Transparency builds trust, and trust builds long-term client relationships.

### 40:00 — 42:00 | Closing

Maya teases episode three, which will explore the ethical and legal dimensions of AI-generated art with a focus on fair use, copyright, and commercial applications, a topic that has become increasingly urgent as AI-generated content populates more of the commercial design landscape.
