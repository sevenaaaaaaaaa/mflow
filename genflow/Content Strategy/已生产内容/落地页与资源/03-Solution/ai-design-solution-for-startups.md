## The Startup Design Gap

Early-stage startups face a unique design challenge: they need the visual output of a funded company on the budget of an unfunded one. The design needs are real and urgent:

**Fundraising.** A pitch deck is the most important design document a startup ever creates. It is read by investors who see hundreds of decks per year and form judgments in the first 3–5 slides. A poorly designed deck communicates amateurism. A well-designed one communicates attention to detail — and investors invest in attention to detail. Yet seed-stage pitch decks are almost always founder-designed, because professional deck design costs $3,000–$8,000.

**Launch.** A startup launch — whether on Product Hunt, TechCrunch, or a landing page with an email capture — requires visual assets: logo, hero image, product screenshots, social media launch graphics, email announcement design, and a website that does not look like a default template. Founders typically cobble these together from Fiverr gigs and Canva templates, producing an inconsistent visual identity that undermines the launch narrative.

**Growth.** As a startup moves from launch to early growth, visual needs multiply: blog post headers, case study PDFs, sales one-pagers, webinar graphics, job postings, culture content for talent branding, and ongoing social media presence. Every channel demands visual content. Every visual is a brand touchpoint. Without a design system, consistency degrades with every new asset produced.
