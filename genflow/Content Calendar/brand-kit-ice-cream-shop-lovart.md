## Choosing Your Color Palette

[IMAGE 2 PLACEHOLDER — Conceptual Diagram]

Ice cream branding thrives on nostalgia, whimsy, and appetite appeal. Pastels dominate, but the best shops anchor sweetness with unexpected structure.

### Primary Colors
- **#FFB5A7 (Peach Sorbet)** — Warm, welcoming, and universally flattering in photos. Use as a wall color, cup liner, or primary brand background.
- **#A8DADC (Pistachio Mint)** — A cool, creamy pastel that balances peach and pink tones. Essential for ice-cream branding because it photographs as clean and refreshing.
- **#F72585 (Bubblegum Pop)** — For shops targeting Gen Z and families: an unapologetic pink that commands attention on delivery apps and social feeds.

### Secondary/Accent Colors
- **#FFD166 (Lemon Curd)** — Warm, cheerful accent for "Today's Flavors" boards, loyalty stamps, and sidewalk signage.
- **#457B9D (Blueberry Scoop)** — A grounded mid-tone blue. Use for text on pastel backgrounds when you need readability without sacrificing whimsy.
- **#F1FAEE (Vanilla Bean)** — Clean, warm white for menu backgrounds and cup exteriors. Avoids the sterile feel of pure #FFFFFF.

## Typography Recommendations

### Logo/Display Font
**Fredoka One** (Google Fonts) — Rounded, bouncy, and built for happiness. Reads like a sugar rush in type form. Perfect for shop names, flavor boards, and window decals. For vintage-parlor concepts, **Lobster Two** adds a retro-script soda-fountain feel.

### Body/Text Font
**Quicksand** — Rounded sans-serif with a friendly personality that doesn't sacrifice readability at small sizes. Pair with **Nunito** for allergen and ingredient lists that feel warm rather than clinical.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q1: What's the biggest branding mistake ice cream shops make?
Using only pastels without a dark grounding color. Pastel-on-pastel becomes illegible and washes out in photos. Always include one deep color (#457B9D, #3D405B) for text and structural elements.

### Q2: Should my brand kit change seasonally?
Yes — build a core brand kit with a seasonal accent palette add-on. Summer gets brighter, winter gets cozier (deep burgundy, cream, gold). Lovart's template system supports seasonal toggles without rebuilding your master kit.

### Q3: How do I brand pint containers for retail shelves?
Supermarket freezers are visual chaos. Your pint needs high contrast (dark background + bright logo) and a distinct top-down shape recognition pattern. Lovart's 3D mockup preview simulates shelf context so you can test before printing.

### Q4: Can a single brand kit work for both a physical shop and an online merch store?
Yes — with one exception: digital-only assets need higher saturation (RGB-native colors) than print assets. Lovart exports both color spaces from the same master palette, so your hoodie design matches your wall color.

### Q5: What photography style best complements an ice cream brand kit?
Flat-lay scoop shots with visible texture (melting edges, mix-in chunks) outperform overly-styled "perfect" scoops. Authentic imperfection triggers craving; clinical perfection reads as stock photography.

### Q6: How do I brand for franchise or multi-location expansion?
Build a master brand kit with immutable core elements (logo, primary palette, type pair) and a flexible module (location-specific illustration, seasonal accent palette). Lovart's locking feature prevents franchisees from breaking the brand while giving creative room.
