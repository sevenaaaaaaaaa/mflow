## Cost, credits, and the real budget of a weekly campaign

The demo economy hides the bill. A free tier shows you a pretty clip and calls it "accessible." Then Thursday arrives and the export is 720p, the series needs nine regenerations, and the operator hours eat the savings. Let me put real numbers on the paired workflow so you can decide with eyes open.

**The Hailuo-only path.** Suppose a retail brand runs four promo clips a week. Each clip needs an offer plate that Hailuo bakes wrong, so each takes ~9 regenerations to get the date right. At, say, 30 seconds of GPU per generation and an operator watching each, that's 36 generations × 30s = 18 minutes of pure generation, plus the operator's attention the whole time because each attempt needs a human judgment. Nine of those clips miss the date and get regenerated next week. The approved-asset count is four; the generation count is ~36. Cost per approved asset is high not because the tool is expensive per clip, but because the editable exit ramp is missing.

**The paired path.** Same four promos. Build the offer plate once in Lovart ChatCanvas with Brand Kit locked — reusable across the month. Generate the food still in Lovart at 8K. Animate each in Hailuo with "no text" — one or two attempts because the motion is the only variable. Fix any copy in Touch Edit, not in Hailuo. Generation count drops to ~8. Operator attention drops because the type is already correct. Cost per approved asset collapses.

The variable that dominates is not the model's price. It's whether a typo costs you a regeneration or a text edit. Lovart's Touch Edit is what flips that variable. If you're weighing "should I pay for Lovart," weigh it against the regenerations you're not doing.

**Free-tier reality.** Both tools have free access. Hailuo's free tier caps export resolution and queues renders — fine for learning, fatal for a client final due Friday. Lovart's free tier gives daily credits you can use to build the still and do the Touch Edit pass. My rule: learn on free tiers, but never ship a client final from a capped export. Generate the hero frame in Lovart at full 8K (PNG/JPG/SVG/PSD) and animate a 1080p motion layer in Hailuo — the still carries the resolution, the motion carries the life.

## A note on honesty: what I'm not claiming

Reviews that sell a tool tell you what it does and skip what it doesn't. I'd rather name the boundaries so your team doesn't discover them at 6 p.m. on deadline day.

I am not claiming Lovart generates video. It doesn't. Hailuo does the motion; Lovart does the frame, the type, and the brand. If you need a talking-head video with no design layer, Hailuo alone is the simpler choice.

I am not claiming this pairing replaces a video editor for long-form. Both tools are sub-10-second. A 3-minute explainer is a different job — assemble short clips in an editor; use Lovart + Hailuo for the shot-level assets.

I am not claiming Brand Kit magically fixes a bad brief. It locks the hex you give it. If your brief is vague, Brand Kit will consistently produce the wrong color, faster. The desk amplifies your discipline; it doesn't manufacture it.

I am not claiming Hailuo's motion is perfect. It holds shape better than most, but complex multi-subject scenes still drift, and fine text in the frame (a label, a sign) can invent content. Proof the frame before you animate it. The failures above are reproducible, not unlucky.

## How this fits a small team's actual week

May's team is three people: her, a part-time designer, a freelancer. They don't have a render farm or a video lead. Here's the Monday-to-Thursday they now run, because it's the only version that fits their headcount.

Monday — May writes the briefs in one sentence each in ChatCanvas. The designer locks Brand Kit. They generate the hero stills in Lovart at 8K, type correct, crops designed.

Tuesday — They animate the stills in Hailuo (no text, motion only). The designer proofs at 390px. Any copy fix goes to Touch Edit.

Wednesday — They export 4:5, 9:16, 16:9 from the Lovart source. The freelancer drops them into the scheduled posts. No re-prompting Hailuo.

Thursday — The asset ships. May records a voice note: "the lunch promo is live, offer's right, logo's on-brand." The hallway test passes.

Contrast with the Hailuo-only week they ran before: by Wednesday they had nine broken clips, a typo'd date, and a decapitated bottle, and Thursday's post went out late with the wrong hex. The tool didn't change. The workflow did. That's the entire difference, and it's the only part a review should help you with.

## The verdict, restated without the hype

Hailuo AI is the best sub-10-second motion model I tested this quarter for coherent object rotation and intentional camera. For a single hero clip with no text and no brand series, use it and stop there — it's genuinely good at exactly that.

For a weekly campaign where the offer must land, the brand must hold, and twelve formats must ship before Thursday, Hailuo alone will burn your date. Pair it with a design desk that owns the type and the brand, and the job becomes shippable. The model that animates is not the tool that ships. Name which half you're buying, and build the other half on purpose.

If you take one sentence from this review: keep your text in Lovart and your motion in Hailuo. The moment you ask the model to render the offer, you've already lost the exit ramp.
