Your pricing hasn't changed — your presentation has.

The auto-layout ensures no orphaned prices, no misaligned columns, and consistent spacing between all elements.

## Step 2: Apply Pricing Psychology to Your Layout

How you present prices matters as much as the prices themselves. Lovart's **Pricing Psychology Toolkit** (View → Pricing Strategies) applies research-backed formatting: **Decoy Pricing** — placing a premium option near your target-priced option makes the target seem like a better value (e.g., "Deluxe Cut & Style $95" next to "Signature Cut & Style $65" makes $65 feel reasonable). **Price Anchoring** — list your highest-priced service first in each category to anchor expectations high, making subsequent prices feel accessible. **Remove Currency Symbols** — "$" signs trigger pain-of-paying psychology; Lovart formats prices as plain numbers (e.g., "65" not "$65") with a small "USD" label in the footer. **Avoid Trailing Price Columns** — align prices immediately after descriptions (not in a right-justified column) to discourage price-shopping behavior and encourage reading the value description first.

## Step 3: Write Service Descriptions That Sell Value

A price list that says "Haircut — $45" sells a commodity. A price list that says "Classic Cut — Precision scissor work tailored to your face shape, includes shampoo, conditioning treatment, and hot towel finish — 45" sells an experience. Use Lovart's **AI Service Description** tool: for each service, enter the service name, what's included, and the target client. The AI generates a 10–20 word description that emphasizes outcome and experience over process. For "Balayage," it might generate: "Hand-painted highlights for a natural, sun-kissed gradient that grows out seamlessly. Includes toner and blowout." Always review AI descriptions for accuracy — if your balayage doesn't include a blowout, edit the description before finalizing.

## Step 4: Design for the Environment Where It Will Be Read

A price list on a reception counter is read from 18–24 inches away, often by someone standing and holding a purse. A price list in a window is read from 3–5 feet. A digital price list on a phone screen is read from 12 inches. Lovart's **Viewing Distance Simulator** adjusts your canvas to simulate legibility at any distance. For counter displays: minimum 11pt body text, with service names at 16pt bold. For window displays: minimum 24pt body, 36pt headers. For digital: minimum 14pt body (mobile screens demand larger type than print). The simulator also accounts for lighting — a backlit digital screen has higher effective contrast than a matte printed page, so Lovart adjusts text-weight recommendations accordingly.

## FAQ

**Q: Should I include or hide prices for premium services?**
A: Display them. "Price Upon Request" increases decision friction and reduces inquiries by 40% compared to transparent pricing (Harvard Business Review, 2024). Use the price anchoring strategy instead: display the premium price, framed next to the value it delivers.

**Q: How often should I update my price list design?**
A: Update prices whenever they change. Redesign the layout when your service menu structure changes (adding/removing categories) or when your branding updates. For price-only changes, Lovart's editable source file lets you update numbers in minutes without layout disruption.

**Q: Can Lovart handle price lists with 50+ items across multiple pages?**
A: Yes. For large catalogs, Lovart generates multi-page layouts with consistent headers, page numbers, and a table of contents on page one. Use the **Master Page** feature (Pro plan) to lock headers and footers across all pages.

**Q: What's the ideal number of price tiers for a service?**
A: Three tiers (Good, Better, Best) is the "Goldilocks" pricing model supported by behavioral economics research. Fewer than 3 offers insufficient choice; more than 3 triggers analysis paralysis. Lovart's Pricing Psychology Toolkit auto-suggests tier structures.

**Q: Can I add before/after photos to my price list?**
A: Yes, for digital price lists. Import before/after photos as image thumbnails next to relevant services. For print, photos increase ink costs significantly — use them sparingly on a separate look-book rather than the price list itself.

**Q: Does Lovart support multi-currency or multi-location price lists?**
A: On the $99 Agency plan, create a master price list and generate location-specific variants with different currencies and pricing. The structured data CSV includes a currency field. Lovart auto-converts and rounds prices based on current exchange rates (manual review recommended).
