## Portrait Styles You Can Generate

### Studio Headshot
Clean background, balanced lighting, natural expression. These rival what you'd get from a professional headshot session costing $200–$500. Perfect for LinkedIn, company about pages, conference speaker profiles, and professional directories.

Lovart's Studio mode lets you specify background color (white, grey, blue gradient), lighting style (Rembrandt, butterfly, loop, split), and expression range (neutral, confident smile, approachable warmth). Fotor offers headshots too, but the lighting options are limited to "bright" or "soft" — no named lighting patterns.

### Cinematic Portrait
Dramatic, storytelling lighting with depth. Think movie poster, editorial magazine spread, or album cover. Cinematic portraits leverage directional light, color grading (teal-orange, monochrome, golden hour), and shallow depth of field to create images with narrative weight.

### Anime / Character Portrait
Transform a real face into an anime-style character that still retains recognizable features. Use it for gaming avatars, social media personas, or character concept art. Leonardo does this well in isolation, but Lovart then lets you place that portrait directly into a design layout — a Twitch banner, a YouTube thumbnail, a Discord server icon.

### Oil Painting Portrait
Classical composition with visible brushstrokes, warm undertones, and a gallery feel. These portraits look like heirloom pieces. Ideal for gifting, premium branding, or personal art collections.

### Digital Illustration
Clean vector-like rendering with bold colors and precise lines. Works well for modern brand imagery, app onboarding illustrations, and editorial spot art.

### Sketch / Pencil Portrait
Arresting, expressive, monochrome. Use it for book author pages, minimalist brand identities, or artistic social media content.

## How It Works

1. **Upload reference photos** — 5 to 10 clear photos of the subject, showing varied angles and expressions. More references = more accurate likeness.
2. **Choose your style** — Studio, cinematic, anime, oil painting, digital illustration, or sketch.
3. **Specify details** — Background, clothing description, expression, lighting.
4. **Generate** — Lovart's MCoT engine creates 4 variations for you to choose from.
5. **Refine with Touch Edit** — Adjust any detail you want changed. Eye shape slightly off? Fix it. Lighting too harsh on one side? Soften it. Background color not quite right? Shift the hue.
6. **Export** — PNG, JPG, or SVG. Standard headshot aspect ratios (1:1, 3:4, 4:5) pre-configured.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: How many reference photos should I upload for best results?**
A: 5 to 10 photos works best. Include front-facing, profile, and three-quarter angles. Vary expressions and lighting conditions. Avoid group photos, extreme makeup, or heavy filters.

**Q: Will the AI portrait actually look like me?**
A: With good reference photos, yes — to a degree that will surprise you. The AI captures facial structure, proportions, and key features. It's not a photograph — it's a generated likeness — but for professional and artistic use, the accuracy is more than sufficient.

**Q: Can I generate portraits of celebrities or fictional characters?**
A: For personal creative use, yes — describe the character. For commercial use involving real people, you need appropriate rights and permissions.
