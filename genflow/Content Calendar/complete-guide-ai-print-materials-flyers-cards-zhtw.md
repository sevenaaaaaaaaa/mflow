## Hook: The Flyer in the Trash

[IMAGE 2 PLACEHOLDER — Conceptual Diagram]

Print marketing has a brutal conversion funnel. Your flyer has to survive: the mailbox-to-recycling-bin journey (2 seconds of attention), the countertop-to-decision moment (3 more seconds), and the "is this worth keeping?" judgment (1 second). Total: 6 seconds to communicate value.

Most small business print materials fail not because the business is bad, but because the design signals "amateur" in under a second. Off-center logos. Comic Sans. Pixelated images. The visual equivalent of a limp handshake.

AI print materials design solves the technical execution — professional layouts, print-ready exports, consistent branding. The creative direction (what you say, who you say it to, why they should care) still requires human judgment. This guide covers both sides of the equation.

## Questions Nobody Answers

### What print materials can AI design?

Lovart Print Studio covers the full print collateral spectrum:
- **Business cards:** Standard (3.5×2"), square, rounded corners, folded
- **Flyers:** A5 (half-page), A4, DL (1/3 A4), US Letter
- **Brochures:** Bi-fold, tri-fold (C-fold and Z-fold), gate-fold
- **Menus:** Single-page, bi-fold, tri-fold, booklet
- **Letterheads and envelopes:** #10 envelope, A4/US Letter letterhead
- **Postcards and invitations:** Standard postcard, oversized, event-specific
- **Stickers and labels:** Die-cut shapes, roll labels, sheet labels
- **Signage:** Yard signs, window decals, banner stands, A-frames

### How does AI handle multi-page documents like brochures?

Multi-page print design requires panel-aware layout — understanding which content goes where in the folded format. A tri-fold brochure has 6 panels (3 front, 3 back) with specific content logic: cover → inside flap → inside spread → inside spread continued → back flap → back cover.

Lovart's Brochure mode understands panel logic and generates appropriate content placement for each panel. For best results:
- Define content for each panel (the AI won't know your business well enough to write panel-specific copy)
- Review fold order — AI sometimes confuses C-fold and Z-fold panel sequencing
- Check margins on folded panels (inner panels need different margins than cover panels)

### What print specifications does AI handle?

Essential print specs handled automatically:
- **Bleed:** 0.125" (3mm) standard, configurable
- **Safe zone:** 0.25" (6mm) from trim
- **Resolution:** 300 DPI minimum enforced
- **Color space:** CMYK with profile-specific conversion
- **Rich black:** 60/40/40/100 for deep blacks
- **Font handling:** Outlined or embedded
- **Overprint/Trapping:** Basic settings for commercial print

Specs NOT handled automatically (must verify): spot color/Pantone matching, specialty finishes (foil, emboss, spot UV) file preparation, and printer-specific imposition requirements.

### How does AI compare to templates (Canva, Vistaprint, Moo)?

Templates are pre-designed layouts where you swap in your content. They're fast, safe, and boring. Every other business card from Canva looks like your business card.

AI design generates original layouts based on your brand, industry, and preferences. You get unique designs without manual layout work. The trade-off: AI designs require more review (the AI may make unconventional choices) but produce more distinctive results.

Hybrid: use AI for original concepts, then refine using template-like precision tools for the final polish.

### What's the most common print mistake AI doesn't prevent?

**Font size on business cards.** AI generates visually balanced layouts on screen that become unreadable when printed at actual size. A 6pt font looks fine on a 27-inch monitor at 200% zoom. It's microscopic on a 3.5-inch card.

Rule: nothing below 8pt on business cards, 9pt for older demographics, 10pt for body text on flyers. Enforce this manually — AI doesn't understand real-world readability.

Other common issues: insufficient contrast for text on backgrounds (looks fine backlit on screen, illegible on paper), borders too close to trim (inevitable cutting variation slices them unevenly), and QR codes too small to scan (minimum 0.8×0.8 inches at typical print resolution).

### Can AI design restaurant menus?

Yes, and it's one of the strongest applications. AI menu design handles:
- Section organization (appetizers, mains, desserts, beverages)
- Price alignment and formatting
- Food photography integration (AI-generated or uploaded)
- Atmosphere-appropriate design (fine dining vs. casual vs. fast-casual)
- Daily specials insert layouts
- Dietary iconography (gluten-free, vegan, spicy indicators)

Restaurant menus benefit from AI design because they combine structured information (menu items + prices) with aesthetic requirements that vary dramatically by restaurant type. Lovart's Menu mode includes restaurant-type presets that generate style-appropriate layouts.

### Can I edit AI-generated print designs after export?

If you export as an editable file: yes. Lovart exports as:
- **PDF (editable):** Open in Illustrator or Affinity Designer to modify every element
- **PDF/X-4 (print-ready):** Flattened, not easily edited — for print submission only
- **PNG/JPG:** Rasterized preview, not for editing

For maximum flexibility, export the editable PDF and finalize in a vector editing tool. For speed, export print-ready PDF directly and skip manual editing.

### What about accessibility and inclusive design?

AI doesn't inherently consider accessibility. Manual checks required:
- Color contrast ratios (WCAG AA minimum: 4.5:1 for normal text)
- Font sizes for visually impaired readers
- Clear information hierarchy for cognitive accessibility
- QR code alternatives for non-smartphone users
- Language localization when designing for multilingual audiences

AI design plus human accessibility review is the responsible workflow.

## What Most Guides Won't Tell You

[IMAGE 3 PLACEHOLDER — Real UI Screenshot]

**Paper choice transforms AI design.** An AI design printed on cheap 80# text stock looks cheap. The same design on 100# cover with soft-touch laminate looks premium. AI designs the graphics. You choose the substrate. The substrate matters more than the AI.

**QR codes are overused and under-tested.** Every AI print template includes a QR code. Most QR codes on print materials go to homepages that don't match the offer on the flyer. If you include a QR code, create a dedicated landing page. Test the QR code with multiple phone models before printing 5,000 flyers.

**Print is a tactile medium.** AI designs in 2D. Print exists in 3D — paper weight, texture, finish, and folding all contribute to the experience. Specify these elements in your AI prompt ("on textured uncoated stock, letterpress effect") for more realistic previews, but always get physical paper samples before committing.
