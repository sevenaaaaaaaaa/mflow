## Phase 1: Client Onboarding (Week 0)

The first interaction with a new client sets the tone for the entire engagement. A slow, disorganized onboarding signals that your process is ad hoc. A smooth one signals professionalism.

### The Onboarding Sequence

**Step 1: Discovery Call → Brand Brief (30 min)**
During your discovery call, you're asking strategic questions: What's the brand voice? Who's the audience? What are the business goals behind this design work? Take notes — these become your ChatCanvas prompts later.

**Step 2: Brand Kit Setup (15 min)**
Immediately after the call, create a new Lovart workspace for this client. Upload their logo. Point Lovart at their website to auto-extract colors and fonts. Upload any existing design materials they've shared. You now have a complete, AI-ready brand profile.

**Step 3: Onboarding Deliverable — A Welcome Graphic (5 min)**
This is a small touch that makes a big impression. Generate a branded "Excited to work together!" graphic with their logo and colors using ChatCanvas. Send it in your follow-up email. It demonstrates that you're already operational and that you understand their brand. Clients remember this.

**Step 4: Scope Document with Visual References (20 min)**
Use ChatCanvas to generate a scoped proposal. Prompt:

> "Create a scope of work overview for a [project type] with [Client Name]. Include sections: Project Summary, Deliverables, Timeline, Revision Policy, Investment. Use their brand colors. Format as a clean, modern PDF-ready layout."

You're not sending this as the contract — you're sending it alongside the contract. It frames the engagement visually and sets expectations for the quality of work to come.

## Phase 2: Concept Generation (Week 1)

This is where Lovart's MCoT (Multi-step Chain of Thought) engine transforms your workflow.

### The Old Way
1. Create a moodboard (1–2 hours)
2. Sketch concepts (1–2 hours)
3. Build initial designs in design software (3–5 hours)
4. Realize the third concept is the keeper, scrap the first two
5. Total: 6–10 hours for 3 initial concepts

### The Lovart Way
1. Write a detailed creative prompt in ChatCanvas (10 minutes)
2. Generate 5–8 concept directions simultaneously (2 minutes)
3. Identify the 2–3 strongest directions (5 minutes)
4. Refine the top concepts with Touch Edit (30–60 minutes)
5. Total: 1–1.5 hours for 3 polished concepts

The creative prompt is the engine. Here's what a strong concept-generation prompt looks like:

> "Generate 5 concept directions for a [project type] for [Client Name]. Brand context: [industry], [audience], [tone]. Style directions: 1) Minimal and editorial, 2) Bold and colorful, 3) Organic and textured, 4) Tech-forward and sleek, 5) Warm and human. Use their brand palette. Show each concept as a single hero composition. Include the concept name and a one-line rationale at the bottom of each."

The key word is "directions" — not "final designs." You're using AI to explore the creative landscape rapidly, not to skip the creative thinking. Your judgment selects the winners. Your skill refines them.

### Presenting Concepts to Clients

Don't dump 8 AI-generated concepts on a client. Curate to your top 2–3. For each, provide:
- The concept (generated + refined by you)
- A 2–3 sentence rationale connecting the design to their strategy
- One variation showing how the concept extends to a second format (e.g., if it's a logo, show it on a business card)

This frames you as a strategic thinker, not an AI operator.

## Phase 3: Production & Revisions (Weeks 2–3)

[IMAGE 3 PLACEHOLDER — Real UI Screenshot]

This is where freelancers traditionally lose margin. Production and revisions are the least billable, most time-consuming phase of any project. Lovart compresses this phase dramatically.

### The Revision Loop — Rebuilt

**Old revision loop:**
1. Client emails feedback ("Can we try a different font? And maybe blue instead of green? And can you move the logo?")
2. You open the source file, make changes manually (20–40 min)
3. You export a new PNG, attach to email, send
4. Client replies: "Actually, can we go back to the green but keep the new font?" (another 20 min)
5. This repeats 2–4 times
6. Total: 1–3 hours of non-creative production work

**Lovart revision loop:**
1. Client leaves comments on the shared preview link
2. You implement changes in Touch Edit or regenerate specific elements via ChatCanvas (5–10 min per round)
3. Client sees updates in real-time
4. Total: 15–30 minutes for a full revision cycle

The difference compounds. Over 5 clients per month with 2 revision rounds each, the old way burns 10–30 hours on revisions alone. The Lovart way: 2.5–5 hours.

### Multi-Format Production

Most freelance projects require a primary deliverable + format variants. A brand identity needs logo + business card + social avatar + email signature + favicon. A social campaign needs feed + story + reel cover + link-in-bio graphic.

The `@commands resize` command handles this in one action:

> "@commands resize --source "hero-design" --targets instagram-story, instagram-feed, facebook-post, linkedin-post, twitter-post, email-header, web-banner"

Lovart intelligently reflows elements for each format. For aspect ratios that require compositional changes (e.g., square → vertical), it adjusts layout to maintain visual hierarchy. You review and touch-edit any format that needs refinement.

What used to be 2–3 hours of manual resizing is now a 5-minute command + 10-minute review.

## Phase 4: Final Delivery (Week 3–4)

The finish line is where freelancers often stumble — disorganized file delivery, missing formats, unclear usage rights. Lovart's export system eliminates the friction.

### The Delivery Package

For every project, export a standardized delivery folder:

1. **Final Designs** — All approved assets in PNG, JPG, SVG (for vectors), and source formats
2. **Brand Guide Snapshot** — A one-page visual summary of colors, fonts, and logo usage (generated in ChatCanvas)
3. **Usage Notes** — Template guide for the client showing how to use delivered files (generated as a simple PDF layout)
4. **Social Media Kit** — Pre-sized versions for every platform they'll use

All exported simultaneously with a single command:

> "@commands export --project "Client-Project-Name" --formats png,jpg,svg,pdf --quality print --naming "ClientName_AssetType_v1""

The naming convention is consistent. The folder structure is clean. No "final_final_v3_USE_THIS_ONE.png" chaos.

## The Weekly Rhythm

This rhythm produces 25–35 hours of focused work per week — down from the 50–60 hours many freelancers report — with higher output and better creative quality.
