# A Step-by-Step Guide to Create a Brochure Without Photoshop — Print-Ready in 5 Steps

You are at a trade show in three weeks. The booth is booked. The banner stand shipped yesterday. But the brochure — the thing people will actually pick up, flip through, and take home — does not exist yet. You opened InDesign once in 2019 and lasted seven minutes before closing it in frustration. A freelance designer quoted you $1,200 for a trifold with a two-week turnaround. The math does not work.

A brochure is not a flyer. It has multiple panels, a logical reading flow, and a physical form factor that constrains design decisions in ways a social media post never does. But the principles are learnable, and with an AI design agent handling the heavy visual lifting, you can design, iterate, and export a print-ready brochure in under an hour. ## Step 1 — Choose Your Format and Define the Reading Flow

[IMAGE 2 PLACEHOLDER — Conceptual Diagram]

Brochures come in standard formats. Choose one before you design anything:

**Tri-Fold (most common)**: An 8.5x11 or A4 sheet folded twice, creating six panels (three on each side). The outside panels function as the cover (front, back, and a flap). The inside panels contain the main content. This is the classic format for service businesses, real estate listings, and event programs.

**Bi-Fold**: A single fold creates four panels — front cover, back cover, and two inside pages. Feels more premium; works well for menus, programs, and corporate overviews.

**Z-Fold**: Alternating fold directions create a zigzag shape. Six panels that unfold in sequence. Good for step-by-step guides, timelines, and storytelling narratives.

**Gate-Fold**: Two side panels fold inward to meet in the center, opening like double doors. High impact, higher cost. Used for luxury products and premium invitations.

For this guide, we will focus on the tri-fold — it is the format 80% of businesses need. A4 finished size (210x297mm flat, 99x210mm folded).

Sketch the panel layout on a piece of paper before prompting the agent:

- **Front cover (panel 1)**: Company name, headline, hero image, one key benefit or offer.
- **Inside left (panel 2)**: Company story, team intro, or overview of services.
- **Inside center (panel 3)**: Main content — services, products, features in detail.
- **Inside right (panel 4)**: Pricing, packages, or process steps.
- **Back cover (panel 5)**: Contact info, social handles, QR code, call to action.
- **Flap (panel 6)**: Often a teaser, testimonial, or calendar of events — the panel visible when the brochure is first picked up.

The order matters because panels 1, 6, and the back cover are on one side of the paper; panels 2, 3, and 4 are on the other. When the brochure is folded, panel 1 is the front, panel 6 is the visible flap, and the back cover is the back. The reading experience flows from the flap (panel 6), to the inside spread (panels 2+3+4), to the back cover. Map this flow before you design.

## Step 2 — Gather and Organize Your Content

Brochures are content-first designs. The layout serves the words, not the other way around. Write out every piece of text before you prompt the agent:

- Headline (front cover) — 8 words max.
- Subhead — one sentence, 15 words max.
- Body sections — 3-5 sections, each 50-100 words.
- Bullet points — for features, services, or benefits.
- Testimonial quote — 1-2 sentences with attribution.
- Contact block — address, phone, email, website, social handles.
- Call to action — what should the reader do next?

Gather your images too — product photos, team headshots, location photos, logo files. The agent can generate some imagery, but your real photos of your real business add authenticity that generated images cannot match.

## Step 3 — Prompt the Agent with Full Panel Instructions

Your prompt needs to describe every panel in sequence so the agent can compose the full layout:

> "A4 tri-fold brochure, 6 panels. Professional, clean, modern design for a financial advisory firm.
>
> **Panel 1 — Front Cover**: Company logo (uploaded) centered at top. Hero image: a professional in a modern office, natural light, approachable. Headline: 'Your Financial Future, Clarified.' Subhead: 'Fee-only fiduciary advice for families and small business owners.' Brand colors: navy (#1B2A4A), gold (#C9A84C), white.
>
> **Panel 6 — Flap (connect to Panel 1)**: A short summary of services with three icon-style graphics — Investment Management, Retirement Planning, Tax Strategy. Each with a one-line description.
>
> **Back Cover (Panel 1 side, adjacent to Panel 6)**: Contact information: address, phone, email, website. A QR code for scheduling a consultation. Small logo bottom-right.
>
> **Panels 2-4 — Inside Spread**: Panel 2: 'Our Approach' — a short narrative about the firm's philosophy. Panel 3: 'Services' — three columns or blocks for each service with brief descriptions. Panel 4: 'Client Results' — two testimonials with client names and a subtle statistic or performance highlight.
>
> Overall: generous margins, clean sans-serif typography, navy and gold accents, professional but warm — not cold and corporate. Print-ready at 300 DPI with 3mm bleed."

What this prompt does right: it maps every panel, describes the content for each, specifies brand colors, defines the tone, and includes print specifications. The agent is not guessing what goes where — it is executing a layout plan.

## Step 4 — Iterate on Layout and Readability

[IMAGE 3 PLACEHOLDER — Real UI Screenshot]

Brochure design has physical constraints that screen design does not. Your refinements should address:

**Fold lines**: Content that crosses the fold gets distorted in printing and looks sloppy. Ask the agent to "ensure no text or key image elements cross the fold lines — add a clear margin zone on either side of each fold."

**Text size at reading distance**: Brochures are read at arm's length. Body text below 9pt becomes unreadable to anyone over 40. "Increase all body text to at least 10pt — this is a print document, not a website."

**Color mode**: Screens use RGB. Print uses CMYK. "Switch to CMYK color profile for print — the navy should be deep and rich, not converted from screen blue."

**Image resolution**: Web images at 72 DPI look fine on screen but produce visible pixelation in print. "Ensure all images are rendered at 300 DPI native resolution — no upscaled low-res images."

**Panel balance**: If one panel is text-heavy and another is sparse, the layout feels lopsided. "Redistribute the content — Panel 4 looks empty compared to the crowded Panel 2. Move one service description from Panel 3 to Panel 4."

## Step 5 — Export for Your Printer

Professional printing requires specific file prep. Before sending your brochure to the printer:

1. **Export as PDF/X-4**: This standard embeds fonts, preserves color profiles, and includes bleed and trim settings that commercial printers expect.

2. **Include 3mm bleed**: Bleed extends your background colors and images beyond the trim edge, so there are no white slivers when the brochure is cut to size. Specify bleed in your export settings.

3. **Add crop marks**: These guide the printer's cutting machine. Include them in the export.

4. **Check total ink coverage**: CMYK ink coverage above 300% can cause drying issues on some paper stocks. Most design software shows ink coverage warnings. If your deep navy background exceeds 300%, ask the agent to adjust "reduce total ink coverage to 280% max."

5. **Request a proof**: Most print shops offer a digital or hard-copy proof for a small fee. Order one. Brochure colors on your monitor will not perfectly match the printed result. A proof catches surprises before you print 500 copies.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I use AI-generated images in a printed brochure?**

Yes. Ensure the images are generated at 300 DPI and in CMYK color space. RGB images converted to CMYK during export can shift in color — generate in RGB if you are printing digitally (many digital presses prefer RGB), or in CMYK for offset printing. Ask your printer which they prefer.

**What paper stock should I use for a tri-fold brochure?**

Standard: 100 lb gloss or matte text stock. Premium: 100 lb cover stock — thicker, more durable, feels substantial in hand. For luxury brands, consider a soft-touch matte coating or spot UV on the logo.

**How do I add my own photos alongside AI-generated content?**

Upload your photos to the agent and specify which panel they belong in: "Panel 1 hero image: use my uploaded photo of our office. Panel 3 service images: generate three icon-style illustrations." The agent mixes uploaded and generated content in the same layout.

**What if I need a bifold or gate-fold instead?**

Specify the format at the start of your prompt: "A4 bifold brochure, 4 panels" or "Gate-fold brochure, A4, 6 panels." The agent adjusts the panel count and fold logic accordingly. Bi-fold and gate-fold have different panel sequences than tri-fold — check your panel count before prompting.

**Can the agent handle multilingual brochures?**

Yes. Provide content in your target language. For languages with longer average word length than English (German, Finnish) or shorter (Chinese, Japanese), note in your prompt: "The body text is in German — allow extra space for longer words" or "Text is in Japanese — adjust line height and spacing for CJK typography."

**How much does it cost to print 500 brochures?**

Roughly $150-$400 for standard tri-fold on 100 lb stock at a commercial printer, depending on paper quality, coating, and turnaround time. Digital printing is cost-effective at this volume; offset printing becomes more economical above 1,000-2,000 copies.

**Can I generate a digital version for email or PDF download?**

Yes. Export the same design as an interactive PDF (single pages rather than printer spreads) for screen viewing. Add clickable links for your website, email, and social handles — the agent can embed hyperlinks in PDF exports if you specify them.
