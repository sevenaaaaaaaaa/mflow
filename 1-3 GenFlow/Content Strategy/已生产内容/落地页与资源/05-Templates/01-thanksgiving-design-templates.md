# Thanksgiving Design Kit: Warm & Grateful Templates for Your Brand

Thanksgiving marketing walks a fine line. Too promotional, and you're the brand that turned gratitude into a sales pitch. Too generic, and your "We're thankful for our customers!" post blends into the same feed of indistinguishable holiday graphics everyone else posted.

The sweet spot — and the design challenge — is warmth that feels genuine, gratitude that feels specific, and visuals that feel like your brand, not a stock template.

Lovart's Thanksgiving Design Kit is built for that balance. Here's what's inside and how to use it.

### Gratitude Posts (6 templates)

- "Thank You" hero graphics with customizable recipient messaging (customers, team, community, partners)

## Design Principles for Thanksgiving Content

### 1. Warmth Comes from Specificity

"We're grateful for you!" is wallpaper. "We're grateful for the 12,000 of you who showed up to our workshops this year — and the 47 volunteers who made them possible" is a message.

The design should support specificity, not substitute for it. Our templates leave generous space for real copy — names, numbers, stories — not just a headline and a logo.

### 2. Color Palettes That Feel Like November

The Thanksgiving Kit defaults to four palette directions, each evoking a different emotional register:

- **Harvest Warmth** — Deep orange, amber, warm brown, cream. Classic and inviting.
- **Golden Hour** — Soft gold, dusty rose, sage green, off-white. Modern and elegant.
- **Hearth & Home** — Burgundy, navy, copper, parchment. Rich and sophisticated.
- **Crisp & Clean** — Forest green, ivory, warm grey, muted terracotta. Fresh and understated.

Your Brand Kit automatically applies your brand colors, so these palettes serve as accent directions — your brand remains the visual anchor.

### 3. Typography That Communicates Tone

Font pairing matters enormously for seasonal content. The kit includes recommended pairings for each mood:
- Serif headlines (Playfair Display, Lora) for warmth and tradition
- Sans-serif body (Inter, Source Sans) for readability
- Script accents (Caveat, Kalam) used sparingly for handwritten warmth

All editable. If your brand uses specific typefaces, they auto-apply through your Brand Kit.

## Campaign Ideas: Beyond "Happy Thanksgiving"

The best Thanksgiving content doesn't just mark the holiday — it uses the cultural moment to deepen brand connection. Here are three campaign directions the kit supports:

### 1. The Gratitude Series (Nov 20–26)

Post one gratitude graphic per day in the week leading up to Thanksgiving:
- Monday: Thank your team
- Tuesday: Thank your community
- Wednesday: Thank your partners/collaborators
- Thursday (Thanksgiving): Thank your customers
- Friday: Highlight a specific customer story
- Saturday (Small Business Saturday): Thank other small businesses

Each post uses a template from the Gratitude collection, customized with specific names, stories, and details. The series effect is cumulative — by Thursday's customer post, your audience has seen a week of genuine gratitude, and the sentiment lands with weight.

### 2. The "Our Table" Campaign

If your brand has a strong community or team culture, use the photo-forward templates to share what "the table" looks like at your organization. This works especially well for:
- Restaurants and food brands (literal tables, literal food)
- Community organizations (the metaphorical table of inclusion)
- Remote teams (the digital table — Slack threads, Zoom screens, shared documents)
- Retail and hospitality (the team behind the counter)

The visual metaphor of "the table" is flexible, warm, and distinct from the generic "grateful" posts saturating feeds.

### 3. The Quiet Thanksgiving

Not every brand needs to go big. For B2B companies, professional services, and organizations where Thanksgiving isn't thematically native, the Business Updates templates offer a restrained approach:
- A simple, beautifully designed holiday closure notice
- A single gratitude post (quality over quantity)
- A preview of what's coming in December

Sometimes the most effective Thanksgiving content is knowing when to post less, not more.
