## Choosing Your Color Palette

[IMAGE 2 PLACEHOLDER — Conceptual Diagram]

Food-truck branding lives outdoors under harsh 4 p.m. sun and 9 p.m. string lights. Your palette must pop in both.

### Primary Colors
- **#E63946 (Fiery Red)** — Universal appetite stimulant. Use as the dominant wrap color or accent stripe. Works across Mexican, BBQ, and Asian-fusion trucks.
- **#F4A261 (Warm Saffron)** — Conveys handmade warmth without tipping into fast-food cheapness. Pairs beautifully with charcoal gray for gourmet taco or bao-bun concepts.
- **#264653 (Dark Teal)** — An unexpected food-truck color that signals elevated street food. Use as a base for the truck body if you sell poke bowls, ceviche, or Mediterranean wraps.

### Secondary/Accent Colors
- **#E9C46A (Sunflower Yellow)** — For callout stickers, arrow decals, and "OPEN" signs.
- **#F1FAEE (Off-White Canvas)** — Background for menu boards, keeping text legible under direct sunlight.

## Typography Recommendations

### Logo/Display Font
**Bebas Neue** (sans-serif, free, Google Fonts) — Tall, condensed, and built for distance. A food truck logo needs to be readable from 40 feet; Bebas Neue does it while feeling editorial. Or, for a handcrafted vibe, **Amatic SC** (a sketched script that mimics chalkboard lettering).

### Body/Text Font
**Open Sans** — High x-height keeps tiny menu descriptions readable. Pair with **Lora** for story-driven trucks that weave origin tales into their packaging.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q1: Why can't I just use a stock logo and call it a day?
Stock logos don't translate to a vehicle wrap — they lack the bleed, scale, and color-calibration specs needed for vinyl printing. A brand kit built on Lovart exports print-ready CMYK files with proper dielines.

### Q2: How many colors should a food truck brand kit have?
Three to four. More than four creates visual noise at distance; fewer than three risks looking unfinished. One dominant, one supporting, one accent is the sweet spot.

### Q3: What's the ideal file format for truck-wrap printing?
Adobe Illustrator-compatible vector files (AI, EPS, SVG) plus a high-res layered PSD for the wrap installer. Lovart's Brand Kit exports all of these in one click.

### Q4: Should my food truck brand match my ghost kitchen door dash listing?
Yes — mismatched branding between your physical truck and delivery-platform listing confuses customers and dilutes repeat business. A single brand kit enforces consistency across both.

### Q5: How often should I update my truck's brand kit?
Refresh every 2–3 years, or when you add a second truck. Seasonal tweaks (holiday stickers, summer color pops) can overlay your permanent kit without a full redesign.

### Q6: Can I create a bilingual brand kit for my food truck?
Absolutely. Lovart's ChatCanvas supports multi-language text layers, so you can maintain one brand kit with toggleable English/Spanish/Chinese menu templates.
