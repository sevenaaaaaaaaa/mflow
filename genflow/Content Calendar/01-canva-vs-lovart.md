## Core Feature Comparison

### Design Approach

**Canva** gives you a layout skeleton and says "fill it in." That's powerful when you know exactly what you want and just need a head start. It's less helpful when you're unsure about what would actually work for your audience, or when you need something that doesn't quite match any template in the library.

**Lovart** starts by asking what you're trying to achieve. Tell it "I need a LinkedIn carousel for a B2B SaaS launch targeting CTOs" — and its MCoT engine analyses the business context, the target persona, the competitive landscape, and optimal visual strategy *before* generating a single pixel. You get a design that was reasoned about, not just randomly assembled from a latent space.

### Editing Capabilities

This is where the two tools diverge most dramatically.

**Canva** uses traditional drag-and-drop editing. It's familiar, precise, and gives you pixel-level control — but it also means *you* do the work. Want to change the background? Select the background layer, delete it, browse backgrounds, drag a new one in, resize it, reorder layers. Thirty seconds if you're fast.

**Lovart** uses **Touch Edit** — you literally tap an element on the generated image and tell it what to change. "Make this blue." "Remove that icon." "Swap this background for something more corporate." One tap, one sentence, done. No layer hunting. No manual resizing. It's not just faster — it fundamentally changes who can edit what.

There's also **Text Edit**, which lets you click any text in a generated image and rewrite it directly — no opening a separate text panel, no font matching gymnastics. The text just changes, in place, preserving the original styling.

### Brand Consistency

**Canva** has a Brand Kit. You upload logos, set colour palettes, pick fonts. Then — critically — you manually apply them to every template you use. Forgot to set brand colours on that Instagram story? Too bad, it's shipping in teal instead of navy.

**Lovart's Brand Kit** works differently: once configured (takes about five minutes), every generated image automatically pulls from your brand palette, font set, and logo library. The MCoT engine considers your brand guidelines *during* generation, not as an afterthought. No manual application step. No forgetting. Every output on-brand by default.

If you're managing multiple brands (agency, anyone?), Lovart's Team Plan supports multi-brand switching in a single workspace.

### Output Formats

| Format | Canva | Lovart |
|---|---|---|
| PNG | ✓ | ✓ |
| JPG | ✓ | ✓ |
| PDF | ✓ | ✓ |
| SVG | Limited | ✓ (editable vectors) |
| PSD | ✗ | ✓ (Photoshop-compatible layers) |
| MP4 / video | Limited | ✓ (6+ video models) |

For anyone who needs to hand designs off to a professional designer for refinement (or just wants true ownership of their assets), PSD and SVG export are a significant differentiator.

### Batch Production

**Canva** has bulk-create (with some spreadsheet gymnastics). **Lovart** has something called **Seedance 2.0**, which enables true batch generation — one creative direction, dozens of output variants across formats and sizes, all on-brand, all at once. Think: one campaign concept → 50 social media posts resized for Instagram, LinkedIn, X, and TikTok, each with minor variations — in minutes, not hours.

### Video Capabilities

Canva has a basic video editor — templates, timeline, stock footage integration. It's serviceable for simple clips.

Lovart integrates **6+ video models**, allowing you to generate video content from the same design prompts and brand parameters that drive your static image output. The gap here isn't huge for casual use, but for teams producing high-volume social video, Lovart's AI-native video pipeline saves real time.

### AI Integration Depth

**Canva's AI** is additive — Magic Write for copy, background remover, some generative fill features. Useful tools, but they sit *on top* of the template paradigm rather than replacing it.

**Lovart's AI** is foundational. The entire design process runs through MCoT reasoning, 9+ image models, and 6+ video models. There's no "template first, AI later" — the AI is the design engine itself.

## Typical Scenarios: Which Tool Wins Where

### Quick Social Media Graphic

**Winner: Either.** For a single Instagram post where you know exactly what you want and need it in three minutes, both tools deliver. Canva wins on library breadth (you'll find a template that's perfect faster). Lovart wins on brand consistency (no manual recolouring).

### Full Brand Identity Package

**Winner: Lovart.** When you need a cohesive system — logo variations, colour palette, typography hierarchy, social media templates, presentation deck, business cards — Lovart's MCoT reasoning ensures visual consistency across every output. Canva can do this, but *you* have to enforce the consistency manually across every document.

### Team Template Collaboration

**Winner: Canva.** Canva has a decade head start on real-time collaboration, commenting, version history, and team libraries. Lovart is building team features but isn't yet at Canva's level of collaborative maturity for template-driven workflows.

### Personalised, Non-Template Creative

**Winner: Lovart.** Need something that doesn't fit any template category — a surreal product launch visual, an editorial illustration, a custom infographic with a unique data visualisation style? Lovart generates from imagination, not from a grid. Canva can't compete here because its paradigm depends on templates existing.

### Enterprise-Scale Content Production

**Winner: Lovart** (for volume). When you need hundreds of on-brand assets per month, the combination of Brand Kit automation, batch generation via Seedance 2.0, and AI-native editing means Lovart scales more efficiently. Canva's template approach works but requires proportionally more human time per asset.
