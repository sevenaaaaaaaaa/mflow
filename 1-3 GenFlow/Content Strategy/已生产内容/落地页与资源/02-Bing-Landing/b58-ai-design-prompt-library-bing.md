# AI Design Prompt Library: 100+ Prompts for Professional Results

## Why Does Prompt Quality Determine AI Design Quality?

AI design tools like Lovart are only as good as the prompts they receive. A vague prompt ("make a nice Instagram post") produces generic, unpredictable results. A well-crafted prompt produces professional, on-brand designs that require minimal adjustment.

The difference isn't artistic talent — it's specificity. The AI needs to understand: what you're designing, who it's for, what style you want, what elements to include, and what constraints to follow. A good prompt answers all of these questions clearly.

This library contains 100+ tested, battle-proven prompts organized by use case. Each prompt follows a consistent structure that maximizes output quality. Copy, customize with your details, and use immediately.

## The Anatomy of a Great AI Design Prompt

Every effective design prompt contains these four components:

1. **Format & Platform**: Specify exactly what you're creating (Instagram Story, business card, presentation slide)
2. **Content & Message**: What text, data, or visual elements should appear
3. **Style Direction**: Mood, aesthetic, color preferences, typography guidance
4. **Technical Constraints**: Dimensions, safe zones, brand requirements, file format

**Example — Weak Prompt**:
> "Make a social media post about our sale."

**Example — Strong Prompt**:
> "Create a 1080x1080 Instagram post announcing our Summer Clearance Sale — 30% off all items. Use our brand colors (navy #1B2A4A, gold #C9A96E). Style: modern luxury, minimal. Include headline text 'Summer Sale — 30% Off,' a subheading with the dates 'June 1-15,' our logo top-left, and a clear 'Shop Now' CTA button. Elegant, uncluttered layout."

The strong prompt is 8x more specific — and will produce results 8x better.

## Prompt Crafting Tips for Best Results

### Do's

- **Be specific about format**: Always include dimensions (1080x1080, 1200x628, etc.) — this tells the AI exactly what canvas to work with
- **Describe style with adjectives**: "Minimal, modern, warm, editorial" produces better results than "nice" or "clean"
- **Specify colors**: Use hex codes when possible (#1B2A4A) or specific color names (navy, terracotta, sage)
- **Include content hierarchy**: "Headline first, subtext second, CTA last" helps the AI structure the layout
- **Reference platforms**: "Instagram post" triggers platform-specific layout conventions better than "square image"
- **Use negative guidance**: "Avoid stock photography look" or "No cliché clip art" helps the AI avoid common pitfalls

### Don'ts

- **Don't be vague**: "Make something cool" guarantees unpredictable results
- **Don't over-constrain**: Too many specific instructions can conflict. 4-6 clear directions work better than 15 micro-instructions.
- **Don't skip brand references**: Always include brand context — colors, fonts, style direction — or the AI will invent its own
- **Don't use abstract art terms unless you mean them**: "Surrealist" or "post-modern" will give you exactly that — which may not be what you want for a business graphic
