## The Scene: Justice Deserves Good Design

[IMAGE 2 PLACEHOLDER — Conceptual Diagram]

Legal branding has historically been terrible — gold-embossed serif logos on navy backgrounds, gavels, and scales of justice. Your clients are facing some of the most stressful moments of their lives — divorce, criminal charges, business disputes. Your brand must project competence, compassion, and the quiet confidence that says I have handled worse cases than yours. Skip the gavel.

|---|---|---|
| Primary Deep Navy | `#1A2744` | Logo, trust, legal authority |
| Secondary Clean White | `#FFFFFF` | Documents, backgrounds |
| Accent Burgundy | `#722F37` | Prestige accent, courtroom warmth |

## Font Recommendations

- **Primary Logo:** Cinzel — dignified serif suggesting tradition and gravitas
- **Headlines:** Lora — warm, authoritative serif for practice area descriptions
- **Body Text:** Inter — maximum clarity for contracts, briefs, and client communications
- **Accent:** Playfair Display — refined serif for firm history and attorney credentials
- **Digital:** Rubik — clean sans-serif for website and client portal

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q1: Can a law firm brand be modern or does it need to look traditional?**
Depends on your practice area and clientele. Estate planning for retirees: traditional conveys stability. Tech startup law: modern signals you understand their world. The rule is: match your clients' expectations, then exceed them by one degree.

**Q2: What are the ethics rules around legal branding?**
Every jurisdiction has rules. Generally: no false or misleading claims, no guarantees of results, no client testimonials without disclaimers in some states. Lovart's legal templates include disclaimer placement zones reviewed for common bar association standards.

**Q3: How do I communicate my expertise without sounding arrogant?**
Let credentials and results do the heavy lifting. Board certifications, published articles, speaking engagements, and representative matters (appropriately anonymized) communicate expertise factually. Lovart's credential display templates are factual and dignified.

**Q4: What's the most important client-facing document to brand?**
The engagement letter — it is the contract that defines your relationship. A well-designed engagement letter sets expectations, communicates professionalism, and reduces scope disputes. Lovart's engagement letter template balances legal necessity with brand polish.

**Q5: Should I niche down or be a general practice?**
Niche firms earn more, market more efficiently, and get better referrals. If you focus on one area (immigration, IP, family law), your entire brand can speak directly to that client's specific anxieties. General practice branding is harder to make memorable.

**Q6: How do I make my website convert visitors to consultations?**
Clear practice area pages, attorney photos (real, professional headshots), transparent fee structures (or at least a clear path to understanding costs), and a simple contact form. Remove friction, not information. Lovart generates law firm website design systems.
