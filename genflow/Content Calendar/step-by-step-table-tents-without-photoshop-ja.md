## Step 1: Define Your Table Tent Type and Dimensions

[IMAGE 2 PLACEHOLDER — Conceptual Diagram]

Open Lovart and select the **Table Tent** template (Templates → Print → Table Tents). Choose your format: **Standard A-Frame** (4×6 inches folded, the most common restaurant table tent), **Triangular 3-Panel** (3 equal panels, good for rotating 3 promotions), **Tall Slim** (3×8 inches, for narrow table spaces), or **Die-Cut Shape** (custom silhouette, available on $49 Pro). Type: **"A-Frame table tent, 4x6 inches folded, double-sided — front side happy hour promotion, back side Wi-Fi and social media. Dark cocktail-bar aesthetic with deep navy background, gold text accents, art-deco-inspired border pattern."** Lovart generates both sides of the tent with print-safe margins and a fold-line indicator.

## Step 2: Design the Front Panel — Your Offer

The front panel of a table tent is a 4×6-inch billboard viewed from 2–3 feet away. Every pixel matters. Write your headline at 36pt minimum — patrons should read it without leaning forward. Type your promotion: **"ESPRESSO MARTINI $7 — 4PM TO 7PM DAILY"** and Lovart sizes and positions the text for readability at distance. Use Lovart's **Legibility Checker** (View → Legibility) to simulate how the tent reads at 3 feet, 5 feet, and 8 feet. Text that's readable at 8 feet works for bar counter displays; 3–5 feet suffices for standard table placements. Add a generated visual: type **"espresso martini glass with three coffee beans floating on foam, dramatic lighting with gold rim light, dark background"** for a photographic accent. Or keep it typographic with art-deco border patterns from Lovart's Ornament Library.

## Step 3: Design the Back Panel — Utility Content

The back panel faces the table, not the room — patrons see it when they sit down or pick up the tent. This is prime real estate for utility content they'll actually use. Lovart's **Back Panel Content Presets** include: Wi-Fi network and password (formatted in a clean, scannable layout), Instagram handle with an auto-generated QR code (tap QR → Generate from URL, paste your Instagram URL, Lovart renders a print-optimized QR), menu highlights, upcoming event dates, or a "Tag Us" social media prompt. For the cocktail bar: **"Front: Happy Hour promo. Back: Wi-Fi credentials in a bordered section at top, Instagram QR code with '@craftandcocktail' handle below, and 'Scan for Our Full Menu' CTA at bottom."**

## Step 4: Add QR Codes That Actually Work

A QR code on a table tent must scan reliably in dim bar lighting at an angle — failed scans mean lost engagement. Lovart's **QR Generator** includes a Print Reliability Mode: it renders QR codes at higher contrast, with a 4-module quiet zone (exceeding the standard 2-module margin), and tests scannability using a simulated low-light, off-angle scanner. After generating, tap **Test QR** — Lovart shows a simulated phone screen with camera viewfinder at 15-degree angle in low ambient light, confirming whether the code reads. For dark-background tents, QR codes render with a white surround to ensure the quiet zone is maintained. Dynamic QR codes (trackable, editable destination URL) require the $49 Pro plan and integrate with Lovart's Analytics dashboard.

## FAQ

**Q: What cardstock weight should I use for table tents?**
A: 14pt to 16pt (roughly 100lb to 120lb cover weight). Anything lighter will tip over or curl from humidity. Lovart includes cardstock recommendations in the export PDF's print specifications. Matte lamination adds spill-resistance.

**Q: Can Lovart generate daily-special table tents in bulk?**
A: On the $49 Pro plan, use **Batch Generate** — upload a CSV with columns for item name, description, and price, and Lovart generates a table tent for each row. Ideal for daily specials, rotating taps, or seasonal menus across multiple locations.

**Q: How do I make sure QR codes scan in dim restaurant lighting?**
A: Ensure the QR code has a white quiet zone border (Lovart does this automatically), maintain at least 0.75-inch print size, and avoid gloss lamination directly over the QR code (matte lamination is fine). Lovart's Print Reliability Mode simulates low-light scanning.

**Q: Can I use Lovart table tents for non-restaurant purposes?**
A: Yes — table tents work for hotel room desks, conference tables, retail counter displays, wedding receptions (table numbers, menus), and trade show booths. The template adapts to any promotional or informational table-top content.

**Q: What's the difference between A-Frame and Triangular table tents?**
A: A-Frame tents have two sides (front and back) and sit like an inverted V. Triangular tents have three panels and stand as a triangle — better for displaying more content (e.g., breakfast, lunch, dinner panels) but cost more to print.

**Q: Can I add scannable NFC tag artwork to my table tent design?**
A: While Lovart doesn't encode NFC tags, you can design a visual "Tap Here" indicator with NFC iconography and a circular design element. Use the Shape and Icon Library to place an NFC indicator, then apply physical NFC stickers to the printed tents at that location.
