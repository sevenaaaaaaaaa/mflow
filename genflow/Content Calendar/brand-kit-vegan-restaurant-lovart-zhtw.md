## Choosing Your Color Palette

[IMAGE 2 PLACEHOLDER — Conceptual Diagram]

Vegan restaurant palettes should avoid cliché "green equals healthy" monotony. Instead, use greens as supporting players while letting unexpected colors carry the emotional weight.

### Primary Colors
- **#2D6A4F (Forest Verde)** — A deep, sophisticated green that pairs with terracotta and cream. Use for logos, menu headers, and exterior signage. It signals "rooted" without shouting "salad bar."
- **#D4A373 (Warm Earth)** — Terracotta/clay tones add the warmth that pure-green palettes often lack. Essential for cozy, date-night vegan concepts.
- **#1B4332 (Dark Evergreen)** — A near-black green for premium positioning. Works as a background color for white-text menus and minimal-luxe storefronts.

### Secondary/Accent Colors
- **#FFB703 (Golden Amber)** — Adds appetite appeal without relying on meat-industry color codes. Use for call-to-action buttons, specials stickers, and social-media templates.
- **#E9EDC9 (Pale Celery)** — A soft, food-safe off-white for menu backgrounds and packaging interiors. Photographs beautifully alongside colorful dishes.
- **#BC4742 (Rust Red)** — A surprise accent for plant-based burger joints and comfort-food concepts. Proves vegan branding can be bold.

## Typography Recommendations

### Logo/Display Font
**Josefin Sans** — Geometric, modern, and warm. Its rounded terminals feel approachable without sacrificing sophistication. For farm-to-table concepts, **Abril Fatface** adds editorial gravitas that works beautifully on window decals and tote bags.

### Body/Text Font
**Work Sans** — Optimized for on-screen readability with generous spacing. Handles everything from ingredient lists to website body copy. Pair with **Crimson Text** for restaurants that tell ingredient-origin stories.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q1: Should a vegan restaurant avoid the color green entirely?
No — but make green a deliberate choice, not a default. Use deep, complex greens (#2D6A4F, #1B4332) instead of bright, juice-bar greens (#4CAF50). The difference is the difference between "special occasion" and "temporary diet."

### Q2: How do I appeal to non-vegans with my brand kit?
Avoid language and imagery that signals deprivation. Show abundant, colorful food photography. Use warm, inclusive palette accents like amber and terracotta. Your brand kit should look like a great restaurant that happens to be plant-based.

### Q3: What packaging materials complement a vegan brand kit?
Kraft paper, compostable containers, and soy-based inks align with your audience's values and look beautiful against earthy palettes. Lovart's mockup generator can preview palette-and-material combinations before you order samples.

### Q4: How do I brand delivery-platform listings consistently?
Use your Lovart Brand Kit's Delivery Template — it auto-generates DoorDash, Uber Eats, and Grubhub image specs from your master palette, so thumbnails match your physical menu perfectly.

### Q5: Can vegan brands use luxury design language?
Absolutely. Minimalist serif typography, restrained cream-on-charcoal palettes, and editorial food photography position vegan restaurants at the same level as fine-dining establishments. Lovart's template library includes white-tablecloth vegan presets.

### Q6: What's the biggest branding gap vegan restaurants overlook?
Consistency between in-store and online. A beautifully designed physical menu that doesn't match the website or delivery listing erodes trust. A single Lovart brand kit syncs every touchpoint.
