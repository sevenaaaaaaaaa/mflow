# Lovart Design Challenge #2: Announcing the Prompt, Prizes, and Judges for Summer 2027

[IMAGE 1 PLACEHOLDER — Persona Scenario]

Three months ago, 847 creators from 42 countries answered Challenge #1's prompt — "Design the cover of a book you wish existed" — with work that exceeded every expectation we had. The winning designs are still being shared, discussed, and referenced in the Lovart community.

Now it is time for Challenge #2. This time, we are going bigger — in prompt ambition, in prize pool, and in global reach. Here is everything you need to know.

## The Prompt: "Design the City of 2047"

[IMAGE 2 PLACEHOLDER — Conceptual Diagram]

Twenty years from now, what does a city look like? Not a generic "city of the future" with flying cars and chrome towers — a specific city, in a specific place, shaped by specific choices about climate, technology, community, mobility, nature, work, and human connection.

Your design can be anything that communicates your vision of a city in 2047:

- A city skyline or streetscape
- A transit map
- A promotional poster for the city ("Visit Neo-Seoul 2047")
- A citizen ID card or welcome packet
- A public service announcement from the city government
- An architectural diagram or urban plan
- A travel guide cover
- A news broadcast graphic about the city
- A mural or public art proposal
- A data visualization of the city's demographics, energy grid, or ecosystem
- A city flag or seal
- A protest poster from a citizen movement within the city
- Anything else that communicates "this is what a city looks like in 2047"

The only constraint: it must be a **specific** city with a **specific** point of view. "Generic utopian city" will not stand out. "Phoenix, Arizona in 2047, redesigned around water conservation and shade architecture" will. "Shanghai in 2047, where every rooftop is a farm" will. "A new floating city in the Bay of Bengal, built by climate refugees" will. Specificity wins.

## Why This Prompt

We chose "Design the City of 2047" for three reasons:

**1. It demands design thinking, not just design execution.** The strongest submissions will not just be visually beautiful — they will communicate a coherent idea about how cities should work. What did the city choose to prioritize? What did it choose to abandon? What problems did it solve, and what new problems emerged? The visual design is in service of an idea. Challenge #1 taught us that Lovart creators excel at this; Challenge #2 raises the bar.

**2. It is open to every design format and style.** Challenge #1's "book cover" constraint was productive for many creators but limiting for others. Challenge #2 is format-agnostic — you choose the medium that best communicates your vision.

**3. It invites global perspectives.** Cities mean different things in different places. We want to see visions from every continent, every climate, every cultural context. The city of 2047 looks different from Lagos than from Reykjavik. Show us.

## Judges

Our panel for Challenge #2 brings together perspectives from architecture, urban design, visual communication, and AI creativity:

- **Amara Okafor** — Urban designer and principal at Civic Future Studio (Lagos/Nairobi). Amara's work focuses on climate-adaptive urban design in rapidly growing African cities. She was named one of Dezeen's "15 Designers Shaping the Future" in 2026.
- **Diego Reyes** — Visual artist and creative director specializing in speculative design and future-visions work. His "City of Ghosts" series (AI-generated visions of abandoned megacities) was exhibited at MoMA PS1 in 2026.
- **Yuki Tanaka** — Architect and professor at Tokyo University of the Arts. Yuki's research explores the intersection of traditional Japanese spatial design and AI-generated architecture. She brings a rigorous design-education perspective to the judging panel.
- **Priya Nair** — Winner of Lovart Design Challenge #1 (Third Place) and UX lead at a Bangalore-based health-tech company. Priya represents the creator community perspective — she knows firsthand what it takes to produce outstanding work with Lovart, and she will be evaluating submissions as a peer, not just a critic.
- **Marcus Chen** — Lovart's Head of Design. Marcus leads the creative direction of Lovart's template library and brand system. He brings deep knowledge of what Lovart's AI can do — and what counts as truly pushing the tool's boundaries.

## Judging Criteria

Every submission will be evaluated on four criteria:

| Criterion | Weight | What We Are Looking For |
|-----------|--------|------------------------|
| **Conceptual Strength** | 30% | Does the design communicate a clear, compelling, specific vision of a city in 2047? Is there a coherent idea behind the visual? |
| **Visual Execution** | 30% | Composition, color, typography, hierarchy, craft. Does the design work as a piece of visual communication? |
| **Originality** | 20% | Is this a vision we have not seen before? Does it avoid clichés of futuristic cities? Does it surprise us? |
| **Emotional Resonance** | 20% | Does the design make us feel something about this city? Do we want to visit it? Do we want to live in it? Are we concerned about it? Does it stay with us after we look away? |

## How to Enter

1. **Create your design** using Lovart (free tier or any paid tier — all are eligible).
2. **Submit** at lovart.ai/challenge by **11:59 PM UTC on July 31, 2027**.
3. Include a **short artist statement** (100-300 words) explaining your city vision. The statement matters — it provides context that helps judges understand the idea behind your visual.
4. One submission per creator. Choose your strongest work.
5. All designs must be original work created during the challenge period. You can use any Lovart features, templates, and AI generation tools. You can bring in external assets (photography, hand-drawn elements) as long as the core design was executed in Lovart.
