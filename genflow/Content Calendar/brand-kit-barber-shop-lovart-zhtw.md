## Recommended Color Palette

Barber branding has evolved from the classic barber pole into two distinct lanes: heritage and modern.

| Color Role | Hex Code | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| Primary Midnight Blue | `#1A1A2E` | Logo, walls, dominant brand color |
| Secondary Rich Gold | `#C9A96E` | Logo accents, mirror frames, foil details |
| Accent Barber Red | `#C1292E` | Pole stripe reference, CTA buttons, loyalty stamps |
| Neutral Charcoal | `#3A3A3A` | Typography, aprons, secondary elements |
| Cream | `#F5F0E8` | Stationery, appointment cards, backgrounds |
| Whiskey Amber | `#B8860B` | Product labels, social accents, warm glow |

**Alternative — Modern Streetwear Barber:** `#000000` (black) + `#FFFFFF` (white) + `#FF4500` (neon orange accent) + `#808080` (silver).  
**Alternative — Vintage Traditional:** `#8B0000` (dark red) + `#F5DEB3` (wheat) + `#2F4F4F` (dark slate) + `#DAA520` (goldenrod).

## Font Recommendations

- **Primary Logo:** Bebas Neue — bold condensed uppercase, staples of barber branding
- **Headlines:** Oswald — strong condensed sans-serif for window signage and price lists
- **Body Text:** Lato — clean, professional sans-serif for service menus and booking confirmations
- **Accent:** Playfair Display — refined serif contrast for "Est. 2015" and heritage markers
- **Digital:** Montserrat — versatile sans-serif for Instagram posts and website

## 5 Frequently Asked Questions

**Q1: Do I need the classic barber pole in my logo?**
Not necessarily. If your shop is traditional, a subtle nod (red-white-blue stripe motif) connects you to barbering heritage. If you're a modern shop, skip it entirely — your vibe will attract the right clientele without historical references.

**Q2: What's the most important branded item in my shop?**
The mirror. Every customer stares at it for 30+ minutes. A subtle logo decal in the corner becomes burned into their memory. Lovart generates mirror-ready decal templates sized for standard barber station mirrors.

**Q3: How do I make my price list look premium rather than cheap?**
Typography hierarchy is everything. Use your bold headline font for service categories, body font for service names, and a lighter weight for prices. Never use clip art or icons for services — let clean typography do the work.

**Q4: Should I sell branded products (pomade, beard oil)?**
Absolutely. Branded grooming products generate passive income and turn customers into walking advertisements in their bathrooms. Lovart's product label template pack covers jars, bottles, and tins.

**Q5: How do I build social proof into my brand kit?**
Include a review card template — a designed graphic that features a customer quote with their photo (with permission). Post one per week. Also create a "client of the month" spotlight template.

**Q6: What's the best way to handle booking system branding?**
Your online booking page should match your brand kit exactly — same colors, fonts, and logo. Inconsistent booking pages cause drop-offs. Lovart exports CSS color values and font names ready for Resurva, Booksy, or Squire integration.
