## Soft, Sophisticated, Editorial: The Makeup Studio Palette

[IMAGE 2 PLACEHOLDER — Conceptual Diagram]

Makeup studio color palettes should feel like looking through a luxury beauty counter — refined, aspirational, and deliberately curated. Lovart's **Makeup Palette Engine**:

**Bridal Makeup Studio:** Ivory (#FDFBF7), champagne (#E8D5B7), rose gold (#B88B7A), soft blush (#E8C4C4), and dove gray (#B8B0B0). This palette mimics the colors of a wedding: the dress, the rings, the flowers, the morning light. It feels timeless — a bride looking at this brand in 2026 and again in 2036 should still find it beautiful. The champagne and rose gold metallics add warmth without color commitment.

**Editorial/Fashion Makeup:** Cream (#FAFAF5), obsidian (#1A1A1A), scarlet (#C41E3A), gunmetal (#545454), and paper white (#F5F5F5). High-contrast, dramatic, fashion-forward. The scarlet accent is intentionally the only bright color — like a red lip on an otherwise neutral face. This palette photographs dramatically and looks intentional in black-and-white conversions.

**Clean Beauty Makeup Studio:** Alabaster (#F5F0EB), sage (#B0BFA0), clay (#C49A8C), oat (#D4C4B0), and mineral gray (#8A8A82). Earth-derived colors that communicate "natural ingredients," "skin-first philosophy," and "non-toxic beauty." The palette feels like a luxury apothecary — scientific credibility wrapped in organic warmth.

Lovart's palettes include **Texture Recommendations** — specific material finishes for each color. For bridal: soft matte for backgrounds, pearl/metallic for accents, uncoated paper for printed materials (feels more personal than glossy). For editorial: high-gloss for photo prints, matte black for packaging, spot-UV for logo on business cards.

## Typography: From Consultation Forms to Instagram

Makeup studio typography must be beautiful (the beauty industry demands it) and highly functional (consultation forms, allergen lists, and color-matching cards require clear communication). Lovart's **Makeup Type System**:

**Studio Name Font** — The logo. High-contrast modern serifs (Bodoni, Didot, Cormorant) dominate luxury beauty. These fonts share DNA with Vogue and high-end cosmetic brands, creating instant category association. Lovart recommends serif logos for bridal and editorial studios; warm sans-serif (Raleway, Josefin Sans) for clean beauty studios.

**Service Menu Font** — For the bridal package menu, price list, and service descriptions. Clean sans-serif at 11–13pt. Lovart's **Menu Hierarchy System** creates automatic formatting: Package Name (bold, 14pt), Package Description (regular, 11pt), Price (medium, 13pt, in accent color), and Inclusions List (regular, 10pt with bullet markers).

**Client Consultation Form Font** — The form brides fill out before their trial. Must be clean, structured, and professional. Lovart generates a fillable PDF with: skin type, allergies, desired look, reference photo upload area, and special notes — all in the brand's body font at 12pt for easy reading and writing.

**Social Overlay Font** — For Instagram before/after photos and TikTok tutorials. Thin, minimal sans-serif (Josefin Sans Thin, Cormorant Light Italic) positioned in negative space — the makeup is the hero, not the text.

## Portfolio Templates: The Before/After That Books Clients

[IMAGE 3 PLACEHOLDER — Real UI Screenshot]

Before/after photos are the single most important marketing asset for makeup studios. Lovart's **Portfolio System**:

**Classic Before/After Split** (1080×1080) — Side-by-side comparison with: minimal brand border, "BEFORE" and "AFTER" labels in the brand's accent color (8pt, discreet — let the transformation speak), and a subtle "By [Studio Name]" credit. The labels use the brand's accent font; everything else stays minimal.

**Bridal Look Trio** (1080×1350) — Three photos stacked vertically: natural/no-makeup, full bridal look, and a detail close-up (eye makeup or lip). This tells the complete transformation story and is optimized for Instagram's portrait format which gets 29% more engagement than square.

**Product Flat-Lay Template** (1080×1080) — For showcasing the products used in a look. A branded template with: product arrangement zone (center), a "Products Used" header, and numbered callout zones around the edges where product names appear. These posts get high save rates because followers want to shop the look.

**Behind-the-Scenes Story Template** (1080×1920) — Instagram Story template showing: the makeup chair setup, products in use, and a "Book Your Trial" CTA sticker zone. Stories convert at 3x the rate of grid posts for service businesses.

## Service Menu Psychology: Packaging Expertise

Lovart's **Service Menu Designer** applies behavioral psychology to pricing presentation: **Package Names** that sell the outcome, not the service ("The Radiant Bride" not "Bridal Makeup — Full Face"), **Tiered Pricing** with 3 levels (Signature, Premium, Luxury — the Goldilocks strategy where most choose the middle), **Visual Anchoring** — list the most premium package first so the middle option feels like exceptional value, and **Inclusions Formatting** — list everything included (trial session, touch-up kit, lashes, airbrush) even if competitors include the same things. Listing creates perceived value; assuming clients know creates perceived scarcity.
