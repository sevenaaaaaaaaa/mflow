# How Travel Blogger Tomi Adeyemi Removed 1,200 Watermarks — And Built a Shutterstock-Free Blog That Ranks on Page One

[IMAGE 1 PLACEHOLDER — Persona Scenario]

Tomi Adeyemi's travel blog, *The Lagos Departure*, had a problem hiding in plain sight.

She'd built a library of 1,200 blog posts over five years — destination guides, hotel reviews, packing checklists, food tours. The content was good enough to earn 80,000 monthly organic visitors and $6,200 in Mediavine ad revenue. But the images were a disaster.

During her first two years of blogging, Tomi had used Shutterstock preview images — watermarked, low-resolution — as placeholders while she wrote, intending to purchase licenses later. "Later" never came. When Mediavine rejected her site in 2024 for "insufficient image rights documentation," she realized those watermarked placeholders weren't just placeholders anymore. They were a business liability.

Purchasing licenses for all 1,200 images would cost roughly $15,000 at Shutterstock's on-demand pricing — more than two months of her blog's total revenue. Removing watermarks manually in Photoshop, assuming 15 minutes per image, meant 300 hours of tedious clone-stamp work.

Then she found AI object removal — and cleaned her entire blog in a single weekend.

## The Background

[IMAGE 2 PLACEHOLDER — Conceptual Diagram]

*The Lagos Departure* launched in 2021 as a side project while Tomi worked as a management consultant. By 2025, it was her full-time income — 80,000 monthly sessions, five-figure annual revenue, sponsorship inquiries from tourism boards and luggage brands.

The business had outgrown its infrastructure. Specifically, its image library.

"My early posts were a mess. I'd find a Shutterstock photo that matched my content, screenshot the preview with the watermark splashed across it, and think 'I'll buy the license before this goes live.' Then I'd publish at 2 AM and forget. Multiply that by 800 posts over two years."

Tomi had three image problems compounding each other:

1. **Watermarked preview images:** 800+ posts used Shutterstock, Getty, or Adobe Stock watermarks. Legally and ethically problematic. Mediavine and premium ad networks won't touch sites with unlicensed stock imagery.
2. **Visual bloat from over-editing:** In a misguided attempt to hide watermarks, Tomi had overlayed heavy vignettes, text boxes, and filter effects on many images — making them look "designed" but not in a good way.
3. **Google image search penalty:** Posts with obviously watermarked photos were being filtered from Google Image Search results, costing an estimated 15-20% of potential image-search traffic.

## The Pain Points

### 1. The Licensing Dead End

Shutterstock's on-demand pricing is straightforward: 10 images for $49, 25 for $119, 350 for $199/month. But Tomi needed to license 1,200 images across three different stock platforms — many of which were years old with no purchase history or saved lightbox. Tracking down each image's original source, license terms, and current availability was a research project that would take months.

### 2. The Manual Retouch Trap

Photoshop's Clone Stamp, Healing Brush, and Content-Aware Fill can remove watermarks — and professional retouchers charge $5-15 per image for this work. At the low end, 1,200 images meant $6,000. And that assumed the watermark wasn't placed over complex areas like faces, text, or patterned backgrounds where manual retouching becomes exponentially harder.

### 3. The Revenue Cliff

Mediavine, Raptive, and premium ad networks all require image rights compliance. Getting rejected by Mediavine meant Tomi was running lower-CPM ad networks — roughly $8 RPM versus the $22 RPM Mediavine publishers average. That gap represented approximately $1,100/month in lost ad revenue. The watermarks were costing her real money every single month.

## FAQ

### Is it legal to remove watermarks from stock photo previews?

No — and this case study is about fixing a past mistake, not recommending the practice. Stock photo previews are copyrighted content, and watermarks exist to protect that copyright. Tomi's use case was remediation of an existing problem; her current workflow uses AI-generated original images. If you use stock photography, purchase licenses. AI removal tools exist for legitimate use cases: removing date stamps from personal photos, cleaning up your own watermarked drafts before applying final branding, or removing unwanted objects from images you own.

### What are legitimate uses for AI watermark/object removal?

Common legitimate uses include: removing date stamps and timecodes from personal photos and videos, cleaning sensor dust spots from digital photographs, removing photobombers and unwanted objects from images you own, restoring old family photos with physical damage or markings, and cleaning up UI elements from screenshots before presentations.

### How does AI watermark removal handle complex backgrounds?

Lovart's object removal analyzes the surrounding pixels in multiple directions, reconstructing patterns, gradients, and textures with context awareness. For simple backgrounds (sky, walls, water), reconstruction is virtually seamless. For complex backgrounds (crowds, foliage, geometric patterns), results are strong but may require Touch Edit refinement on edge transitions. The AI handles approximately 90% of backgrounds with no visible artifacts.

### Will removing watermarks from old blog posts help my SEO?

If your blog posts were penalized or filtered for low-quality image content, cleaning and re-uploading properly attributed images can improve page experience signals. Tomi's case: resubmitting her sitemap after the cleanup resulted in 340% image search impression growth within 30 days. Note that Google's algorithms also evaluate image originality — AI-generated original images will outperform cleaned stock images long-term.

### How long does it take to clean a full blog's image library?

The processing is fast; the bottleneck is human review. AI removal of a single watermark takes 3-8 seconds. Brushing over the watermark area takes 10-15 seconds. The full workflow — brush, process, review, approve — averages 25-35 seconds per image. A library of 1,000 images takes approximately 7-10 hours, which most bloggers complete in a weekend.

### Can I use Lovart to generate original images instead of buying stock photos?

Yes, and this is the recommended long-term approach. Lovart's text-to-image generation produces original, commercially licensed images tailored to your specific content. A blog post about "best ramen in Tokyo" can have an original AI-generated image of Tokyo ramen rather than a generic stock photo. Original images perform better in search, eliminate licensing concerns, and create a distinctive visual brand.

### What other image problems can AI fix besides watermarks?

Lovart's object removal handles logos from clothing, text overlays, date stamps, power lines in landscapes, sensor dust spots, and small photobombers. The enhancement tools address low resolution, noise, and soft focus. The expander fixes tight crops. Combined, these tools can restore most common image quality issues in a single session.
