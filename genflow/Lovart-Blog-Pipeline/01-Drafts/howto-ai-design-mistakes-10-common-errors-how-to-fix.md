# 10 Common AI Design Mistakes and How to Fix Them (2026)

A marketing coordinator stares at her screen. Six weeks in, some outputs are remarkable; others look like a robot’s guess at “professional.” Same tool, same user, wildly different results. She is not bad at this. She is making mistakes that become obvious the moment someone names them.

This guide names ten recurring failures and the fixes that work in production—not theory slides. Most failures are input problems, not model problems. Fix the brief, activate Brand Kit, specify dimensions, and shift your role from producer to editor.

## Who this is for

Marketing coordinators, founders doing their own social, freelancers onboarding clients to AI design, and anyone auditing why “AI quality” swings week to week.

## Mistake 1: The generic prompt

**Problem:** “Make a social post for our product launch.”

**Why it fails:** No product, audience, brand, format, or aesthetic. The model fills voids with statistical averages—generic “social post.”

**Fix:** Specify platform pixels, audience, brand tokens, headline intent, and mood. Example: “Instagram carousel 1080×1080, sustainable sneaker launch, warm earth tones, clean sans-serif via overlay, editorial minimalism, no aggressive sale energy.”

## Mistake 2: Skipping brand parameters

**Problem:** Every output has different visual DNA.

**Why it fails:** Consistency is the difference between a brand and a pile of images.

**Fix:** Load Lovart Brand Kit—colors, type roles, logo lockups, forbids. Every generation inherits automatically.

## Mistake 3: Accepting the first output

**Problem:** One roll of the dice, then ship.

**Why it fails:** Generation is probabilistic. Professionals iterate.

**Fix:** Four-take stop rule. Score against brief criteria, not mood alone. Directed refinements: “darker background, larger product, more negative space.”

## Mistake 4: Wrong dimensions

**Problem:** Beautiful 1:1 used on 9:16 Stories; awkward crops everywhere.

**Fix:** Pixel dimensions in every brief. Generate platform-native; do not crop as an afterthought.

## Mistake 5: Letting AI handle typography

**Problem:** Garbled letters—the top AI tell.

**Fix:** Generate composition without embedded text. Add headlines and CTAs with text overlay tools where you control font, size, and content.

## Mistake 6: Ignoring resolution requirements

**Problem:** Screen resolution sent to print or large-format.

**Fix:** State DPI and color space: web RGB at target px; print 300 DPI CMYK when needed.

## Mistake 7: Single-style homogeneity

**Problem:** One prompt formula for everything; feed looks tired.

**Fix:** Style library of 5–8 treatments within brand rules. Rotate by content type.

## Mistake 8: No exclusion list

**Problem:** Clutter, decorative borders, random hands.

**Fix:** Constraints section every brief: “No gradients. No decorative borders. No hands. Max three visual elements.”

## Mistake 9: Vague iteration

**Problem:** “Make it better.” “Something feels off.”

**Fix:** Name the failure: “Product too small—increase 40% and center.” Measurable direction only.

## Mistake 10: Skipping human judgment

**Problem:** Publish without strategic review.

**Fix:** Editor checklist: brief objective, brand respect, instant comprehension, cultural risk, competitor confusion.

## Flop diary

### Flop: the generic carousel
Launch post could have been any DTC brand. Fix: audience + product truth in brief; kit active.

### Flop: typography trust fall
Headline baked into image; three letters wrong. Fix: overlay type only; image is background.

### Flop: print surprise
Poster pixelated at venue size. Fix: resolution line in brief before generate.

## FAQ

### What is the single biggest mistake?
Treating AI as a “make it look good” button instead of a production tool that rewards specific direction.

### Why does AI text look wrong?
Image models render letter-like shapes, not language. Use overlay typography.

### How many iterations should I expect?
With a strong brief: one to three directed iterations. With a weak brief: unlimited thrash.

### Can I fix mistakes without starting over?
Yes—Touch Edit and directed ChatCanvas edits preserve what works.

### Why do sessions look different?
Missing Brand Kit or inconsistent prompt language. Kit is continuity memory.

### When should I restart instead of iterate?
If three directed edits fail, the brief is wrong—rewrite it.
