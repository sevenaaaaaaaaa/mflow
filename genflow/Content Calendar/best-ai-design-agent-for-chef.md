## 3 Specific Use Cases for Chefs

### 1. Tasting Menu Cards & Amuse-Bouche Notes

A tasting menu is theater. Each course has a title, a description, sometimes a backstory ("This dish is inspired by the chef's grandmother's garden in Provence"), and a wine or sake pairing note. The physical menu card the guest holds — or the card placed at their setting before each course — is part of the performance. It should feel like a curated object, not a printed Word document.

Lovart generates tasting menu cards that match your cuisine's aesthetic. For a modernist Nordic chef: "Design a 10-course tasting menu card for 'Vintersol' — our winter tasting menu. Minimalist, negative-space-driven, like a Japanese woodblock print met Scandinavian architecture. 10 dishes, each with a poetic 3-word title and a 15-word description. Course 3 is 'Bark. Smoke. Root.' — celeriac roasted in birch bark with smoked marrow. Include wine pairing notes in a lighter weight. Our restaurant logo, subtle. Format: single card, double-sided, 5x7, print on textured cream stock. The card itself should feel like a memento guests want to keep." The AI produces a card that's as considered as the food it describes — because your guests' experience begins with the menu, not the first bite.

For amuse-bouche placement cards: "Create small tent cards (2x3) for tonight's three amuse-bouche: 1) Oyster with frozen horseradish snow, 2) Beet macaron with foie gras mousse, 3) Parmesan broth sip. Each card just the dish name and a single-sentence description. Minimal, elegant, matching our tasting menu card design language."

### 2. Plating Diagrams & Kitchen Training Materials

Every chef knows the pain of teaching a new line cook how to plate your signature dish. You demonstrate once, they try once, it's not right, you fix it during service, they make a mental note that's wrong by tomorrow. Written plating guides solve this — but most kitchens have handwritten notes taped to the lowboy that look like hieroglyphics after one week of grill heat.

Lovart creates precise, professional plating diagrams. "Design a plating diagram for our hamachi crudo — 6 components: sliced hamachi (fanned at 2 o'clock), blood orange suprêmes (scattered 10 to 4 o'clock), pickled fresno rounds (3 evenly spaced), micro shiso (small cluster at center), citrus-soy vinaigrette (pooled at 6 o'clock), finishing salt (pinch scattered). Each component labeled with placement instruction. Clean diagram style — not photorealistic, more like an architectural elevation. Our kitchen would print this on 8.5x11 and laminate it for the garde manger station." The AI generates a visual standard that eliminates the telephone game of verbal plating instructions — every cook plates from the same reference, every plate leaves the pass looking like the chef's vision.

For larger kitchen operations: "Create a station guide binder for each of our 5 stations: Garde Manger, Hot Apps, Grill, Sauté, Pastry. Each station gets: cover page (station name, chef de partie name, station philosophy), plating diagrams for every dish (8-12 per station), mise en place checklist with photos, cleaning/closing procedure, and a station hand-off checklist for shift change. Professional, organized, clearly branded to our restaurant."
