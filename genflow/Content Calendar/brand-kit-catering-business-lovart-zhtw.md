## Choosing Your Color Palette

[IMAGE 2 PLACEHOLDER — Conceptual Diagram]

Catering palettes must flex across three contexts: high-end galas (moody, elegant), corporate lunches (clean, professional), and casual celebrations (warm, inviting). A strong catering palette spans all three.

### Primary Colors
- **#2B2D42 (Charcoal Slate)** — Sophisticated and neutral enough to sit beside any event decor. Use as the primary logo color and on vehicle wraps and staff uniforms.
- **#8D99AE (Warm Steel)** — A soft, refined gray that bridges corporate and wedding aesthetics. Ideal for proposal-document backgrounds and menu cards.
- **#D4A373 (Burnished Gold)** — The catering color that signals quality without trying too hard. Use sparingly as an accent on embossed menus, foil-stamped proposals, and chafing-dish labels.

### Secondary/Accent Colors
- **#EF233C (Ruby Red)** — A confident accent for CTA buttons, "Book Now" elements, and holiday-season promotions.
- **#EDF2F4 (Linen White)** — The background for everything: menus, proposals, table cards. Warmer than pure white, it photographs beautifully under banquet-hall lighting.
- **#3A5A40 (Herb Green)** — For farm-to-table, organic, and garden-party caterers. Works as a secondary logo color and on eco-friendly packaging.

## Typography Recommendations

### Logo/Display Font
**Cormorant Garamond** — A refined serif that reads as "white tablecloth" without feeling dated. Perfect for logo lockups, event-menu headers, and van graphics. For modern, tech-forward catering brands, **Montserrat** provides clean confidence.

### Body/Text Font
**Karla** — A warm, highly readable sans-serif that handles everything from ingredient lists to contract terms. Pair with **Libre Baskerville** for caterers whose brand story relies on chef-driven narratives.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q1: My catering brand does both weddings and corporate — can one kit serve both?
Yes. Use a flexible two-palette system: your primary palette stays constant (logo, type, core colors), while a secondary event-context palette shifts per market. Lovart's template toggles handle this without duplicating work.

### Q2: What's the most important branded asset for a caterer?
The proposal deck. Before anyone tastes your food, they judge your PDF. A branded proposal with consistent typography and professional food photography wins contracts. Lovart exports presentation-ready decks directly from your brand kit.

### Q3: How do I brand chafing dishes, buffet cards, and service ware?
Small-format print is the caterer's secret weapon. Table tents, buffet labels, and menu cards with your logo and palette turn anonymous steam trays into a branded experience. Lovart generates all these from one template set.

### Q4: Should my delivery van be fully wrapped or just logoed?
Full-wrap ROI is higher for catering — vans park at venues where guests see them during load-in and load-out. A partial-wrap with your logo + website + phone number on the rear doors is the minimum viable investment.

### Q5: How do I photograph food for a catering brand kit without a studio?
Natural window light, a neutral linen background (#EDF2F4), and your own plated dishes shot at a 45-degree angle. Lovart's AI photo enhancer can lift shadows and correct color temperature afterward, giving you studio-quality results from iPhone shots.

### Q6: What about branding for staff uniforms and aprons?
Embroidered logos on aprons and chef coats are walking billboards at every event. Include an embroidery-safe version of your logo (simplified, single-color) in your brand kit exports. Lovart generates this automatically.
