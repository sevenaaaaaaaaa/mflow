## Clear, Structured, Learnable: The Course Creator Palette

Course brands must signal "I will make this easy to learn." Lovart's **Course Palette Engine**:

**Professional Skills Course (business, tech, design):** Trust blue (#2563EB), charcoal (#1E293B), white (#FAFAFA), amber accent (#F59E0B), and warm gray (#9CA3AF). Blue signals competence and clarity — the colors of education platforms (Coursera, Udemy, LinkedIn Learning). The amber accent adds energy without distracting from content. This palette says "serious education that will advance your career."

**Creative / Passion Course (art, writing, music, crafts):** Warm coral (#E07A5F), deep teal (#1A4A5A), cream (#FDF6EC), yellow (#EAB308), and sage (#9CAF88). Warmer, more personal than the professional palette. The coral and yellow signal creative energy; the teal and sage ground it in calm focus. This palette says "create something you'll love with guidance from someone who gets it."

**Wellness / Personal Development Course:** Eucalyptus (#A3B5A6), clay (#C49A8C), oat (#D4C4B0), lavender (#B8B0C8), and cream (#F5F0E8). Soft, nurturing colors that reduce learning anxiety. This palette says "personal growth in a safe, supportive space." The lavender adds a touch of the spiritual/contemplative without going full mystic.

Lovart's palettes include **Learning Psychology Notes** — how each color affects student engagement. Blue: improves focus and retention (Mehta & Zhu, 2009, "Blue or Red? Exploring the Effect of Color on Cognitive Task Performances"). Warm neutrals: reduce cognitive load, making complex content feel approachable. Amber/yellow accents: draw attention to key points and CTAs without the "alert" feeling of red.

## Typography for Learning: Reduce Cognitive Load

Course typography must make learning effortless. The font shouldn't be noticed — it should disappear behind the content. Lovart's **Course Type System**:

**Course Title Font** — Bold, clear, and scannable. Heavy sans-serif (Inter Extra Bold, DM Sans Bold) for most courses. The title font appears on the sales page hero, course platform header, and certificate. It must read clearly at large sizes (sales page hero) and small sizes (course platform sidebar).

**Lesson Body Font** — For video slides, workbook text, and written lessons. High-readability serif or sans-serif (Source Serif 4 for long-form reading, Inter for shorter digital reading) at 16–18pt. Larger than typical web text — students are learning, not just reading. Larger type reduces cognitive load and improves comprehension speed.

**Slide/Video Font** — For presentation slides within video lessons. Bold sans-serif at 32–44pt for headlines, 24–28pt for body. Video slides are viewed on screens ranging from phones (small) to TVs (large, but far away). Lovart's **Video Slide Checker** simulates text legibility at common viewing distances and screen sizes.

**Workbook Font** — For downloadable PDF exercises. Clean sans-serif at 12–14pt with 1.6x line height. Workbooks need generous spacing — students write in margins, highlight, and annotate. Tight text discourages engagement with the exercises.

## The Course Sales Ecosystem

[IMAGE 3 PLACEHOLDER — Real UI Screenshot]

**Sales Page Template** — The most important page. Sections: hero (course name + transformation headline + CTA), problem agitation (who this is for/not for), course overview (modules with topics), about the instructor, student results/testimonials, curriculum deep-dive, bonuses, pricing (with payment plan), FAQ, and final CTA. Lovart generates the visual framework — course creators add their copy and content.

**Checkout Page Design** — The moment of purchase. Clean, minimal, with: course name, price, what's included (bullet list — reminds them of value), secure checkout badges, and a single CTA button in the conversion accent color. No navigation, no distractions — this page has one job.

**Thank You / Confirmation Page** — Post-purchase page with: welcome message, "What Happens Next" (3 steps — check email, join community, start Module 1), and a bonus download (immediate value delivery resets buyer's remorse).

**Student Welcome Email** — The first email after purchase. Warm welcome from the instructor, course access link, community invite, and "Start Here" orientation guide. Uses the brand palette in the email header/footer for consistency with the course experience.

## Student Experience Materials

**Workbook Template** (20–60 pages) — Branded workbook with: cover, welcome page, module-by-module exercises, reflection prompts, and completion certificate at the end. Consistent formatting reduces the "I'm doing homework" resistance — a beautiful workbook invites engagement.

**Slide Deck Template** (16:9) — For video lessons. Title slide, content slide (headline + 3 bullet points + supporting image), example/case study slide, key-takeaway slide, and exercise slide. Using the same slide design across all lessons creates a cohesive learning experience and reduces production time.

**Certificate of Completion** — Branded certificate with: student name, course name, instructor signature, and date. Certificates increase course completion rates by 24% (according to Coursera's learning science research) because they provide a tangible outcome.

**Community / Group Graphics** — If the course includes a community (Slack, Circle, Facebook Group), Lovart generates: community cover image, welcome post template, weekly discussion prompt template, and milestone celebration post template. Branded community spaces feel more valuable than generic ones.

## FAQ

**Q: Should my course brand be separate from my personal brand?**
A: It depends. If you're a solo creator planning multiple courses: personal brand as the umbrella, course names as products within it. If the course might eventually become a company: give the course a distinct brand identity with you as the founder/instructor. Lovart's kit supports both structures.

**Q: How do I make course slides that don't look like corporate PowerPoint?**
A: Use your course brand palette (not random template colors), limit each slide to one idea (not 7 bullet points), and use large, bold type. Lovart's slide template enforces the "one idea per slide" rule — each content slide has space for a headline and 3 supporting points maximum. Less text = better learning outcomes.

**Q: Can Lovart design a course platform landing page (Teachable, Kajabi, Thinkific)?**
A: Lovart exports brand assets (logo, colors, fonts, images) as a **Platform Setup Kit** with instructions for applying them to Teachable, Kajabi, Thinkific, and Podia. The kit includes: hex codes for each platform's color settings, font recommendations from the platform's available font list, and image dimensions for each platform's required assets.

**Q: How does Lovart handle course launches with urgency elements (countdown timers, early-bird pricing)?**
A: Lovart generates **Launch Graphics**: countdown timer design (numbers in the brand's signature font), early-bird badge, cart-open/cart-close announcement graphics, and waitlist page design. These are temporary launch assets that use the brand palette with added urgency colors (a warm amber or coral that stands out from the primary palette).

**Q: What's the ideal workbook design for completion rates?**
A: Lovart's workbook template follows research-backed design principles: one exercise per page (reduces overwhelm), progress indicator (a simple progress bar or page numbers — "Page 8 of 24"), generous writing space (minimum 2-inch-tall answer areas), and completion celebrations at the end of each module ("You finished Module 2! Here's what you've accomplished.").

**Q: Can Lovart help me rebrand an existing course that's already launched?**
A: Yes. Use the existing course content with Lovart's **Rebrand Mode**: upload screenshots of your current course, and Lovart suggests a visual upgrade that preserves the existing curriculum structure. Students see the same content with a professional new look — no curriculum changes needed.
