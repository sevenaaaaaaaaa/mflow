## The boundary I keep relearning

Every few months a new model drops and the temptation is to declare the design desk dead. "Now the AI does video, so why do I need Lovart?" I've watched three teams fall for this and all three came back within a month, because the model solved the demo and left the job. The desk isn't a legacy layer the model will eat. The desk is the part the model structurally cannot do: hold your brand across a series, keep your type editable, and survive twelve formats. Those are memory and edit problems. Generation models optimize for neither.

The useful mental model: a text-to-video model is a brilliant intern with no memory and no undo. It produces a stunning first draft at 2 a.m. It cannot remember your hex from yesterday, cannot fix a typo without redoing the whole thing, and cannot tell you why the logo drifted. A design desk is the senior producer who gives the intern the brief, keeps the brand bible open, and owns the final cut. You don't fire the producer when you hire the intern. You finally ship.

## A short note on what "review" should mean

Most AI tool reviews in 2026 are launch coverage with a score. They tell you the model is impressive and rank it against others on a vibe axis. That serves the model's marketing, not your Tuesday. A review worth reading tells you where the tool breaks on a boring brief, what it costs you in regenerations, and which half of the job it leaves for another tool. This review is written to that standard — which is why it spends more words on failure and workflow than on feature praise. The praise is real (Hailuo's motion is genuinely good). The failure is real too (baked text, drift, no memory). Both belong in the same document, or the document is an ad.

## Closing: the sentence to keep

If you run one team and one campaign date, here is the only sentence you need from 6,000 words: keep your text in Lovart and your motion in Hailuo. The model that animates is not the tool that ships. Build the half you're not buying on purpose, and Thursday becomes a publish button instead of a prayer.
