## AI Animation From Script: How It Works

The `@animate` command in Lovart accepts three things: a script, a style, and a duration.

```
@animate --script "A robot walks into a coffee shop. The barista looks up, startled. The robot orders a double espresso. The barista hands over the cup. Steam rises." --style 2d-cartoon --duration 15s
```

Lovart's MCoT engine then:

1. **Parses the script** into scenes — it understands narrative structure, identifying scene breaks, character actions, and dialogue.
2. **Generates keyframes** for each scene — composition, character placement, camera angle.
3. **Fills motion** between keyframes with AI interpolation — characters move smoothly, backgrounds shift with parallax, cameras track and pan.
4. **Applies style** consistently — the art direction from scene 1 carries through to scene 12. Characters don't suddenly change appearance between cuts (a common failure mode in Vidu and Pollo).
5. **Renders** the full sequence with audio timing baked in if you've attached a voiceover track.

The result is a coherent, watchable animation — not a fever dream of morphing shapes that loosely match your description.

## Animation Styles

### 2D Cartoon
Flat colors, expressive characters, clean outlines. This is the explainer video style — think corporate training videos, product demos, SaaS onboarding. Lovart's 2D cartoon pipeline produces clean linework and consistent character models across frames.

### 3D Animation
Volumetric characters with depth, lighting, and shadows. Suitable for product visualization, architectural walkthroughs, and higher-production-value content. Canva doesn't offer 3D at all. Vidu's 3D is experimental and often produces uncanny results.

### Motion Graphics
Text and shapes animating with kinetic typography. Perfect for social media ads, title sequences, and data visualization. This is closest to what Canva does with its template animations — but Lovart generates motion graphics from your script rather than forcing you into a pre-built template.

### Stop Motion
Claymation aesthetics with visible frame-stepping. Charming, tactile, distinctive. Use it for artistic shorts, brand storytelling, or content that needs to stand out in a feed full of slick motion graphics.

## AI Animation vs AI Video Generation: Know the Difference

This is a crucial distinction that confuses a lot of buyers.

**AI Animation** builds scenes from scratch — characters, backgrounds, lighting, motion — based on a creative script. The output is intentionally stylized. It looks like a cartoon, a motion graphic, or a 3D render. It's for storytelling, explanation, and creative expression.

**AI Video Generation** (like Sora, Runway, or Pika) generates photorealistic or semi-realistic footage from text prompts. It's for creating "real" looking video without a camera.

Lovart's `@animate` command is animation, not video generation. If you need photorealistic stock-footage-style clips, that's a different tool. If you need animated storytelling — product demos, educational content, brand narratives — that's animation, and that's what Lovart does.

Pollo AI and Vidu blur this line, offering something in between — AI video with stylistic filters applied. The result is often a compromise: not quite real, not quite animated, ultimately not satisfying either use case. Lovart picks a lane and executes it properly.
