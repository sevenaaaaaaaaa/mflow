## The 4-Phase BFCM Content Calendar

We've structured the kit around a proven BFCM timeline:

### Phase 1: Tease (Nov 1–14)
Build anticipation. Use the "Coming Soon" and "Sneak Peek" templates. Highlight what's coming without revealing the exact deal. Goal: get saves, bookmarks, and sign-ups.

### Phase 2: Early Access (Nov 15–21)
VIP and email-list exclusive previews. Use the "Early Access" templates with urgency markers. This is where you segment your audience and reward loyalty.

### Phase 3: Main Event (Nov 22–28)
Full deal launch. Rotate through deal reveal templates, social proof graphics, and countdown timers. Post 2–3x your normal cadence. Lovart's batch generation feature (just describe the series in ChatCanvas) lets you produce a week's worth of variations in under 30 minutes.

### Phase 4: Last Chance + Cyber Monday (Nov 29–Dec 1)
Urgency peaks. "Almost Gone," "Final Hours," and "Extended" templates. This is also where you test Cyber Monday-specific angles if your offer shifts.

## What Makes a BFCM Design Convert

If you take nothing else from this post, remember these three principles:

1. **Contrast kills scroll.** Your deal badge needs to be unmissable. The BFCM kit uses high-contrast sale badges tested against busy social feeds. Red, black, gold, and white dominate — colors proven to signal "deal" across cultures.

2. **One message per asset.** A common BFCM mistake: cramming the discount, the product, the testimonial, and the CTA into one graphic. Each template in the kit is single-purpose. The countdown timer exists to create urgency. The testimonial graphic exists to build trust. The deal reveal exists to stop the scroll. Stack them in sequence, not in a single frame.

3. **Mobile-first every time.** 79% of BFCM traffic comes from mobile devices. Every template is designed for small-screen readability first, then scaled up for desktop.
