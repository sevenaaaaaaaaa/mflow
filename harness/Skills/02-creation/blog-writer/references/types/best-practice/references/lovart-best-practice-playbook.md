# Lovart Best Practice Playbook

## Writing goal

Turn a workflow that "can work" into a workflow that is reliable under real team pressure.

## Core structure

1. Quick Overview
2. Why this workflow breaks without standards
3. The Best Practice operating model
4. Step-by-step workflow in Lovart
5. Rules, defaults, and naming conventions
6. Common mistakes and how to prevent them
7. Team rollout or scaling guidance
8. FAQ
9. Key Takeaways

## Must-have moves

- define the workflow boundary early
- state who the article is for
- separate mandatory rules from nice-to-have optimizations
- include at least one first-person operating scenario
- include at least one approval or handoff layer
- include parameter-level or naming-level examples

## Lovart-specific angles

- prompt blocks vs repair prompts
- ChatCanvas collaboration norms
- Brand Kit or asset-locking rules
- Touch Edit for surgical fixes instead of full rerolls
- reusable templates, naming, and export handoff logic

## Five topic templates

1. Solo workflow best practices
   - focus on speed, consistency, and low-overhead defaults
2. Team creative ops best practices
   - focus on review stages, permissions, and shared standards
3. Prompt library best practices
   - focus on structure, tagging, reuse, and update cadence
4. Approval and handoff best practices
   - focus on sign-off criteria and export readiness
5. Asset governance best practices
   - focus on naming, folders, variants, and archive logic

## Anti-patterns

- vague advice like "stay organized"
- no distinction between draft and final assets
- no criteria for when to reroll versus repair
- no explanation of what breaks at scale
- checklist with no workflow narrative
