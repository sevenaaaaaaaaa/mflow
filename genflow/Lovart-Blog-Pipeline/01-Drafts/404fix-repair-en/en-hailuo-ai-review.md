## What Hailuo AI is, in one honest sentence

Hailuo AI (built by MiniMax) is a text-to-video and image-to-video generation model. You give it a prompt or a starting frame; it returns a short clip, typically 6 to 10 seconds. Its strength is coherent motion — objects rotate without melting, camera moves feel intentional, and the model respects physical-ish behavior better than most competitors in the sub-10-second range. That's the genuine win. The honest limit: it generates a beautiful rough cut, then hands you a video where the text plate is baked in, the brand color drifted, and the aspect ratio is wrong for half your channels.

I want to be precise about scope. Hailuo is a generation model. It is not a design workspace. It has no persistent brand memory, no editable text layer after generation, and no multi-format export logic. When reviewers praise "cinematic output," they are praising the first pass. The second pass — the one that makes it shippable — is a different tool's job.

## The problem underneath: generation is not shipping

Here is the root-cause breakdown that most reviews skip. A video generation model optimizes for "does the clip look good on first watch." A design desk optimizes for "can a human correct this without regenerating from scratch." Those are opposite incentive structures. The model wants wow. The desk wants editable. When you ask one tool to do both, you get wow with no exit ramp — every tiny copy change forces a full regen, every brand drift forces a new prompt, and the campaign date arrives while you're still on attempt nine.

Why is this the central problem in 2026 specifically? Because the volume of short video a brand must produce exploded. Three years ago a skincare brand posted one hero video a month. Now May's team posts four Reels, two Stories, one TikTok, and a YouTube Shorts teaser every week — same product, different crops, different type, same offer. The bottleneck is no longer "can AI make a video." It's "can the brand survive editing that video across twelve formats without the logo drifting and the price going missing." Hailuo solves the first sentence. It leaves the second sentence completely open.

## What I tested (boring briefs on purpose)

I ran Hailuo AI on production spikes around three briefs, not demo prompts. Boring briefs expose operations capability; inspiring prompts flatter the model.

**Brief A — product orbit.** A 6-second clip of a perfume bottle rotating on a reflection floor, soft key light, no text. Purpose: see if the object holds shape through a full rotation (the classic melty-bottle failure).

**Brief B — founder talking head.** A 8-second b-roll of a founder walking through a studio, medium shot, shallow depth. Purpose: test identity consistency — does the face stay the same person across the clip, and does the background hold?

**Brief C — retail weekly promo.** A 6-second loop of a "two-for-one lunch" offer over a food close-up. Purpose: the brutal one — can Hailuo render legible type, and does the offer survive?

For each, I logged: time to first usable direction, regenerations to an approved asset, text edits required, and whether a coworker could repeat the offer back after a 10-second watch.

## Where Hailuo surprised me (the genuine wins)

I owe the model a fair shake, because the wins are real and they matter.

**Motion coherence is best-in-class for sub-10s.** On Brief A, the perfume bottle rotated a full 360 degrees and did not melt, wobble, or gain extra caps. Compared to the three tools May had tried, this was the first time the rotation looked like a product shot rather than a melting simulation. For e-commerce hero loops, this is the single most useful thing Hailuo does.

**Camera intent reads as intentional.** On Brief B, the slow dolly-in felt directed, not random. The background held — the studio wall didn't ripple or shift identity mid-clip. For founder-led content where a shaky background destroys credibility, this is a real advantage over models that treat camera as noise.

**Image-to-video is forgiving.** Feeding Hailuo a still frame and asking for "slow push" produced a clip that respected the source composition. This is the workflow hinge: generate the frame where you have control, then let Hailuo animate the part that should move. More on this below, because it's where Lovart's ChatCanvas becomes the better first step.

**Prompt adherence on objects beats on text.** Describe a physical object with material, light, and motion, and Hailuo delivers. Describe a sentence you want on screen, and it falls apart. That split is the whole review in one line.

## Where Hailuo broke (the honest failure log)

This is the part that matters for a desk. Every failure below is reproducible, not a bad day.

**Failure 1 — baked text.** On Brief C, the moment I asked for "two-for-one lunch, 11am–2pm" as on-screen text, Hailuo rendered it as a texture, not a layer. I could not select it, move it, or fix a typo. The promo date "11am–2pm" came out "11am-2pm" with a hyphen that looked like a minus. To change one character I had to regenerate the entire clip. Nine attempts later the text was still baked. This is the single most expensive failure mode for retail.

**Failure 2 — brand color drift.** When I dropped a brand hex into the prompt ("use #C8642B accent"), Hailuo approximated it for the first two seconds, then drifted toward a safer orange by second four. No persistent memory. Every clip needed a color correction pass, and color correction on video is slower than on a still.

**Failure 3 — aspect-ratio mismatch.** Hailuo's native output favored 16:9. May needed 4:5 for Instagram and 9:16 for Stories. Cropping a generated clip to 9:16 cut the bottle in half. Re-prompting with "vertical" produced a different composition, not a reframed one. Twelve formats meant twelve generations, not twelve crops.

**Failure 4 — identity drift on talking head.** On Brief B, the founder's face held for the first five seconds, then subtly changed eye shape by second six. For a single hero it was fine. For a series where the founder appears in five clips, the five founders looked like five cousins. No consistency anchor.

**Failure 5 — offer clarity fails the hallway test.** I showed Brief C's clip to a coworker. Ten seconds later I asked what the offer was. They said "lunch something?" The baked, drifting, tiny type meant the actual offer never landed. A beautiful clip that fails the hallway test is not a shippable asset.

## Prompt contract (steal this)

I write the brief as a contract that travels across tools. Hailuo reads the motion part; ChatCanvas reads the layout part.

```
FORMAT: [channel + aspect ratio, e.g. Instagram 4:5]
JOB: [one sentence offer, e.g. "perfume orbit, no text"]
ZONES: headline / subhead / CTA / legal / logo
TYPE: max lines, min size, editable AFTER gen (Lovart side)
BRAND: Brand Kit on + hex list
BANS: baked dates, double CTA, fake badges
MOTION: [what moves, e.g. "slow bottle rotate only"]
PROOF: width in px + viewing distance
```

The discipline that matters: **type lives in Lovart, motion lives in Hailuo.** The moment you ask Hailuo to render the offer, you've lost the exit ramp. Keep text in the design layer.

## FAQ

**Q1: Is Hailuo AI good for product videos?**
Yes for the motion. For a 6-second e-commerce orbit, it's among the best available — objects hold shape through rotation. But you need a separate layer for type and brand, which is where Lovart fits.

**Q2: Can Hailuo render text like prices and dates?**
Poorly. It bakes text as texture, so you can't edit it after generation. The reliable workflow is to keep all type in Lovart (ChatCanvas + Touch Edit) and let Hailuo animate only the visual. Ask Hailuo for "no text" and add copy in Lovart.

**Q3: How do I keep brand color consistent across a video series?**
Hailuo has no brand memory — color drifts within seconds. Lock your palette in Lovart's Brand Kit, generate the source frames in Lovart with Kit on, then animate each frame in Hailuo. The series stays on one hex.

**Q4: Does Lovart replace Hailuo?**
No. Lovart doesn't generate the motion; Hailuo does that better. Lovart handles the editable type, locked brand, and multi-format export that make the clip shippable. They solve opposite halves of the same job.

**Q5: What's the biggest red flag when using Hailuo for business?**
Tiny copy changes that force full regenerations. If you can't fix the date without rebuilding the clip, your campaign date is at the model's mercy. Move text to Lovart's Touch Edit and you regain control.

**Q6: Can I use this for print or static too?**
Yes. Build the frame in ChatCanvas, export at 8K (PNG/JPG/SVG/PSD) from Lovart, and use it as a poster or social still. Hailuo animates only if you need motion.

## How I evaluate a text-to-video model (the scoring rubric I actually use)

I'm sharing the rubric because it's the only honest way to compare Hailuo against anything else — and because it explains why I pair it with Lovart instead of ranking it "number one" and moving on.

Hailuo scores well here: on Brief A the perfume orbit was usable on attempt two. A model that needs eight attempts to hold a bottle shape fails this axis regardless of how the ninth looks.

**Axis 3 — Brand consistency across a series (weight 20%).** Five clips of the same founder should look like five clips of the same founder. Hailuo has no memory; identity and color drift. Brand Kit in Lovart is what supplies the memory Hailuo lacks. Model-alone score: low. Paired score: high.

**Axis 4 — Channel crop survival (weight 15%).** Does the asset survive at 390px width on a phone? Hailuo's native 16:9 often crops the subject in half at 9:16. Building the frame in ChatCanvas at the target zones is the fix. Model-alone: medium. Paired: high.

**Axis 5 — Failure honesty (weight 20%).** Does the tool document where it breaks, or only show highlight reels? Under this rubric, Hailuo is a strong Axis-1 model with weak Axis-2/3/4 alone, rescued on the paired desk. That's the real ranking. Not "best," not "worst" — best at the part it does, unfinished at the part you ship.

## When to use Hailuo alone (and when not to)

Honest guidance, not a sales pitch.

**Use Hailuo alone when:** you need one hero clip, the subject is a physical object or a scene (not text), and you don't need brand consistency across a series. A single product orbit, a one-off cinematic city shot, a personal creative reel. In these cases Lovart is optional — generate in Hailuo, ship the clip.

**Use Hailuo + Lovart when:** the clip carries an offer, a brand, or a face that must repeat; you need more than one format; or the asset is part of a weekly campaign. Retail promos, founder series, product launches with typed CTAs, any work where a typo means regenerating. Here the desk isn't optional — it's the difference between shippable and stuck.

**Don't use Hailuo when:** you need editable text in the final video, long-form (it's sub-10s), or persistent character identity across many clips without a source-frame system. For those, generate the controlled frame in Lovart first, or use a tool built for series work.

The pattern across all three: Hailuo's value is bounded by "does the clip look good on first watch." Your job is bounded by "can a human correct this before Thursday." Know which boundary you're paying for.

## Prompt patterns that actually work (copy these)

After two weeks I have a short list of prompt shapes that reliably produce shippable motion in Hailuo. These are the ones I reuse; the ones that fail are in the failure log above.

**Object orbit.** "〔object〕rotating 360° on 〔surface〕, 〔light〕key light, constant slow rotation, no text, 6s." The constants that matter: name the rotation speed ("constant slow"), name the surface ("reflection floor"), and ban text. Without "no text" Hailuo adds decorative type you'll have to remove in Lovart.

**Founder b-roll.** Start from a Lovart-generated still of the founder with Brand Kit locked. Prompt Hailuo: "slow dolly-in, shallow depth, 8s, keep subject identical to source frame." Feeding the correct still is what holds identity — don't ask Hailuo to invent the face.

**Food close-up loop.** " Extreme close-up of 〔dish〕, steam rising slow, 6s loop, no text." Then build the offer plate in Lovart ChatCanvas and composite it over the loop. The food is Hailuo; the offer is Lovart. Never merge them in the prompt.

**City establishing shot.** " Wide establishing shot of 〔city〕at blue hour, slow crane up, 10s, cinematic, no text." Hailuo's camera intent shines here. Keep it text-free and add any title in Lovart after.

**Product hero for social.** State the crop in the brief before generating: " composed for 4:5 Instagram, product centered, negative space top for headline, no text." Build the negative space in ChatCanvas so Lovart's headline drops in clean. Hailuo animates the product; Lovart owns the zone.

The pattern across all five: describe the physical motion precisely, ban text explicitly, and let Lovart own everything that must be edited later. Prompts that mix "make it pretty" with "put the offer on screen" produce pretty clips with broken offers.

## The questions I'd ask before betting on any text-to-video tool
