## What Lovart's AI Photo Enhancer Actually Does

### Resolution Upscaling
Take a 720p image and bring it to 4K — without the pixelation and artifacts you get from traditional bicubic upscaling. Lovart's enhancement engine reconstructs missing detail using its trained understanding of textures, patterns, and edges. This isn't stretching pixels; it's *hallucinating plausible detail* where none existed.

Fotor offers "AI Enlarger" but limits you to 2x upscaling on the free plan. Canva's image resizing is resolution-dependent — it can't add detail that wasn't captured.

### Noise Reduction
Low-light photos suffer from chroma noise (those ugly color speckles) and luminance noise (grain). Lovart's denoising targets both, preserving edge sharpness while smoothing flat areas. The result is clean, natural-looking images that don't have that over-processed, plastic-skin look.

### Color Correction
White balance off? Colors washed out or oversaturated? The enhancer's color engine analyzes the image's histogram and applies perceptually accurate corrections. Skin tones stay natural. Skies stay the right shade of blue. Greens look like actual foliage, not neon alien plants.

### Face Retouch
Portrait mode without the Portrait mode. Lovart identifies faces, smooths skin texture subtly (not the aggressive airbrushing that makes people look like wax figures), enhances eyes, and corrects undereye shadows. It's the difference between "you look great" and "did you get work done?"

Canva's face retouch is limited to basic smoothing. Fotor has a dedicated beauty module, but it requires manual slider adjustments. Lovart does it intelligently in one pass.

### Lighting Fix
Backlit subjects, harsh shadows, underexposed interiors — the lighting fix analyzes the light distribution in your image and rebalances it. Shadows are lifted without introducing noise. Highlights are recovered without washing out. This is computational photography applied to *any* photo, not just ones shot on the latest iPhone.
