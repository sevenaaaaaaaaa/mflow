## High Contrast, High Energy, No Pastels: The Gym Color Palette

[IMAGE 2 PLACEHOLDER — Conceptual Diagram]

Gym color psychology is straightforward: bold, high-contrast, energizing. Pastels and muted tones signal relaxation — the opposite of what a gym promises. Lovart's **Gym Palette Engine**:

**Strength/Performance Gym:** Obsidian black (#0D0D0D), infrared red (#E63946), steel gray (#71797E), white (#FFFFFF), and charcoal (#1C1C1C). Black-dominant with a single high-energy accent (red). The limited palette creates a serious, focused atmosphere. Red is the highest-arousal color — it increases heart rate and perceived energy, exactly the psychological state for lifting heavy. This palette photographs well: chalk dust, barbell knurling, and sweat all read clearly against the dark background.

**Functional Fitness/CrossFit:** Matte black (#1A1A1A), safety orange (#FF6B35), gunmetal (#4A5568), cream (#F5F0E8), and rubber gray (#2D2D2D). Orange provides the energy burst that red provides for strength gyms, but with a slightly less aggressive tone — appropriate for the community-oriented CrossFit culture. The cream addition softens the palette just enough to feel welcoming, not intimidating.

**Boutique/HIIT Studio:** Deep navy (#1B2838), electric coral (#FF6B6B), sand (#E8D5C0), and charcoal (#2D2D2D). Navy replaces black as the dominant dark — slightly softer, slightly more premium. The coral accent is energetic but fashion-forward, appealing to the boutique fitness demographic (predominantly women 25–44). Sand as a neutral adds warmth that pure white doesn't provide.

Lovart's palettes include **Environmental Application Notes** — which colors go on walls (dominant dark), which on equipment (accent color for upholstery, dark for frames), which on flooring (rubber gray, always — it's the color of actual gym flooring), and which in digital applications (white backgrounds with dark text for readability).

## Typography: Bold Enough to Read While Sweating

Gym typography must work in extreme conditions — read while moving, at distance, in mirrors, on screens covered in chalk. Lovart's **Gym Type System**:

**Gym Name/Logo Font** — Heavy, condensed, industrial. Fonts like Industry, Bebas Neue, Anton, or Eurostile Extended. These fonts communicate strength through sheer visual weight. Lovart's **Logo Weight Test** simulates the logo at 24 inches wide on a wall 20 feet away — if it's still legible, it passes.

**Class Schedule/Whiteboard Font** — The most-read text in any gym. Lovart recommends a clear sans-serif (Inter, Work Sans) at the largest practical size for the board format. The typeface should have easily distinguishable numerals (some fonts make 6 and 8 too similar at distance).

**Membership & Waiver Font** — Legal documents that members sign. Must be readable at 9–10pt. Lovart uses a compact serif (Source Serif 4) for waivers and legal text — serifs improve reading speed for dense text blocks by creating a stronger horizontal visual flow.

**Social Media & Challenge Font** — For Instagram posts about PRs, challenges, and transformations. Heavy sans-serif display fonts for numbers ("100kg PR") and bold statements. Lovart's **Number Display Test** ensures weightlifting numbers (with decimal points and "kg/lb" suffixes) render clearly at Instagram post sizes.

## FAQ

**Q: Should my gym use black as the primary brand color?**
A: Black dominates fitness branding for good reason — it's slimming, serious, and photographs well with sweat and equipment. To differentiate, use a single bold accent color (red, orange, neon green) rather than trying to replace black. Let your accent color be your signature — "the orange gym" is more memorable than "another black gym."

**Q: Can Lovart design gym merchandise (t-shirts, tanks, hoodies)?**
A: Yes. On the $49 Pro plan, Lovart generates **Apparel Templates**: front-chest logo placement (3.5-inch wide), back print (full-width design), sleeve print, and tag design. Exports include print-ready files for screen printing and direct-to-garment printing with specified Pantone colors.

**Q: How do I make my gym brand feel premium when the facility is industrial?**
A: Industrial aesthetic IS premium in fitness. Embrace it with: consistent dark palette, high-quality photography (well-lit equipment shots, not phone photos), restrained typography (one display font, one body font — no more), and frictionless digital touchpoints (Apple Wallet membership, online booking). Premium = intentional, not expensive.

**Q: Does Lovart include a photography style guide for gym content?**
A: Yes. The Gym Photo Style Guide covers: lighting (dramatic side-lighting for muscle definition, 5500K for accurate skin tones), composition (fill the frame with the athlete — no dead space), and subject direction (action shots, not posed — a loaded barbell or mid-box-jump is more compelling than a smiling portrait).

**Q: Can Lovart design in-gym signage (motivational quotes, zone markers, safety signs)?**
