## Logo: Identity Before Everything

Restaurant logos fall into predictable genre patterns. Knowing which one fits your concept saves you 20 bad generations:

**Fine dining:** Minimalist, often wordmark-only. Serif typography. Single-color. Gold, black, or white. No clip art. Prompt: "Elegant minimalist wordmark logo for fine dining restaurant, serif typeface, tracked letter-spacing, gold on black, refined, understated, Michelin aesthetic."

**Fast casual:** Bold, recognizable icon + friendly wordmark. Warm colors. Distinctive shape. Prompt: "Modern fast casual restaurant logo, bold icon mark, rounded sans-serif wordmark, warm orange and cream, friendly and energetic, Chipotle-meets-Sweetgreen aesthetic."

**Cafe / Coffee shop:** Vintage or cozy. Illustrated elements. Earth tones or warm neutrals. Prompt: "Vintage coffee shop logo, hand-drawn coffee plant illustration, serif wordmark, warm brown and cream, cozy and artisanal, third-wave coffee aesthetic."

**Food truck / Pop-up:** High-impact, visible from distance. Bold shapes, bright colors. Prompt: "Bold food truck logo, geometric icon, heavy sans-serif wordmark, red and yellow, high visibility, carnival-meets-street-food aesthetic."

**Pizzeria / Italian casual:** Classic, sometimes cliché by design. Script or serif. Red, green, white. Prompt: "Classic Italian pizzeria logo, hand-drawn pizza oven illustration, script wordmark, red and cream, traditional and warm, neighborhood trattoria aesthetic."

Generate 10-15 variants in your chosen genre. Pick 3. Generate variations of those 3. The logo should look good at 40px (favicon) and 400px (signage). Test both sizes before deciding.

## Menu Design: The Layout That Sells More Food

Menu engineering is a real discipline. Restaurants with optimized menus see 8-12% revenue increases from layout changes alone. Here's the AI-assisted approach:

**The psychology-aware menu prompt:**

```
Restaurant menu design, [cuisine type], [number of sections], [dining style], menu items with prices, clear typography hierarchy, [color palette], [ambiance], professional print layout, 8.5×11 portrait, [additional element — QR code area, daily specials box, chef's recommendations highlight]
```

Example: "Restaurant menu design, modern American bistro, 5 sections (starters, salads, mains, desserts, cocktails), upscale casual dining, clear hierarchy with dish names prominent and descriptions smaller, earth tone color palette with terracotta accent, warm candlelit ambiance, professional print layout, 8.5×11 portrait, QR code area bottom right."

**Layout rules AI menus should follow:**
- Dish name: largest text element. Price: second largest, aligned right.
- Description: one line maximum unless it genuinely adds value.
- Group items in odd numbers (3, 5, 7). People scan odds more naturally.
- Remove dollar signs from prices. Studies show it reduces price sensitivity.
- The upper-right corner is the "sweet spot" — put your most profitable dish there.

**Section-specific prompts:**
- **Brunch menu:** "Brunch menu design, 3 sections (eggs & savory, sweet, drinks), bright morning aesthetic, hand-drawn egg illustrations, warm yellow accents, playful but clean."
- **Cocktail menu:** "Cocktail menu design, 4 sections (signatures, classics, spirit-free, wine), speakeasy dark aesthetic, gold foil text effect, art deco borders, mysterious and sophisticated."
- **Kids' menu:** "Kids menu design, fun illustrated characters, bright primary colors, simple 4-item layout, activity section at bottom (coloring area), playful sans-serif typography."

## Food Photography: The AI Shortcut

[IMAGE 3 PLACEHOLDER — Real UI Screenshot]

Professional food photography costs $500-2,000 per session. For most independent restaurants, that's a hard expense to justify. AI offers two paths:

**Path A: Enhance your phone photos.** You take a photo of your burger under warm kitchen lights on a busy Friday. It's decent but not menu-worthy. Upload to Lovart. Prompt: "Enhance this food photo to professional restaurant photography quality, improve lighting to soft diffused studio light, enhance colors for appetite appeal, sharpen details, clean background, editorial food magazine style."

**Path B: Generate from scratch when you don't have the photo.** New menu item, no photo yet. Prompt: "Professional food photography, [dish name], [key visible ingredients], [plating style], [angle — overhead/45-degree/eye-level], [lighting], restaurant menu quality, 4K, editorial."

Both paths have limits. AI-generated food can look slightly too perfect. Enhance your real photos whenever possible — the slight imperfections (crumbs, imperfect plating) actually signal authenticity. Reserve fully synthetic photos for concepts and placeholder work.

## Digital Menu Board: The QSR Superpower

If you run a counter-service spot, cafe, or quick-service restaurant, digital menu boards are where AI design delivers the most immediate ROI. Static printed menus cost money to update. Digital boards update in minutes.

**Digital menu board prompt formula:**

```
Digital menu board display, [cuisine type], [number of menu panels], [layout — grid/list/split], large readable typography, [color scheme], high contrast for visibility, TV screen format 16:9, modern QSR aesthetic, [brand logo placement]
```

Key considerations for digital boards:
- Font sizes must be readable from 10+ feet away (typically 48pt minimum for headers, 28pt for items)
- High contrast essential — dark background + light text or vice versa
- Prices and item names get equal visual weight (unlike print menus where you de-emphasize prices)
- Consider a dedicated "promotions panel" for upsells and combos

Lovart supports TV-friendly 16:9 aspect ratios. Generate the board, export as PNG, load onto your display via USB or digital signage software.

## Social Media Content System

Restaurant social media follows rhythms. Daily specials. Weekend vibes. New menu drops. Seasonal promotions. Build templates once:

**Template 1 — Dish Spotlight:** Photo-forward. Dish name + brief description overlay. Minimal branding. Post when you want to feature a specific menu item.

**Template 2 — Behind the Scenes:** Kitchen, prep, team. Warm, authentic. Less polished than Dish Spotlight. The "we're real people" content.

**Template 3 — Specials/Promos:** Bold, urgent. "TODAY ONLY" energy. Happy hour, weekend brunch, seasonal menu launch.

**Template 4 — Customer Love:** Review quote card. Screenshot of a great review, stylized. Social proof.

Generate all four as master templates in Lovart. Each week, open the template, swap the photo or text, export. 20 minutes of social content production per week instead of 2 hours.

## FAQ

**Q: Can AI render my actual menu items accurately for food photography?**
Generate food photography for concept and marketing use, not as literal menu photos if a customer will order based on the image. For actual menu photos, take real photos of your dishes and use AI to enhance lighting, color, and background.

**Q: How do I handle dietary labels and allergen info on an AI-designed menu?**
Add them in your prompt: "Include dietary labels (V, GF, DF) as small icons next to each item." AI will generate approximate icon placements. For legal compliance, have your final menu reviewed and the text placed precisely — use AI for the visual layout, not the legal text accuracy.

**Q: Will my AI-generated brand work for both my physical restaurant and delivery apps?**
Yes, if you build the system correctly. Generate your logo at multiple sizes (favicon, app icon, sign). Define your color palette with exact hex codes. Use the same typography across all touchpoints. Inconsistency across channels is what makes a brand feel amateur — not which tool you used to create it.

**Q: How do I print an AI-designed menu at professional quality?**
Export your menu at 300 DPI (2550×3300 for 8.5×11) in PNG format. Upload to a printer like Vistaprint, Moo, or a local print shop. Specify the paper stock — matte for elegance, semi-gloss for durability, synthetic for outdoor menus. AI generates the design; you specify the production.

**Q: Can I update just one section of the menu design without redoing everything?**
Yes. In Lovart's ChatCanvas, select the section you want to update (e.g., "Seasonal Specials"), describe the new items, and regenerate just that area. The rest of the menu stays locked.

**Q: What about QR code menus?**
Generate your print menu. Export as PNG. Upload to a QR menu service (there are dozens — Untappd for bars, Mr Yum for restaurants). The QR code links to the digital version. AI handles the design; the QR platform handles the hosting.

**Q: How often should I update my menu design vs just the content?**
Content updates: whenever items change (weekly specials, seasonal rotations). Design refresh: every 2-3 years, or when you rebrand. Don't redesign your menu layout every season — customers build visual familiarity with how your menu is organized.
