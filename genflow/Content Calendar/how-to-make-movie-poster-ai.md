## The Anatomy of a Cinematic Poster

[IMAGE 2 PLACEHOLDER — Conceptual Diagram]

Before you open any tool, you need to understand what makes a movie poster work. Great posters share a structural language that's been refined over a century:

**The Three-Layer Composition.** Every iconic movie poster has three layers: a background that sets the world (a city skyline, a desert, a void), a mid-ground where the characters or central image lives, and a foreground that holds title and billing. This depth is what separates "graphic design" from "cinematic."

**Lighting Is 80% of Mood.** Movie posters are lit photographs, not flat graphics. When you prompt Lovart, lead with lighting: *"dramatic side lighting, deep shadows, one key light on the protagonist's face, noir-inspired rim light, volumetric atmosphere — cinematic poster for a psychological thriller."* The **film poster generator** interprets lighting cues as the primary mood driver.

**Typography as Cast Member.** The poster's typeface carries as much narrative weight as any image. A horror film in Helvetica is a comedy. A rom-com in Trajan is a period piece nobody asked for. Specify your typographic direction: *"serif title treatment, distressed texture, reminiscent of 1970s horror one-sheets."*

**Color Grading Defines Genre.** Teal-orange says action blockbuster. Desaturated cold blue says dystopian sci-fi. Warm amber says indie drama. Punchy saturated primaries say animated family film. Build your color palette into every prompt.

## Creating Movie Posters in Lovart: The Workflow

Here's a repeatable, professional workflow for making a **cinematic poster** with AI:

**Phase 1 — Reference Building (5 minutes).** Before prompting, gather 3–5 reference posters from films in your genre. Don't copy them. Study them. Identify what they share: composition patterns, color temperatures, title placement, billing block style. Feed these observations into your prompt as adjectives.

**Phase 2 — The Hero Generation (3–4 iterations).** Write a rich prompt that covers all four anatomy elements — composition depth, lighting, typography, color grading. Lovart's model selection (nano-banana for photorealism, FLUX for stylized illustration) lets you match the engine to the genre. Generate. Refine with ChatCanvas: *"make the shadows deeper,"* *"add lens flare in upper right,"* *"the billing block text is too prominent — reduce to 30% opacity."*

**Phase 3 — Text Integration.** AI image generators historically struggle with text. Lovart handles titles and billing blocks natively, but you can also export the image and add text in any editor. For film festival submissions, the billing block is mandatory — Lovart can generate it in industry-standard format.

**Phase 4 — Aspect Ratios for Distribution.** A theatrical one-sheet is 27×40. A digital festival program needs 1080×1920 or 1080×1080. Instagram teases need 1080×1350. Generate your hero poster, then batch-produce all platform variants with Lovart's multi-size capability — same design, adapted layouts.

Theatrical standard is 27×40 (1:1.48). Digital festival programs typically use 1080×1920 (9:16). Generate both — one for print display, one for online program guides.
