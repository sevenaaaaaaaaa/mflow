## Step 1: Define Your Video Structure as a Storyboard

[IMAGE 2 PLACEHOLDER — Conceptual Diagram]

Open Lovart and select the **Short Video Storyboard** preset (Templates → Video → Storyboard). Type: **"6-scene short video storyboard for a 30-second product explainer. Scene 1: attention hook — statistic about the problem, bold text overlay. Scene 2: pain point illustration — frustrated user with outdated interface. Scene 3: product reveal — Lovart UI demo frame. Scene 4: feature highlight 1 — AI generation in action. Scene 5: feature highlight 2 — Brand Kit application. Scene 6: CTA with app store badges."** Lovart generates a 6-panel storyboard with visual descriptions, suggested text overlays, estimated timing per scene (auto-calculated for the target 30-second total), and transition suggestions between scenes.

## Step 2: Generate Visual Frames for Each Scene

For each storyboard panel, Lovart generates a static frame that serves as your video's visual foundation. Tap Scene 1 and select **Generate Frame:** **"bold text '78% of marketers' on a dark gradient background with abstract data visualization particles, high contrast, modern tech aesthetic"** — Lovart produces a frame matching the mood and composition described. For Scene 2 (pain point), generate: **"person staring at a cluttered computer screen, blue light casting shadows, frustrated expression, cinematic lighting, shallow depth of field."** Each frame renders at 1080×1920 (vertical short-form ratio) and maintains consistent color grading and visual language across all six scenes.

## Step 3: Create Motion Directives for Each Frame

Static frames are half the product. Lovart's **Motion Directive** feature lets you describe how each frame should animate when compiled into a video: **"Scene 1: text slides in from the left with a bounce ease, particles pulse subtly. Scene 2: slow zoom-in on the person's face over 4 seconds. Scene 3: device frame rotates into view from 15-degree angle. Scene 4: UI elements appear sequentially — search bar, then results, then highlight. Scene 5: colors cascade from logo outward. Scene 6: CTA button pulses once, then holds."** When exported alongside the frames, these directives form a complete production brief that any video editor (human or AI) can follow. On the $149 Enterprise plan, Lovart's motion engine animates the frames natively.

## Step 4: Add Audio Direction and Captions

Tap **Audio Notes** on each scene. For Scene 1, note: **"upbeat synth music bed, volume -18dB, with a 'whoosh' transition sound at 0:03."** For Scene 5, note: **"music swells to peak at the product reveal, then fades to -22dB for voiceover."** Lovart also generates **caption text** for each scene based on your storyboard content — burn-in captions at the bottom safe zone with word-by-word highlight timing. The caption style (font, size, highlight color) inherits from your Brand Kit. The audio notes and caption data export as a structured JSON file compatible with video editing APIs and tools.

## Step 5: Export as Production Package

[IMAGE 3 PLACEHOLDER — Real UI Screenshot]

Lovart's **Video Production Package** export bundles: (1) all 6 frame PNGs at 1080×1920, (2) a motion directive JSON with keyframe instructions, (3) an audio notes JSON with timing, volume, and music descriptions, (4) a captions SRT file with time-coded text, (5) a compiled storyboard PDF for client/team review, and (6) cover art for the short video (the most visually arresting frame, exported separately for platform thumbnails). The entire package imports into CapCut, Descript, or Premiere Pro as a templated project. For teams using AI video tools like Runway or Pika, the package includes frame-by-frame image prompts for consistent AI video generation.

## FAQ

**Q: Can Lovart generate the actual video, not just frames?**
A: As of v2.4.1, Lovart generates video frames, motion directives, and production packages. Native frame-to-video rendering (animating static frames with the motion directives) is available on the $149 Enterprise plan. For other plans, export the package to a video editor for assembly.

**Q: What's the difference between Lovart's video storyboard and a traditional storyboard?**
A: Lovart generates finished visual frames (not sketches), includes motion and audio directives, and exports production-ready assets. A traditional storyboard is rough sketches for communication. Lovart's output is a pre-production asset package.

**Q: Can Lovart maintain consistent character appearance across video scenes?**
A: Yes. Use Character Lock (on $99 Agency plan) to define a character, and Lovart generates that character consistently across all storyboard frames. Without Character Lock, specify detailed character descriptions in each scene prompt.

**Q: What video formats and lengths does Lovart support?**
A: The Storyboard preset supports 3–12 scenes for 15–60 second videos. Aspect ratios: 9:16 (TikTok, Reels, Shorts), 16:9 (YouTube), 1:1 (LinkedIn/Instagram feed). Custom scene counts and durations are available on Pro and above.

**Q: Can I use Lovart's video frames commercially on social media?**
A: Yes. All generated frames are yours to use commercially. If you compile them into a video using a third-party video editor, the video inherits the same commercial license terms as the source frames.

**Q: Does Lovart integrate with TikTok's or Instagram's video upload APIs?**
A: Direct platform upload is on the product roadmap for Q4 2026. Currently, export the production package and upload manually or through your scheduler.
