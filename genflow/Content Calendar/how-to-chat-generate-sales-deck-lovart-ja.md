## Why Sales Decks Are Different from Regular Presentations

[IMAGE 2 PLACEHOLDER — Conceptual Diagram]

A standard presentation informs. A sales deck persuades. The difference is not cosmetic — it is structural. Every slide in a sales deck must move the prospect one step closer to a "yes." There are no neutral slides. There are no data dumps. Every visual, every stat, every testimonial serves the singular purpose of reducing the prospect's perceived risk and increasing their perceived upside.

The design must reflect this intensity. Sales decks are cleaner, bolder, and more visually confident than internal presentations. They use contrast aggressively. They make numbers feel monumental. They frame the product as the inevitable solution to a pressing problem. They do not whisper. They speak clearly and stop.

## The Chat-Generate Sales Deck Workflow

### Step 1 — Map the Persuasion Arc

A sales deck is a narrative, not a reference document. Each slide is a chapter. The chapters must flow from problem to solution to proof to action:

1. **Title Slide**: The one-line promise — what this deck will prove.
2. **The Status Quo Problem**: The pain of doing nothing. Make it visceral.
3. **The Cost of Inaction**: Quantify the problem. Dollars, hours, missed opportunities.
4. **The Solution**: Your product as the inevitable answer. Not "a solution" — THE solution.
5. **The Mechanism**: How it works. Keep it simple enough to retell at the water cooler.
6. **The Proof**: Case study results. Specific numbers, named clients, before/after metrics.
7. **The Differentiator**: Why you, not the competitor. One slide, maximum two differences.
8. **The ROI**: What they gain. Hard numbers. Payback period. Cost savings.
9. **The Implementation**: How it happens. Timeline, onboarding, support.
10. **The Social Proof**: Logos, testimonials, awards, recognitions. Credibility by association.
11. **The Call to Action**: What happens next. Specific, time-bound, easy to say yes to.
12. **The Appendix**: Backup slides for Q&A — pricing detail, technical specs, competitor deep-dives.

### Step 2 — Write the Sales Deck Design System

```

BRAND KIT: [Company Brand Kit] — confident navy (#0F1B33), vibrant electric blue (#3B82F6), 
clean white (#FFFFFF), subtle warm gray (#F5F5F7), text charcoal (#1E1E24). 
Font pairing: Bebas Neue (display/headings — bold, condensed, confident) + 
Inter (body/data — clean, modern, professional).

MASTER SLIDE RULES:
1. Background: Clean white (#FFFFFF). Exception: title slide and section dividers use 
   full-bleed navy (#0F1B33).
2. Accent stripe: A vertical electric blue (#3B82F6) stripe, 8px wide, on the left edge of 
   every content slide, full height. This is the visual signature of the deck.
3. Slide title: Inter Bold, 32pt, navy, top-left aligned, 1.0 inch from top, 
   1.2 inches from left (inside the blue stripe).
4. Body text: Inter Regular, 18pt, charcoal, 1.4x line height. Never more than 
   4 lines of body text on any slide. If you need more text, split the slide.
5. Data emphasis: Key numbers (revenue, savings, percentages) rendered in Bebas Neue, 
   electric blue, 48-72pt depending on hierarchy. Numbers are the visual heroes.
6. Footer: Thin gray rule line (1pt, #E5E5EA), full width minus the blue stripe. 
   Below: company name (left, Inter Light, 9pt, gray) and "Confidential" (right, same).
7. Page numbers: Bottom right, Inter Light, 10pt, gray.

TRANSITION RULE: Every slide should feel like it belongs in the same family but never 
like a duplicate of the previous slide. Vary the layout while maintaining the 
blue stripe + title position + typography system.
```

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How is a sales deck different from an investor pitch deck?**

Structure: both follow a problem-solution-proof-ask arc. The difference is the ask. An investor deck asks for money and emphasizes market size and team. A sales deck asks for a decision and emphasizes ROI and implementation. The design intensity is similar; the content emphasis differs.

**Can I generate both an in-person and a virtual version of the same deck?**

Yes. In-person deck: image-heavy, minimal text — the slides support the speaker. Virtual/email deck: more body text on slides — the deck must communicate without you in the room. Generate the virtual version first (more complete), then strip text for the in-person version.

**How many slides should a sales deck have?**

For a 30-minute meeting: 10-12 slides. For a 60-minute deep-dive: 15-18 slides. The appendix can add 5-10 backup slides for Q&A. Never more than one idea per slide, and never more than one slide per 2 minutes of presentation time.

**Should I include pricing in the sales deck?**

It depends on your sales process. If pricing is discussed in the meeting, include it (usually in the ROI slide or as an appendix). If pricing is handled separately by a different team, note "Pricing discussed separately — tailored to your usage" on the CTA slide. Never leave the prospect guessing about the existence of pricing information.

**Can I customize the deck for different industries without redesigning from scratch?**

Yes. Save the design system (slide rules, colors, typography). Generate industry-specific versions by swapping: case study clients, cost-of-inaction statistics, ROI calculations based on industry-average salaries/metrics, and testimonial quotes from relevant industries. The design remains consistent; the content becomes relevant.

**How do I handle technical or complex product explanations visually?**

Simplify ruthlessly. If your product requires 5 slides of architecture diagrams to explain, you are over-explaining. Use one "How It Works" slide with a 3-step visual. Direct technical audiences to the appendix for deep-dive architecture slides. In the main deck, communicate the outcome, not the mechanism.
